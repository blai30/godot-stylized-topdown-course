## What you will build

The character's real mesh: one flat-shaded model of about 530 triangles, 1.8 meters tall, that fits the blockout you built in lesson 36. You model it the way most artists model a body. Start with one box, make it half of the torso, and grow the rest from it by **extruding** (pulling a face out into a new part of the mesh) and **insetting** (shrinking a face inward, to leave a border). A **Mirror** modifier makes the other half for free. Shapes that are not part of the skin, the head, hair, pack, belt, and shoulder pads, are separate pieces, joined at the end.

	caption="The refined character, flat shaded."
/>

<Aside type="note" title="Why box modeling and not a pile of shapes">
Lesson 36 stacked separate boxes because a blockout only has to answer the question of size. A real body is one connected surface: its shoulder, elbow, and knee are places where the same mesh bends, and the skinning in lesson 40 depends on that. Pulling a body out of one box, with a mirror for the symmetry, is the standard way to get there. Artists who start from a sphere or a plane and "sculpt" with extrudes and edge loops are doing the same thing with a different first shape. A low-poly character is a good place to learn it, because every face is visible and every one counts.
</Aside>

## Half a torso

<Steps>

1. With `character` active in the Outliner, hover the viewport and choose **Add > Mesh > Cube** (<Kbd keys="Shift+A" />). Rename it `body` with <Kbd keys="F2" />.

2. Press <Kbd keys="Tab" /> to enter Edit Mode and <Kbd keys="A" /> to select everything. Press <Kbd keys="S" /> and type `0.1`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0.14`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0.265`, then <Kbd keys="Enter" />. A cube is 2 m wide, so these are half sizes: the box is 0.2 m across, 0.28 m deep, and 0.53 m tall.

3. Press <Kbd keys="G" /> and type `0.1`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `1.165`, then <Kbd keys="Enter" />. The box now spans `x` from 0 to 0.2 and `z` from 0.9 to 1.43: the torso and hips of the left half of the body, up to the shoulders.

	<Figure
		src={halfTorso}
		alt="Blender in Edit Mode with the half-torso box lying beside the center line of the front reference sheet, covering the left half of the sheet's torso and hips."
		caption="Half the torso: its right face is on the center line."
	/>

4. Press <Kbd keys="3" /> for face select mode. Press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+Numpad 3" mac="Cmd+Numpad 3" /> for the left view, click the face that faces the center line, press <Kbd keys="X" />, and choose **Faces**. It faces the other half of the body, which the Mirror modifier will make, so it must go.

	<Figure

## The Mirror modifier

<Steps>

1. Open the **Modifiers** tab, the wrench, and choose **Add Modifier**. Type `mirror`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. The axis is **X**, and **Merge** is on at `0.001 m`, which welds the two halves where their points meet on the center line.

	<Figure
		src={mirror}
		alt="The Mirror modifier's panel with Axis X selected, Bisect and Flip off, Clipping off, and Merge on at 0.001 m."
		caption="From now on, whatever you do on the left half happens on the right."
	/>

</Steps>

## Extrude the arms

<Steps>

1. In the right view (<Kbd keys="Numpad 3" />), click the face on the +X side of the torso. Press <Kbd keys="I" />, type `0.09`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" /> to **inset** it: it shrinks into a smaller face with a border.

2. Press <Kbd keys="S" />, <Kbd keys="Z" />, type `0.29`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />, and press <Kbd keys="G" />, <Kbd keys="Z" />, type `0.2` and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. The face is now the size of the top of an arm and sits at the shoulder.

	<Figure
		src={armFace}
		alt="The right view with a small square selected on the side of the torso near the shoulder."
		caption="The shoulder face, ready to extrude."
	/>

3. Press <Kbd keys="E" /> and type `0.04`, <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Press <Kbd keys="R" />, <Kbd keys="Y" />, type `69.4`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. The new face now tilts down. Press <Kbd keys="E" /> and type `0.43`, <Kbd keys="Enter" />: the arm. Extrude `0.015`, scale it by `1.25`, and extrude `0.1`: the cuff and the hand.

4. Press <Kbd keys="Numpad 1" /> for the front view.

## Extrude the legs

<Steps>

1. In the bottom view (<Kbd keys="Ctrl+Numpad 7" mac="Cmd+Numpad 7" />), click the bottom face of the half torso. Press <Kbd keys="I" />, type `0.035`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" /> to leave a gap at the center, and press <Kbd keys="S" />, type `1`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0.62`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `1`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" /> to narrow it into a leg's footprint.

2. Extrude down in four steps. For each, press <Kbd keys="E" />, type the distance, press <Kbd keys="Enter" />, then <Kbd keys="S" /> and the scale:

	| Part | Extrude | Scale after |

	| Thigh | `0.4` | `0.95` |
	| Shin | `0.3` | `0.95` |
	| Boot shaft | `0.1` | `1.15` |
	| Boot | `0.1` | none |

## The neck

<Steps>

1. In the top view, click the top face of the torso (it is the only face looking up at `z = 1.43`). Press <Kbd keys="E" />, type `0`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" /> to make a copy of the face in place. Press <Kbd keys="S" /> and scale to `0.25`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0.4`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `1`. Scaling is around the face's own center, which is 0.1 m from the center line, so the face leaves the center line. Press <Kbd keys="G" /> and type `-0.075`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0`, and <Kbd keys="Enter" /> to put it back, between 0 and 0.05.

2. Press <Kbd keys="E" /> and type `0.14`: the neck rises to `z = 1.57`, where the head will cover its top.

## Apply the mirror

The pieces you add next are symmetric by themselves and must not be mirrored a second time. **Apply** the modifier, which turns its result into real faces.

<Steps>

1. Press <Kbd keys="Tab" /> for Object Mode. Hover the Mirror modifier's panel and press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" />. The modifier is gone and the mesh now holds both halves: 112 faces.

	<Figure
		src={applied}

		caption="The modifier is applied. The mesh is whole."

</Steps>

## Pieces

Some shapes are not the body's skin: a head is a ball sitting on a neck, hair is a cap sitting on a head, a pack is a box sitting on a back. Each is a primitive, added, sized, and moved into place. For every piece in the table:

<Steps>

1. Press <Kbd keys="Shift+A" />, type `ico sphere` or `cube`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />.

2. Open the **Add** panel at the lower left, and set its values: for an ico sphere, **Subdivisions** and **Radius**, for a cube **Size** `2`, and **Location** and, for the fringe, **Rotation Y**.

3. Press <Kbd keys="F2" /> and name it. Press <Kbd keys="S" />, type the three **half sizes** with <Kbd keys="Tab" /> between them, press <Kbd keys="Enter" />, then <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" /> and choose **Scale**, and <Kbd keys="S" /> again to be sure nothing is still scaled.

</Steps>

An **ico sphere** is a sphere made from triangles, so its facets are even. At **Subdivisions** `2` it has 80 faces; at `1`, 20.

## Join, flatten, and check

<Steps>

1. Select every mesh with <Kbd keys="F3" />, `select all by type mesh`. Then <Kbd keys="Ctrl" />-click `body` in the Outliner, so it is the **active** object, the one the others join into. Press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+J" mac="Cmd+J" />. Thirteen objects become one: `body`.

2. Press <Kbd keys="F3" />, type `shade flat`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Every face now has its own light, and the facets show.

3. Check: the mesh has 374 faces and 528 triangles, and its lowest point is `z = 0` and its highest `1.8`.

</Steps>

Use the same exporter as in lesson 36, on the `character` collection.

<Steps>

1. Open the **Collection** tab, click the exporter's folder button, and save `character.glb` in the `art/character` folder of your Godot project, next to `character_blockout.glb`.

	<Figure

## Try it

- Extrude the body's hand into a thumb: select a hand's front face, inset it, extrude. How many extra faces does it cost?
- Move the `skirt` up by 0.05 and check the side view. Which is better, a long tunic or a short one?
- Hover the viewport and press <Kbd keys="Numpad 7" /> for the top view. Is the head the biggest shape, as it should be for your camera?

## If it does not work

- **A mirrored half is missing.** The center-line face is still there, or the half torso is not touching the center line. The box must span `x` from 0 to 0.2.
- **The mirror creates a gap in the middle of the neck.** The neck's face was scaled and left off the center line. Move it back with the `-0.075` step.
- **The pieces appear twice.** You added them while the Mirror modifier was still on `body`, or you added them before applying it. Apply the modifier first.
- **Joining makes `reference_front` part of the body.** The empties were selected. Use `select all by type mesh`, which skips them.
- **The shading is smooth in Godot.** You did not use Shade Flat before the export, or you ran Shade Auto Smooth. Run `shade flat` and export again.
