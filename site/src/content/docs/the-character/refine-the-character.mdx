## What you will build

The character's real mesh: **one connected, flat-shaded model** of about 400 triangles, 1.8 meters tall, that fits the blockout you built in lesson 36. You start from one box and grow the whole body out of it: the belt and chest, the skirt, the arms and legs, a pack, a neck, and a head. Nothing is added as a separate shape.

<Figure

	alt="Blender's 3D viewport with a gray low-poly character on the ground, seen from the front and a little to the side: a blocky head with a brow, a sloped shoulder line, a tunic with a belt and a skirt, angled arms, straight legs, and boots."
	caption="The refined character, one mesh, flat shaded."
/>

<Aside type="note" title="Box modeling">
This is **box modeling**: start from a box and pull the shape out of it, by **extruding** a face (pushing it out into a new part of the mesh), **scaling** it, and **insetting** it (shrinking it inward to leave a border). The Mirror modifier means you build half. The method is slow on a fine mesh and fast on a low-poly one, where every face counts, which makes it a good way to learn.
</Aside>

## The idea: a body in rings

Most of a body is a tube with a changing size: the torso, the neck, the head, and the skirt are stacks of **rings**. A ring is one extrusion: you select the face at the end of the stack, extrude it a distance, and scale it to the next ring's width and depth. Arms, legs, and the pack come out of side and back faces. All of it is half a body, on the left, because the Mirror modifier does the right.

You only ever work on the left half of the body, the half with `x` above 0. The Mirror modifier draws the other half for you, but you cannot select it. Clicks and boxes only find the half you built.

One detail repeats for every ring. The faces you extrude are half faces, one edge of which lies on the center line. Scaling a face scales it around its own center, and that is 0.1 m away from the center line, so the face slides off it. After each scale you move the face back with <Kbd keys="G" /> so its inner edge is on the line again. The distance is half of the face's old width times the scale minus one. The tables below give you the numbers, so you will not need to work them out.

## One box

<Steps>

1. With `character` active in the Outliner, choose **Add > Mesh > Cube** (<Kbd keys="Shift+A" />). Rename it `body` with <Kbd keys="F2" />.

2. Press <Kbd keys="Tab" /> to enter Edit Mode and <Kbd keys="A" /> to select everything. Press <Kbd keys="S" />, type `0.1`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0.14`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0.05`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. A cube is 2 m wide, so these are half sizes: the box is 0.2 m across, 0.28 m deep, and 0.1 m tall.

3. Press <Kbd keys="G" /> and type `0.1`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0.95`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. The box covers `x` from 0 to 0.2 and `z` from 0.9 to 1.0: the left half of the hips.

	<Figure
		src={halfHips}
		alt="Blender in Edit Mode with a small box lying on the center line of the front reference sheet, over the left half of the hips."
		caption="Half the hips: its right face is on the center line."
	/>

4. Press <Kbd keys="3" /> for face select mode. Press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+Numpad 3" mac="Cmd+Numpad 3" /> for the left view, click the face that faces the center line, press <Kbd keys="X" />, and choose **Faces**. It faces the other half of the body, which the modifier will make, so it must go.

	<Figure
		src={deleteFace}
		alt="The Delete menu in Edit Mode listing Vertices, Edges, Faces, Only Edges and Faces, and Only Faces."
		caption="Delete the face on the center line."
	/>

5. Open the **Modifiers** tab, the wrench, choose **Add Modifier**, type `mirror`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. The axis is **X**, and **Merge** is on at `0.001 m`, which welds the two halves where their points meet on the center line.

	<Figure
		src={mirror}
		alt="The Mirror modifier's panel with Axis X selected, Bisect and Flip off, Clipping off, and Merge on at 0.001 m."
		caption="From now on, whatever you do on the left half happens on the right."
	/>

</Steps>

## The belt and the chest

<Steps>

1. Press <Kbd keys="Numpad 7" /> for the top view, and click the top face of the box.

2. Make each ring in the table: press <Kbd keys="E" />, type the distance, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Press <Kbd keys="S" />, type the **scale** `X`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `Y`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `1`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Press <Kbd keys="G" />, type the **move**, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. If the scale is `1`, skip the last two keys.

	| Ring | Extrude | Scale `X`, `Y` | Move `X` | Ends at `z` |
	|---|---|---|---|---|
	| Belt, out | `0` | `1.05`, `1.0714` | `0.005` | 1.00 |
	| Belt | `0.04` | none | none | 1.04 |
	| Belt, in | `0` | `0.95238`, `0.93333` | `-0.005` | 1.04 |
	| Chest | `0.2` | none | none | 1.24 |
	| Shoulders | `0.19` | none | none | 1.43 |

	The belt is a lip: an extrusion of no height that widens, a short band, and an extrusion of no height that narrows again, so the belt stands out of the body by 1 cm.

	<Figure
		src={torso}
		alt="The front view in Edit Mode: the box has grown into a torso with a belt lip a quarter of the way up, over the front reference sheet."
		caption="The torso, with its belt."
	/>

</Steps>

## The arm

<Steps>

1. In the right view (<Kbd keys="Numpad 3" />), click the side face of the shoulders ring, the topmost face on the torso's outer side. It is the face on the +X side, which the right view looks at. Press <Kbd keys="I" />, type `0.05`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" /> to **inset** it. Press <Kbd keys="S" />, type `1`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0.55`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `1.1`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Press <Kbd keys="G" />, `0`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0.03`, and <Kbd keys="Enter" />. It is now a 10 cm square at the shoulder.

	<Figure
		src={armFace}
		alt="The right view with a small square selected on the side of the torso near the shoulder."

2. Press <Kbd keys="E" />, type `0.04`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. ## The skirt and the legs

<Steps>

1. In the bottom view (<Kbd keys="Ctrl+Numpad 7" mac="Cmd+Numpad 7" />), click the bottom face of the hips. Make the three rings of the skirt the same way as before. The extrusions go down, because they follow the face's normal:

	| Ring | Extrude | Scale `X`, `Y` | Move `X` | Ends at `z` |
	|---|---|---|---|---|
	| Skirt, out | `0` | `1.075`, `1.0714` | `0.0075` | 0.90 |
	| Skirt | `0.1` | none | none | 0.80 |
	| Skirt, in | `0` | `0.93023`, `0.93333` | `-0.0075` | 0.80 |

2. Press <Kbd keys="I" />, type `0.035`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" /> to inset the face and leave a gap at the center. Press <Kbd keys="S" />, type `1`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0.62`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `1`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" /> to narrow it into a leg's footprint.

3. Extrude down in four steps. For each, press <Kbd keys="E" />, type the distance, press <Kbd keys="Enter" />, then <Kbd keys="S" /> and the scale for the first three:

	| Part | Extrude | Scale after |
	|---|---|---|
	| Thigh | `0.3` | `0.95` |
	| Shin | `0.3` | `0.95` |
	| Boot shaft | `0.1` | `1.15` |
	| Boot | `0.1` | none |

	The mesh now ends at `z = 0`, the ground.

	<Figure
		src={legs}
		alt="The front view of both legs, each a long box that narrows at the knee and widens at the boot, standing on the ground line under the skirt."

4. In the front view, click the front face of the left boot, the one at `x` above 0, and extrude it `0.13` forward, for the foot.

## The pack

Press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+Numpad 1" mac="Cmd+Numpad 1" /> for the back view. Keep X-ray off, so a box selects only the faces you can see (press <Kbd keys="Alt+Z" /> if the viewport shows through the body). ## Apply the mirror and clean up

<Steps>

1. Press <Kbd keys="Tab" /> for Object Mode. Hover the Mirror modifier's panel and press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" />. The modifier is gone and the mesh holds both halves.

	<Figure
		src={applied}
		alt="The Modifiers tab with no modifiers, and the viewport showing the full mirrored body."
		caption="The modifier is applied. The mesh is whole."
	/>

2. Press <Kbd keys="Tab" /> for Edit Mode, <Kbd keys="Alt+A" /> to deselect, and <Kbd keys="F3" /> and type `select interior faces`. These are the faces left along the center line, where the two halves' extrusions met: they are inside the body and nothing should draw them.

	<Figure
		src={interior}
		alt="The viewport in Edit Mode with a row of faces selected down the center of the body."
		caption="The faces on the center line, selected."
	/>

3. Press <Kbd keys="X" /> and choose **Faces**. Press <Kbd keys="Tab" />.

4. Press <Kbd keys="F3" />, type `shade flat`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Every face has its own light, and the facets show.

</Steps>

Check the mesh: about 400 triangles, its lowest point at `z = 0` and its highest at `1.8`, and its origin at the world's origin, between the feet.

## Try it

- Add a loop cut across the arm and move it to bend an elbow (<Kbd keys="Ctrl+R" mac="Cmd+R" /> hovering an arm edge). How many faces does it cost?
- Extrude the front of the head forward by `0.02`, for a nose. Does it read from above?
- Press <Kbd keys="Numpad 7" /> for the top view. Is the head the biggest shape, as it should be for your camera?

## If it does not work

- **A ring slides off the center line.** You skipped the move after a scale, or typed it with the wrong sign. The moves in the tables are signed: a ring that gets bigger moves in `+X`, and a ring that gets smaller moves in `-X`.
- **A mirrored half is missing.** The center-line face of the first box is still there, or the box is not touching the center line. The box must span `x` from 0 to 0.2.
- **A face you click is not the one you meant.** A click selects the nearest face the viewport shows. Use the view the face is seen from, and check the number of selected faces in the Statistics overlay.
- **The pack selection has extra faces.** The box is too wide: it reaches an arm. Make it narrower, or turn X-ray off.
- **The shading is smooth in Godot.** You did not use Shade Flat before the export, or you ran Shade Auto Smooth. Run `shade flat` and export again.
