## The library

The **Universal Animation Library**, UAL, is a set of over forty humanoid animations by Quaternius in its free Standard version: idles, walks, a jog, a sprint, jumps, fights, a dance, and more, released into the public domain (CC0), so you may use it in any game. It comes as a file with a skeleton, a plain mannequin mesh, and the clips. The skeleton has humanoid bone names of its own, and once you import it with a map, so does yours; that is why a clip from it can play on your character.

Download the pack from `https://quaternius.itch.io/universal-animation-library` (the free Standard pack is enough). Inside, the folder `Unreal-Godot` holds two glb files. Use `UAL1_Standard.glb`, the one without `_RM`: the `_RM` file has the character's movement baked into the clips (root motion), and your game moves the character itself.

<Aside type="note" title="Clip names">
After the import you will see the names without their `_Loop` ending: `Idle_Loop` becomes `Idle`, `Walk_Loop` becomes `Walk`, `Jog_Fwd_Loop` becomes `Jog_Fwd`, and `Sprint_Loop` becomes `Sprint`. Godot also sets those clips to loop. The reference project keeps the file at `art/animations/UAL1_Standard.glb`.
</Aside>

## The library's own BoneMap

The library's skeleton is a different skeleton from yours: it has fingers you do not. It needs a **BoneMap** of its own, made the same way as yours.

<Steps>

1. Click **Skeleton3D** in the scene tree. Click the **Bone Map** field and choose **New BoneMap**. Open it, click **Profile**, and choose **New SkeletonProfileHumanoid**.

	<Figure
		src={skeleton}
		alt="The Advanced Import Settings window with the library's Skeleton3D selected and its Bone Map field empty."
		caption="The library's skeleton, with no map yet."
	/>

	<Figure
		src={boneMapNew}
		alt="The Bone Map opened with an empty Profile field."
		caption="A new map."
	/>

2. Godot auto-maps. This time it matches **53 of the profile's 56** bones: every bone except `LeftEye`, `RightEye`, and `Jaw`.

	<Figure
		src={autoMapped}
		alt="The Bone Map with a human figure showing green dots for nearly every bone, and the Retarget options with Rename Bones and Make Unique on and Skeleton Name GeneralSkeleton."
		caption="53 of 56 bones mapped."
	/>

</Steps>

Three bones with no match is expected, and Godot may warn about them in the Output panel ("Auto Mapping couldn't guess"). The library has no eyes or jaw, so there is nothing to map, and no clip needs them.

The renamed skeleton is called `GeneralSkeleton`, the same name your character's skeleton has. Every track in the library now reads `%GeneralSkeleton:Hips`, `%GeneralSkeleton:LeftUpperLeg`, and so on: a **unique name** `%GeneralSkeleton` and a bone name. A clip moves whichever skeleton in the scene has that unique name, and your character's has it.

The reference run reads one clip back: `Walk` has 58 tracks, and only one of them is a position track, `Hips`. Every other bone only rotates, because a position track on a leg would not fit a character with different leg lengths. The `Hips` position is rescaled by **Normalize Position Tracks**, the Retarget option listed in lesson 41, which is on by default, so the walk's bob matches your character's size.

## The clips already loop

A clip ends when it reaches its last frame, unless it is set to **loop**. The walk and the run are cycles, so they must start over. Click `Idle` in the scene tree of the Advanced window: Godot read the `_Loop` ending of its original name and set its **Loop Mode** to **Linear**, which plays the clip again from its start. The reference run reads it back: `Idle`, `Walk`, `Jog_Fwd`, and `Sprint` all loop, and clips such as `Death01` and `Interact` do not.

<Figure
	src={idleOptions}
	alt="The Advanced Import Settings window with the Idle clip selected and its options Loop Mode, Save To File, and Slices."
	caption="The Idle clip, with its import options."
/>

Click **Reimport** when you are done.

## If it does not work

- **The character does not move when a clip plays.** The track paths do not find a skeleton. The skeleton must be named `GeneralSkeleton`, unique in the scene (lesson 41's Rename Bones and Make Unique), and the AnimationPlayer must be a child of the character's root.
- **Some bones are stiff.** The library's map did not cover them. Check the auto-mapping shows 53 bones mapped.
- **The character floats or sinks while walking.** The hips' position track does not fit your character's size. Check that **Normalize Position Tracks** is on in the Retarget options of the library's Skeleton3D, and reimport.
- **The clip plays once and stops.** **Loop Mode** is **None**. Set it to **Linear** in the library's Advanced Import Settings.
- **Godot warns "Auto Mapping couldn't guess".** For `LeftEye`, `RightEye`, and `Jaw`, this is expected.
