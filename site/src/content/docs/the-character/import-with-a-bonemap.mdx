<Steps>

1. Open `character.blend`. Open the **Collection** tab of the Properties editor with the `character` collection active. It still has the glTF exporter from lesson 36, which lesson 37 pointed at `art/character/character.glb`, and now the collection holds the mesh and the armature.

	<Figure

	/>

2. Uncheck **Animation** in the exporter. The character has no clips of its own: lesson 42 brings a library of them.

	<Figure
		src={animationOff}
		alt="The exporter panel with the Animation checkbox unchecked."
		caption="No animation in this file."
	/>

3. Click **Export All**, then save the Blender file.

	<Figure
		src={exported}
		alt="Blender after the export, with the character and its skeleton in the viewport."
		caption="Exported."
	/>

</Steps>

The reference run reads the file back: one skin with 22 joints named for the bones, and each vertex carries `JOINTS_0` and `WEIGHTS_0`, one set of four. Four is what glTF's standard skin carries and what the course asks for, which is why lesson 40 limited every vertex to four bones.

## Import with a BoneMap

Godot reads the glb as a scene with a **Skeleton3D**. The skeleton's bones have your names, which here happen to be the profile's, but a character from another artist may have `upper_arm.L` or `mixamorig:LeftArm`. A **BoneMap** says, for each bone of the humanoid profile, which bone of this skeleton plays that part. Godot then renames the bones to the profile's names, so every skeleton ends up with the same ones and animations made for one can play on any other.

<Steps>

1. Open your Godot project. In the FileSystem dock, click `art/character/character.glb`, and click the **Import** tab at the top of the Scene dock.

	<Figure
		src={importDock}
		alt="The Import dock for character.glb with its import options and an Advanced button."
		caption="The Import dock."
	/>

2. Click **Advanced...** at the bottom of the dock. Click **Skeleton3D** in the scene tree on the left. Its options include **Retarget > Bone Map**, empty.

	<Figure
		src={advanced}
		alt="The Advanced Import Settings window: the scene tree on the left with Armature, Skeleton3D, and body, a 3D preview of the white character in the middle, and the Scene options on the right."
		caption="Advanced Import Settings."
	/>

	<Figure
		src={skeletonSelected}
		alt="The Advanced Import Settings window with Skeleton3D selected and its options on the right: Script, Skip Import, Load Pose, and Bone Map, empty."
		caption="Select the Skeleton3D."
	/>

3. Click the **Bone Map** field and choose **New BoneMap**.

	<Figure
		src={boneMapMenu}
		alt="The Bone Map field's menu listing New BoneMap, Quick Load, and Load."
		caption="New BoneMap."
	/>

4. Click the new **BoneMap** to open it. It has one field, **Profile**. Click it and choose **New SkeletonProfileHumanoid**.

	<Figure
		src={boneMapNew}
		alt="The Bone Map opened, with a Profile field that is empty."
		caption="A BoneMap has a profile."
	/>

	<Figure
		src={profileMenu}
		alt="The Profile field's menu listing New SkeletonProfile, New SkeletonProfileHumanoid, Quick Load, and Load."
		caption="The humanoid profile."
	/>

5. When the profile is set, Godot **auto-maps**: it matches the skeleton's bone names to the profile's by name and shape. The window shows a figure with the matched bones lit in green.

	<Figure
		src={autoMapped}
		alt="The Advanced Import Settings window with the Bone Map opened: a Profile, a human figure with green dots for the mapped bones, and the Retarget options with Rename Bones, Make Unique, and Skeleton Name GeneralSkeleton."
		caption="Auto-mapped: 22 bones of the profile's 56 are green."
	/>

</Steps>

The reference run reads the map back: **22 of 56** bones are mapped, each to the bone of the same name. The 34 left over are the profile's fingers, eyes, jaw, and `Root`, which your character does not have. That is as it should be: a map does not need every bone, only the ones the skeleton has.

The other **Retarget** options matter too, and their defaults are right:

| Option | Value | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Rename Bones** | on | renames each bone to its profile name |
| **Make Unique** | on | makes the skeleton a node with a unique name in the scene |
| **Skeleton Name** | `GeneralSkeleton` | the unique name, so any scene can say `%GeneralSkeleton` |
| **Normalize Position Tracks** | on | when animations are imported with this skeleton, scales their position tracks to its size (it matters for the library in lesson 42) |

## Save the map

The map lives inside this one import. Save it as a file, so you can use it again: a second character built on the same bones, or a reimport after a change, takes the same map from a file, with no new mapping.

<Steps>

1. Click the **Bone Map**'s arrow and choose **Save As...**.

	<Figure
		src={boneMapSaveMenu}
		alt="The Bone Map resource menu with Save As... listed."
		caption="Save As..."
	/>

2. Save it as `humanoid_bone_map.tres` in `art/character`.

<Figure
	src={turned}
	alt="The game at noon with the character after walking right: it has turned to face the right, in the grass."
	caption="The character turns to face where it walks."
/>

- The grass parts around the character as it did around the capsule, because the `PushPoint` is still on the player.
- The character slides: it does not move its legs yet. Lesson 42 brings the clips, and lesson 43 plays them.
- Open `humanoid_bone_map.tres` in the Inspector and click a bone in its figure. The right side lists the bone it is mapped to. If your skeleton has other names, this is where you would choose them.

## If it does not work

- **The Bone Map shows no green bones.** The profile is empty. Set **Profile** to a **SkeletonProfileHumanoid**.
- **Some bones are not matched.** Their names differ from the profile's. Click a bone on the figure and pick the skeleton's bone for it by hand.
- **The character is white in the scene.** The material is not on the mesh. Select `body` and check **Material Override**.
- **The character is lying on its back or facing away.** The glTF exporter's **+Y Up** is off, or the character faced +Y in Blender. Check lessons 36 and 37.
- **The skeleton is not named `GeneralSkeleton`.** **Rename Bones** or **Make Unique** is off. Turn them on and click **Reimport**.
- **The skin looks wrong at a joint.** A vertex may have more than four bones, and the extra ones were dropped on export: go back to lesson 40 and run Limit Total.
