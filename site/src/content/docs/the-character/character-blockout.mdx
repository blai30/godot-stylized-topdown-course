## What you will build

A **blockout** of the character: its body in big, simple shapes, at its real size and in its real proportions, standing exactly on the outlines you drew in lesson 35. A blockout has no detail and no color. It answers one question before any detail exists: is it the right size and shape? A mistake found now costs a minute. The same mistake found after the body is skinned costs an afternoon.

<Figure
	src={threeQuarter}
	alt="Blender's 3D viewport showing a gray figure built from boxes and a sphere: a round head with a flatter cap, a neck, a square torso, a skirt block, angled arms with square hands, long square legs, long boots, and a pack on the back."
	caption="The blockout: fourteen parts, no detail."
/>

You work in Blender and finish with one check in Godot. The body is 1.8 meters tall, so it matches the capsule it will replace.

## Put the sheets behind the viewport

Blender can show an image in the 3D viewport as an **image empty**: an object that draws a picture and nothing else. You will put your front view behind the model, where it is seen from the front, and your side view behind it, where it is seen from the left.

<Steps>

1. Hover the viewport and press <Kbd keys="Numpad 1" />, for the front view. Choose **Add > Image > Reference**, find `character_front.png` in the File Browser that opens, and click the button at the lower right of the browser that adds the image.

	<Figure
		src={referenceBrowser}
		alt="Blender's File Browser listing the character folder with character_front.png typed into its file name field and the Add Empty Image button at the lower right."
		caption="Add > Image > Reference opens a File Browser."
	/>

	The image appears facing you, standing on the origin, and 5 meters wide: Blender turned it to face the view you were in.

	<Figure
		src={referenceAdded}
		alt="The front view showing the reference sheet's figure inside a gray square much bigger than the grid cells, centered on the origin."
		caption="The sheet is too big and too low: the origin is at its middle."
	/>

2. Open the **Object** tab of the Properties editor and set **Location** `Y` to `1` and `Z` to `0.922`. The `Y` moves the image behind the model, because the front view looks along +Y. The `Z` lifts the sheet until its ground line is at zero. The sheet is 1024 pixels tall, which is 2 meters at 512 pixels per meter, and its ground line is 984 pixels down, so the sheet's center is 984 minus 512, or 472 pixels, above the ground: 0.922 meters.

	<Figure
		src={referenceLocated}
		alt="The Object properties with Location 0, 1, 0.922 and Rotation 90, 0, 0, and the sheet sitting behind the origin."
		caption="Behind the model, and lifted."
	/>

3. Open the **Data** tab of the Properties editor, the last one, and set:

	- **Size** to `2`. The sheet is 2 meters wide. **Offset** `-0.5` and `-0.5` already centers it.
	- **Depth** to **Back**, so the model always draws in front of the picture.
	- **Only Axis Aligned** on, so the picture shows only when you look straight at it, and does not clutter the view from other angles.
	- **Opacity** on, set to `0.6`, so the grid and the model show through.

	<Figure
		src={referenceData}
		alt="The Empty properties: Display As Image, Size 2 m, Offset -0.50 and -0.50, Depth Back, Side Both, Show In Orthographic and Perspective on, Only Axis Aligned on, and Opacity on at 0.600."
		caption="The Data tab for an image empty."
	/>

4. Press <Kbd keys="F2" /> over the viewport and name it `reference_front`.

</Steps>

Do the side view the same way, from the left.

<Steps>

5. Press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+Numpad 3" mac="Cmd+Numpad 3" /> for the left view and add `character_side.png` with **Add > Image > Reference**. Blender turns it to face the left view. Set **Location** `X` to `1` (behind the model as seen from the left), `Y` to `0`, and `Z` to `0.922`. On the **Data** tab, set the same four values: **Size** `2`, **Depth** **Back**, **Only Axis Aligned** on, **Opacity** on at `0.6`. Name it `reference_side`.

	<Figure
		src={sideAdded}
		alt="The left view with the side reference sheet added: its figure faces right, with the pack on the left."
		caption="The side sheet in the left view. The character faces right."
	/>

6. Press <Kbd keys="Numpad 1" />, then <Kbd keys="Home" /> to frame everything, and scroll in a few steps. The ground line of the sheet lies on the red X axis.

	<Figure
		src={references}
		alt="The front view with the reference sheet centered, its ground line on the red axis, and the outliner listing reference_front and reference_side."
		caption="Both sheets are in place."
	/>

</Steps>

<Aside type="note" title="Which way the character faces">
Blender's front view looks along +Y, so a character that faces the front view faces -Y. Blender's glTF exporter turns a model that faces -Y into one that faces +Z in Godot. That is the way imported models face, and the way the player's `LookAt` turns the capsule's nose (lesson 6) when `PlayerMovement` passes `useModelFront`. The sheet's side view has the character facing right in the left view, which is -Y. Everything you build in this module faces -Y.
</Aside>

<Steps>

## The parts

Each part is a shape you add, name, size, and place. The shapes are **cubes** and **UV spheres**. A cube is 2 meters wide and a sphere has a 1 meter radius, so the numbers you type with <Kbd keys="S" /> are **half** the part's size. For every part:

<Steps>

1. Hover the viewport, press <Kbd keys="Shift+A" />, type `cube` or `uv sphere`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />.

2. Press <Kbd keys="F2" /> and give it the part's name.

3. Press <Kbd keys="S" />, type the three half sizes with <Kbd keys="Tab" /> between them, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Then press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" /> and choose **Scale**, so the size becomes the mesh's own and the object's scale goes back to `1`.

4. Press <Kbd keys="G" />, type the location's `X`, `Y`, and `Z` with <Kbd keys="Tab" /> between them, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />.

5. For the arms, press <Kbd keys="R" />, <Kbd keys="Y" />, type the turn in degrees, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />.

</Steps>

| Part | Shape | Half sizes `X`, `Y`, `Z` | Location | Turn about `Y` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `head` | uv sphere | `0.115`, `0.125`, `0.14` | `0`, `0`, `1.66` | none |
| `hair` | uv sphere | `0.135`, `0.14`, `0.10` | `0`, `0.015`, `1.70` | none |
| `neck` | cube | `0.05`, `0.05`, `0.05` | `0`, `0`, `1.48` | none |
| `torso` | cube | `0.20`, `0.14`, `0.205` | `0`, `0`, `1.225` | none |
| `skirt` | cube | `0.20`, `0.14`, `0.11` | `0`, `0`, `0.91` | none |
| `pack` | cube | `0.14`, `0.065`, `0.15` | `0`, `0.205`, `1.22` | none |
| `arm.L` | cube | `0.05`, `0.05`, `0.245` | `0.285`, `0`, `1.19` | `-20.7` |
| `arm.R` | cube | `0.05`, `0.05`, `0.245` | `-0.285`, `0`, `1.19` | `20.7` |
| `glove.L` | cube | `0.05`, `0.05`, `0.06` | `0.38`, `0`, `0.91` | none |
| `glove.R` | cube | `0.05`, `0.05`, `0.06` | `-0.38`, `0`, `0.91` | none |
| `leg.L` | cube | `0.065`, `0.065`, `0.40` | `0.10`, `0`, `0.50` | none |
| `leg.R` | cube | `0.065`, `0.065`, `0.40` | `-0.10`, `0`, `0.50` | none |
| `boot.L` | cube | `0.07`, `0.125`, `0.05` | `0.10`, `-0.065`, `0.05` | none |
| `boot.R` | cube | `0.07`, `0.125`, `0.05` | `-0.10`, `-0.065`, `0.05` | none |

These numbers come straight from the sheet: the head's half height `0.14` is half of the head's 0.28 m, from 1.52 to 1.80, and its location `1.66` is the middle of that. The pack's `Y` is `0.205` because the pack is behind the body, and behind is +Y. The `.L` and `.R` suffixes are Blender's convention for the character's left and right: the character faces -Y, so its left side is +X.

<Aside type="tip" title="Why the turn is minus twenty point seven">
The sheet's arm runs from the shoulder at 0.20 across and 1.42 up to the hand at 0.37 across and 0.97 up. That is 0.17 across over 0.45 down, and the angle whose tangent is 0.17 over 0.45 is 20.7 degrees. A rotation about the `Y` axis tilts the top of a part toward -X by a negative angle, which is what the arm on the +X side needs.
</Aside>

## Check it against the sheets

<Steps>

1. Click the skirt in the viewport to select it, press <Kbd keys="Numpad ." /> to frame it, and scroll out until the whole figure fits. Press <Kbd keys="Alt+Z" /> to turn on X-ray, which shows the parts as see-through, so you can see the sheet through them.

	<Figure
		src={frontXray}
		alt="The front view in X-ray: the gray blockout parts line up with the sheet's figure behind them, with the arms sitting on the sleeves and the legs on the trousers."
		caption="Front view in X-ray: the parts sit on the sheet's shapes."
	/>

2. Press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+Numpad 3" mac="Cmd+Numpad 3" /> for the left view and compare again.

	<Figure
		src={sideXray}
		alt="The left view in X-ray: the torso, skirt, legs, boots, head, and the pack on the back line up with the side sheet's figure."
		caption="Left view: the pack stands behind the body and the boots reach forward."
	/>

3. Press <Kbd keys="Alt+Z" /> again to turn X-ray off, and <Kbd keys="Numpad 1" /> for the front view.

</Steps>

The picture matches because you typed the sheet's own numbers. The measurement matches too. The reference project reads the parts' combined bounds back from Blender: **0.86 m wide** (the gloves' outer edges), **0.46 m deep** (the pack's back to the boots' toes), and **1.80 m tall**, from the soles on the ground to the top of the head and hair. Every part's origin is at its center, which the next lessons change when they join the parts.

## Export it for a size check

You will not use this file in the game. You need to see the body next to the capsule, so export it with the collection exporter, as in lesson 24, to `art/character`.

<Steps>

1. Open the **Collection** tab of the Properties editor with the `character` collection active. Under **Exporters**, click **+** and choose **glTF 2.0**.

	<Figure
		src={exporterPanel}
		alt="The Collection properties with the Exporters list holding glTF 2.0, an Export All button, and the exporter's File Path, Format glTF Binary, and Copyright fields."
		caption="An exporter on the character collection."
	/>

2. Click the folder button beside **File Path**, go to the `art` folder of your Godot project, make a folder named `character`, and save as `character_blockout.glb`.

	<Figure
		src={exportName}
		alt="Blender's File Browser in the art/character folder with character_blockout.glb typed as the file name."
		caption="The file goes into the Godot project."
	/>

3. Click **Export All**. Then save the Blender file with <Kbd keys="Ctrl+S" mac="Cmd+S" />.

</Steps>

The file holds the fourteen parts and no pictures, because the reference empties are not in the exported collection.

## See it in the meadow

Open your Godot project. Godot imports `character_blockout.glb` on its own. Open `levels/meadow.tscn`, drag `character_blockout.glb` from the FileSystem dock into the 3D viewport, and set its **Position** to `0.9, 0, 0`, beside the player. Press <Kbd keys="F5" mac="Cmd+B" /> to run.

<Figure
	src={meadowClose}
	alt="The game at noon, zoomed in: the gray capsule on the left and the pale blockout figure standing beside it at the same height, in the grass between two pines."
	caption="The capsule and the blockout, side by side."
/>

The head of the blockout is as high as the top of the capsule. Run the game and zoom out with the wheel: from the starting view the blockout is a small figure, but its head, arms out from the body, and legs still read.

<Figure
	src={meadow}
	alt="The game at noon, zoomed out: the capsule and the pale figure beside it, in the meadow between oaks and pines."
	caption="At the starting zoom."
/>

Godot names each part after its Blender object but swaps the dot for an underscore, so `arm.L` becomes `arm_L`: a node name cannot hold a dot. The importer reads the glb's 14 meshes as `MeshInstance3D` nodes. Delete the instance from the meadow again, because the blockout was only a check: select it in the Scene dock and press <Kbd keys="Delete" mac="Cmd+Backspace" />, and save the scene.

## Try it

- Move the `pack` down 0.1 meters and look at the side view. Does it still read as a pack, or as a belt bag?
- Widen `torso` by 0.05 on each side and look at the front view against the sheet. How much wider can it get before it stops matching?
- Turn X-ray off and press <Kbd keys="Numpad 7" /> for the top view. This is closest to the camera's own view. The head and hair are the largest shapes, then the shoulders.

## If it does not work

- **The reference image is not visible.** The image empty is showing only when you look straight at it: check that you are in the front view (<Kbd keys="Numpad 1" />) for `reference_front` and the left view (<Kbd keys="Ctrl+Numpad 3" mac="Cmd+Numpad 3" />) for `reference_side`. Check **Only Axis Aligned** and **Opacity** on the Data tab.
- **The sheet is the wrong size or is floating.** **Size** must be `2` and **Location** `Z` must be `0.922`. The ground line of the sheet should lie on the red axis.
- **A part is far too big or too small.** The values you type with <Kbd keys="S" /> are half sizes, because a cube is 2 meters wide. If a part is double the sheet, you typed full sizes.
- **A part is missing from the export.** It is in the wrong collection. Drag it to `character` in the Outliner, or check which collection was active when you added it: the viewport's header names it.
- **The exported figure is on its side or backward in Godot.** The glTF exporter's **+Y Up** option is off, or you built the character facing +Y. Check the exporter's **Transform** section, and the figure's facing in the left view.
- **The reference pictures are in the exported file.** The empties are in the `character` collection. Move them to `Collection`.
