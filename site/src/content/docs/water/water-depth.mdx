## What you will build

The pond from lesson 44 has a bank and a brown stand-in for a floor. In this lesson it gets the real thing: a **lake bed** under it, the part you can see through the water, and a **water surface** over it that tints the bed deep blue. The deeper the water, the bluer and the darker it gets, and where the water is shallow, by the bank, you see the bed through it.

The water is not a texture or a mesh you model. It is a flat plane with a shader, and the shader looks at what is behind it. That is the whole lesson: how a plane finds out how much water lies between the camera and the bed, and what it does with the answer.

## How the water knows its depth

A shader draws one pixel of the plane at a time. For that pixel it knows where the plane is, but not what is under it: the bed, the bank, a rock standing in the water. Godot can tell it. When a shader declares a `sampler2D` with the hint `hint_depth_texture`, Godot gives it the **depth texture**, a picture of how far from the camera every opaque pixel in the frame is. Looking up this pixel's place in it gives how far away the bed is. The water's own distance is known too, so the difference is the **thickness**: how much water the view ray crosses before it meets something.

The depth texture stores distance in a form the shader has to undo. It holds a number from 0 to 1 per pixel, the pixel's depth in clip space. Rebuilding the point in view space takes one multiplication by `INV_PROJECTION_MATRIX`, Godot's inverse of the camera's projection, and a division by the fourth component. With the camera's orthographic projection, as in this template, the answer is exact, and the same for every pixel on the screen.

Water also takes light away unevenly. Red light is absorbed fastest, then green, then blue, so deep water is blue and the red is gone first. The shader keeps a share of each color that falls off exponentially with the thickness: `exp(-absorption * thickness)`, with a different rate for each of the three colors. The rest of what you see is the water's own color, the **scatter color**, a deep blue-slate. Shallow water, where the thickness is close to nothing, would show the bed at full strength and no water at all. Real water does not do that, and a game's would look like a hole, so the shader never lets less than a quarter of the scatter color go, with a **minimum opacity**.

### Step 2: the thickness

Now let the shader find out what is under it, and show the answer as gray. Replace the shader's contents so that:

- after `render_mode unshaded;` it declares `uniform sampler2D depth_texture : hint_depth_texture, filter_nearest, repeat_disable;`, with a comment: the depth of the frame drawn so far, which the water reads to find what lies behind it;
- `fragment()` starts with `float depth = textureLod(depth_texture, SCREEN_UV, 0.0).r;`, the depth under this pixel, where `SCREEN_UV` is where the pixel is on the screen, from 0 to 1;
- `vec4 position = INV_PROJECTION_MATRIX * vec4(SCREEN_UV * 2.0 - 1.0, depth, 1.0);` rebuilds the point as the camera sees it, with a comment: the view-space position of the opaque surface behind this pixel, rebuilt from its depth. The screen runs from 0 to 1 and clip space from -1 to 1, so the coordinates are doubled and moved;
- `vec3 behind = position.xyz / position.w;` finishes it;
- `float thickness = length(behind - VERTEX);` is the distance from the water's own point, `VERTEX`, which in a fragment is in view space too, to the point behind it, with a comment: how much water the view ray crosses before it meets the bed;
- `ALBEDO = vec3(thickness);` shows it as a gray, one meter being white.

<Aside type="note" title="Why one flat bed shows so little">
A bed that is the same depth everywhere makes the water look one flat color, except at the banks. A lake whose bed slopes down toward the middle would be pale at its edge and dark in its middle, which is how real ponds look. This template keeps the bed flat, so that it is a single tile a reader can paint. To get a deeper middle, make a second tile like this one with its center offset at -1.6, and paint it in the middle.
</Aside>

### Step 3: the bed under the water

Last, the color. Replace the gray with the bed, faded by the thickness. The final shader is:

- after `render_mode unshaded;`, two uniforms: `screen_texture`, a `sampler2D` with `hint_screen_texture`, `filter_linear`, and `repeat_disable`, and `depth_texture` as before, under a comment: the frame drawn so far, with the lake bed and the bank in it, and its depth;
- a group named `color`, opened with `group_uniforms color;`, holds three uniforms, each with a comment. `scatter_color` is as before, with `source_color`. `absorption` is a `vec3`, `vec3(3.0, 1.5, 1.0)`: how much of each color the water absorbs per meter the view crosses, with red going first. `min_opacity` is a `float` with `hint_range(0.0, 1.0)` and `0.25`: how much of the scatter color shows even over a bed right under the surface. The group ends with `group_uniforms;`;
- `fragment()` keeps the lines that find `thickness`;
- `vec3 bed = textureLod(screen_texture, SCREEN_UV, 0.0).rgb;` reads what the camera has drawn at this pixel, which is the bed or the bank, since the water is drawn after them;
- `vec3 transmitted = exp(-absorption * thickness) * (1.0 - min_opacity);` is how much of each color of the bed gets through, with a comment that it fades with the thickness, red first, from `1 - min_opacity`. It is 0.75 at no thickness, falling away from there, and never more than that, because of the minimum;
- `ALBEDO = bed * transmitted + scatter_color * (1.0 - transmitted);` mixes the bed's share with the water's own color, which takes up the rest.

## Tune it

Every number that sets the water's color is a uniform in the `color` group, so you can change it in the material.

- **`scatter_color`** is the water's own color. Make it a green for a swamp, or a lighter blue-green for a shallow tropical pond.
- **`absorption`** sets how fast each color goes: the three numbers are red, green, and blue per meter. Equal numbers make the water gray; a lower blue makes it bluer in the deep.
- **`min_opacity`** is how much of the scatter color there is over the shallowest bed. At `0` the water over a bed right under it is invisible, and at `1` the bed never shows.

## If it does not work

- **The pond is gray-brown with no blue.** The water scene is not in the meadow, or it is not at `-0.35`. Select the `Water` node in `meadow.tscn` and check its position.
- **The whole meadow is blue.** The water's position is at or above `0`. It must be under the ground.
- **A blue frame shows at the meadow's edge.** The plane is bigger than the painted ground. Set its size to `19.6, 19.6`.
- **The pond is the same flat color in all three steps.** The shader file was not saved, or the material's **Shader** is not `water.gdshader`.
- **The thickness is white everywhere.** The numbers are too big to see: the water is 0.4 meters over the bed, so `vec3(thickness)` is 0.4 to 0.5 gray. If it is white, the bed is not under the water, or the bed is not drawn.
- **The lake bed is at ground level.** The Water tile's height is in its node's position, not in the mesh's **Center Offset**. A library item keeps only the mesh.
- **The `Water` tile is not in the GridMap's palette.** The library was not exported again, or `GroundTiles` still points at an old library. Export it again, and select **GroundTiles**.
- **Grass grows in the pond.** Press **Scatter** on the **Grass** node, and check that the Water tile's **Grass Density** is `0`.
- **The shader says it cannot find the depth texture.** The hint is `hint_depth_texture`, with the hint after a colon.
