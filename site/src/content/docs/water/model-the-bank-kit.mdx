## What you will build

The meadow is about to get a pond. A pond is a hole in the ground with water in it, and the edge of the hole is where it looks like a pond or does not. This module gives the hole a real bank: a wall of earth about three quarters of a meter deep, with a lumpy lip, turf hanging over its top, and a ragged line where the grass stops. This lesson models the bank, and you will see it before there is any water.

The bank is a **kit** of seven pieces, each half a meter square, that you place on a second GridMap beside the ground's. Painting the pond's outline is the ground's job. Placing the right piece at each corner of that outline is the bank's, and lesson 47 does it with code. Here you model the pieces and place twelve of them by hand around a small pond, to see that they fit.

## How a bank kit works

The ground is a grid of cells half a meter on a side. A pond is some of those cells taken out. Each cell on the pond's edge has land on one side and water on the other, so the bank has to go between cells, along their shared edges and around their corners.

A kit that puts one piece on every ground cell needs a different piece for almost every neighbor. There is a trick that needs far fewer, called a **dual grid**. Put the bank's grid half a cell off the ground's, so that each bank cell sits on a corner of the ground grid and covers the four ground cells that meet there. Each of those four cells is land or water, so there are sixteen cases, and only four shapes:

| Water around the corner | Piece | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| None, or all four | nothing | all land is ground, all water is water |
| Two cells side by side | straight | a wall along the cell's middle |
| One cell | water corner | land on three sides, a notch of water in one corner |
| Three cells | land corner | one land cell, so the wall curves around it |
| Two cells diagonally | diagonal | two notches, in opposite corners |

The straight, the water corner, and the land corner can each be turned in four ways. The diagonal has only two that look different, and a turn of a quarter turn makes the other. That is fourteen cases, from four shapes. The kit has three straight pieces and two land corners so that a long bank does not repeat itself, and one each of the others, whose walls are too short to show a pattern.

The pieces share three rules, and the rest of the lesson is how to meet them.

- **The top is flat at 0.012 meters.** The ground tiles sit at 0.008 and reach 0.2 meters past their cells, as lesson 17 made them. The bank's top has to cover that overhang and sit just above it, so the bank takes the ground's own color and the two join without a seam.
- **The wall stands 0.2 meters into the water's cell.** That is as far as the ground's overhang reaches. A wall closer to the land would stand under the overhang. It also means a pond needs at least three cells across to show any water: a one-cell channel is only 0.1 meters wide between its two walls.
- **Every piece meets its neighbor the same way.** Where a piece ends, on any of its four edges, the wall is exactly 0.2 meters from the middle of the edge, and the wall's vertices are at exactly the same heights. Anything that moves the wall near an edge would open a crack, so the lumps you add die away to nothing before the edge.

The wall's foot is at -0.75 meters, which is the bed of the lake, and the water will be at -0.35. So the wall shows 0.35 meters above the water and 0.4 under it. Blender's Z is up, and Godot's is too, but Blender's Y is Godot's Z flipped, so the water side of every piece, which Godot sees at +Z, is at -Y in Blender: toward you in the front view.

### A mask for the lumps

You will roughen the wall with a modifier. It must not touch two places: the wall's two ends, so that it meets its neighbors, and the foot. It should move the lip only half as far as the wall, which gives the shore its wandering line. A **vertex group** holds a weight from 0 to 1 for each vertex, and a modifier can take it as a mask: a vertex with weight 0 stays put, one with weight 1 moves all the way.

The mask is built by selecting rings of vertices from the outside in. The wall's border is open mesh, so Blender can find it for you.

<Steps>

1. Press <Kbd keys="Alt+A" mac="Option+A" /> to select nothing. Choose **Select > Select All by Trait > Non Manifold**. In the panel at the bottom left, check that **Boundaries** is on. The border is selected: the wall's two ends, its foot, and the top's other three sides, 43 vertices in all.

2. Choose **Select > Invert** (<Kbd keys="Ctrl+I" mac="Cmd+I" />). Now the lip and the inside of the wall are selected, 180 vertices.

3. Click the **Data** tab in the Properties editor, the green triangle. Under **Vertex Groups**, click **+**, double-click the new group's name, and type `lumps`. In the **Weight** field below the list, which says `1.0`, type `0.5`, then click **Assign**. Every selected vertex gets half weight.

4. Choose **Select > Select More/Less > Less** (<Kbd keys="Ctrl+Numpad -" mac="Cmd+Numpad -" />). The selection shrinks by one ring: the lip, the row above the foot, and the first column inside each end drop out, leaving 130 vertices. Set **Weight** to `1.0` and click **Assign**.

	<Figure
		src={group}
		alt="The front view of the wall with an inner block of vertices selected in orange, leaving two columns on each side, one row at the top, and two rows at the bottom unselected."
		caption="The inner block is weight 1. The next ring in has weight 0.5, and the border has none."
	/>

</Steps>

Moving from the end inward, a vertex's weight is 0, then 0.5, then 1, and the 0.5 is what turns a cliff into a slope. The first two columns are 6 centimeters, close to the 7 the lumps need to die away in.

Press <Kbd keys="Tab" /> to return to Object Mode, and slide the piece a meter off the origin: <Kbd keys="G" />, <Kbd keys="X" />, type `1`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. You will see why when the lumps go on.

## All the lumps at once

The five lumpy pieces have a vertex group named `lumps`. They get their lumps from one modifier on the first straight piece, copied to the others and then applied.

### The modifier

A **Displace** modifier moves each vertex along its normal by the value of a texture. A procedural texture gives it a lumpy pattern with no image.

<Steps>

1. Click `bank_straight_a` in the Outliner, so it is the only piece selected. Click the **Modifiers** tab, the blue wrench, then **Add Modifier**, **Deform**, and **Displace**. Rename the modifier `Lumps`.

2. Click **New** in the modifier's **Texture** field. Click the **Texture** tab, the checkered square at the bottom of the tabs, and set **Type** to **Clouds**. Set **Noise Basis** to **Improved Perlin**, **Size** to `0.3`, and **Depth** to `3`.

3. Back on the modifier, set **Coordinates** to **Global**, **Direction** to **Normal**, **Strength** to `0.3`, **Midlevel** to `0.4`, and **Vertex Group** to `lumps`.

	<Figure
		src={displace}
		alt="The modifier's panel in the Properties editor: a modifier named Lumps with texture Lumps, Coordinates Global, Direction Normal, Strength 0.300, Midlevel 0.400, and the vertex group lumps."
		caption="The Displace modifier."
	/>

</Steps>

Here is what each setting does. The Clouds texture makes soft noise with values from 0 to 1, with features about 0.3 meters wide at three levels of detail. The modifier moves a vertex by **Strength** times the texture minus **Midlevel**, so the lumps are mostly outward, up to 0.3 times 0.6 = 18 centimeters, and a few hollows go 12 centimeters inward. **Direction** is **Normal**, so a vertex moves straight out of the wall, whichever way the wall faces: that is what lets the same modifier work on a curved piece. **Coordinates** is **Global**, which reads the texture at the vertex's place in the world and not on the piece. That is why you slid the piece a meter: two pieces at different places get different lumps, from one modifier and one texture.

### Copy and apply

<Steps>

1. In the Outliner, click `bank_land_corner_a`, <Kbd keys="Ctrl" />-click `bank_land_corner_b`, `bank_straight_b`, and `bank_straight_c`, and last <Kbd keys="Ctrl" />-click `bank_straight_a`, which makes it the active object. Choose **Object > Link/Transfer Data > Copy Modifiers**. Each of the five now has a `Lumps` modifier.

2. Choose **Object > Convert > Mesh**. The modifiers are applied, and the lumps are real geometry.

	<Figure

The lip moved with the lumps, half as far, and that is what you want sideways. But it also rose and fell, and it holds the top: a top that rises or falls would show through the ground or float above it. In the front view you will flatten it, so the lip's outline wanders and its height does not.

## The bank shader

The bank has two surfaces that must look like different things: a top that is exactly the ground, and a wall of earth. It has no texture of its own, no UVs, and no vertex colors. The shader tells them apart by height, since the top is flat at 0.012 and the wall is everything below, and it reads the wall's pattern from where the wall is in the world.

<Steps>

1. Click **Shader Editor** at the bottom of the window. In its **File** menu, choose **New Shader...**. Set **Path** to `res://art/shaders/bank.gdshader`, keep **Mode** on **Spatial**, and click **Create**.

	<Figure
		src={createShader}
		alt="The Create Shader dialog: Type Shader, Mode Spatial, Template Default, Path res://art/shaders/bank.gdshader."
		caption="A new shader beside the others."
	/>

</Steps>

Type into it, in this order, so that:

- `shader_type spatial;` is followed by `#include` lines for `stylized_lit.gdshaderinc` and `ground_color.gdshaderinc`, from lessons 17 and 23, so the wall lights like everything else and the top can ask for the ground's color;
- a comment says that these are the ground's own textures and noise, so the top of a piece is exactly the ground's color and joins the land without a seam, above five uniforms the ground shader has: `albedo_texture` (a `sampler2D` with `source_color`, `filter_linear_mipmap`, and `repeat_enable`), `albedo_tint` (a `vec3` with `source_color`, `vec3(1.0)`), `texture_scale` (`0.5`), `edge_noise` (a `sampler2D` with `filter_linear_mipmap` and `repeat_enable`), and `edge_noise_scale` (`0.4`);
- a group of uniforms named `earth` follows, opened with `group_uniforms earth;`, and closed with `group_uniforms;`. Each has a comment: `earth_color`, a `vec3` with `source_color`, `vec3(0.44, 0.32, 0.20)`, the wall's dark brown, which is in its own shadow most of the day; `clod_height`, a `sampler2D` with `filter_linear_mipmap` and `repeat_enable`, and `clod_normals`, a `sampler2D` with `hint_normal`, `filter_linear_mipmap`, and `repeat_enable`, one noise shown as a height map and as a normal map, read on the wall's own plane; `clod_scale`, a `float` with `hint_range(0.1, 4.0)`, `1.0`, how many times the clods repeat per meter; `clod_relief`, `hint_range(0.0, 2.0)`, `0.9`, how strongly the clods tilt the light on the wall; `sky_lean`, `hint_range(0.0, 1.0)`, `0.35`, how far the wall's light leans toward the sky, so that it reads as warm earth and not as a black outline; and `depth_darkening`, `hint_range(0.0, 1.0)`, `0.2`, how much darker the wall is at the water than at the lip;
- a second group, `turf`, holds `turf_length`, a `vec2`, `vec2(0.035, 0.085)`, how far the grass hangs over the top of the wall, in meters, at least and at most, and `turf_blades`, `hint_range(5.0, 60.0)`, `30.0`, how many blades hang per meter of wall;
- three constants follow, each with a comment: `TOP`, `0.012`, the height of a piece's top, from this lesson; `LIP`, `0.005`, how far below it the wall begins; and `WALL_DEPTH`, `0.35`, how deep the wall takes to darken fully, in meters;
- three varyings, `vec2 world_xz`, `vec3 world_normal`, and `float height`, are filled in `vertex()`: the world position's `xz`, the normal turned by `MODEL_NORMAL_MATRIX`, and `VERTEX.y`, the vertex's own height, which does not change when the GridMap puts the piece somewhere else;
- `fragment()` starts with `float wall = 1.0 - smoothstep(TOP - LIP - 0.004, TOP - LIP, height);`, a comment saying that the top is flat at `TOP` and the wall is below it, so the height alone tells them apart, and `vec3 ground = ground_color(albedo_texture, albedo_tint, texture_scale, edge_noise, edge_noise_scale, world_xz);`;
- the wall is read on its own vertical plane. `bool along_x = abs(world_normal.z) > abs(world_normal.x);` says whether the wall faces along z, and `vec2 plane = vec2(along_x ? world_xz.x : world_xz.y, height);` is the wall's own two coordinates, along it and up it;
- `float clod = texture(clod_height, plane * clod_scale).r;`, `float depth = clamp((TOP - height) / WALL_DEPTH, 0.0, 1.0);`, and `vec3 earth = earth_color * (0.8 + 0.4 * clod) * mix(1.0, 1.0 - depth_darkening, depth);` make the earth: its color, brighter on the clods and darker in the cracks, and darker toward the water;
- the turf hangs over the top. `float blade` is `abs(fract(plane.x * turf_blades + texture(edge_noise, world_xz * edge_noise_scale * 2.0).r * 4.0) - 0.5) * 2.0`, a zigzag along the wall that the noise bends; `float length_noise` is `texture(edge_noise, vec2(plane.x * 3.0, 0.5)).r`; `float hang` is `mix(turf_length.x, turf_length.y, length_noise) * (1.0 - 0.6 * blade)`; and `earth = mix(earth, ground * 0.85, step(TOP - height, hang));` paints the ground's color, a little darker, wherever the wall is within `hang` of its top;
- `ALBEDO = mix(ground, earth, wall);` and `ROUGHNESS = 0.95;` finish the color;
- a comment says that the clods tilt the wall's normal on its plane, and the whole normal leans toward the sky. `vec3 bump = texture(clod_normals, plane * clod_scale).xyz * 2.0 - 1.0;` unpacks the normal map; `vec3 across = along_x ? vec3(1.0, 0.0, 0.0) : vec3(0.0, 0.0, 1.0);` is the direction along the wall; `vec3 bumped = normalize(world_normal + clod_relief * (bump.x * across + bump.y * vec3(0.0, 1.0, 0.0)));` tilts the normal; `vec3 leaned = normalize(mix(bumped, vec3(0.0, 1.0, 0.0), sky_lean));` leans it; and `NORMAL = normalize(mix(NORMAL, (VIEW_MATRIX * vec4(leaned, 0.0)).xyz, wall));` puts it into view space, on the wall only;
- the last line, with a comment that the sky's glow, which the day animation already sets for the rim, lifts the wall that the sun never lights, is `EMISSION += soft_rim(NORMAL, INV_VIEW_MATRIX, ALBEDO) + wall * rim_color.rgb * rim_strength * ALBEDO * 2.2;`.

<Aside type="note" title="Why the wall faces the sky">
The wall faces the camera, and the sun never lights it, so under plain lighting it is a black strip along every bank. Leaning the wall's normal toward the sky, and adding the same glow that lifts the rim, makes it read as warm shadowed earth. ## Tune it

Every number that sets the bank's look is a uniform, so you can change it in the material, with the game running or in the editor, and watch it change.

- **`earth_color`** is the wall's brown. Make it redder for clay, or grayer for chalk.
- **`clod_scale`** sets the clods' size: `1.0` repeats every meter, so `2.0` makes them half as big. **`clod_relief`** sets how sharply they catch the light. `0` shows none at all.
- **`sky_lean`** is how far the wall's light turns toward the sky. At `0` the wall is a black strip against the grass. At `1` it is as lit as the top.
- **`depth_darkening`** darkens the wall toward the water. The water will cover its lower half in lesson 45.
- **`turf_length`** and **`turf_blades`** set how much grass hangs over the lip. A longer minimum gives a shaggy bank.

## If it does not work

- **The wall's edges have a gap where two pieces meet.** The lumps moved a vertex near the end, so the wall is not 0.2 meters from the edge's middle. Check the vertex group: the end column must have no weight, and the next one `0.5`. The group's first selection must have been made with **Non Manifold**, and the wall must be open at both ends.
- **Select All by Trait finds 0 vertices.** The pieces were joined into a closed mesh, or the wall is not extruded from the lip's edge. The mesh has to be open at the ends and at the foot.
- **The lip is not flat after the flattening.** **Pivot Point** was not set to **3D Cursor**, or the cursor is not at the origin. Press <Kbd keys="Shift+C" /> to bring the cursor back to the origin, and try the scale again.
- **The wall is smooth like glass, with no lumps.** The Displace modifier's **Vertex Group** is empty, or the group has no weights. Check the modifier's panel and the **Data** tab.
- **Every piece has the same lumps.** The modifier's **Coordinates** is **Local**, not **Global**.
- **The library's items are named `bank_Plane_003`.** **Use Node Names** was not checked when you imported. Check it and reimport.
- **The bank is gray, with no turf or clods.** The material did not change: the Advanced Import Settings' **Use External** is off, or its path is empty.
- **The shader shows an error about `rim_color`.** The shared lighting include is not found, or its globals are not in **Project Settings > Globals > Shader Globals**. Lesson 23 made both.
- **Each piece stands in the wrong place.** The **Bank** GridMap's position is not `-0.25, 0, -0.25`, or its **Cell > Size** is not `0.5`.
- **A piece's wall faces the land.** It needs two more quarter turns: press <Kbd keys="S" /> twice with the piece selected in the palette, or use the table.
- **The pond is full of grass.** Press **Scatter** on the **Grass** node after you erase the ground cells.
