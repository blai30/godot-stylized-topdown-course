## What you will build

In lesson 44 you placed twelve bank pieces by hand around a pond of nine cells, working out the turn of each. A pond is never nine cells. You paint it, change your mind, paint it again, and the bank has to follow. This lesson writes the code that does it: a button, **Update shore**, that clears the bank and places every piece again, from where the water is.

## One question, sixteen answers

The bank's GridMap sits half a cell off the ground's, so each of its cells is on a corner where four ground cells meet, as lesson 44 set up. For each such corner the script asks one question, **which of the four cells are water?**, and the answer is one of sixteen. Sixteen answers is few enough for a table, and a table is the clean way to write the logic: it is data, and it can be read and checked, where a tangle of `if` statements can only be followed.

Give each of the four cells a number, so that an answer is a set of numbers:

| Cell around the corner | Number |
|---|---|
| lower x, lower z | 0 |
| lower x, higher z | 1 |
| higher x, lower z | 2 |
| higher x, higher z | 3 |

An answer is those cells' bits added up: `1 << number` for each. All four cells land or all four water is nothing to draw. The rest are the pieces' shapes, each in four turns.

The script does not list the fourteen cases by hand. It lists the **four** shapes, as the cells that are water when a piece is not turned, as it comes out of Blender, and builds the table from them by turning each shape, one quarter turn at a time, four times. A quarter turn about the vertical axis moves each cell around the corner to the next, and the table keeps what the answer is after each. That is why turning is all the table has to know: a straight piece with water at its back is the same piece as one with water at its right, turned.

## The bank map

<Steps>

1. In the FileSystem dock, make `src/Ground/BankMap.cs`, a C# script that inherits `GridMap`. Select the `Ground` folder, use **Script > Create Script...** in the Script editor, and set **Inherits** to `GridMap`, **Language** to **C#**, and **Path** to `res://src/Ground/BankMap.cs`.

</Steps>

Write the script so that:

- the class is `public partial class BankMap : GridMap`, in the `TopDownTemplate.Ground` namespace, with `[Tool]` above it, so it runs in the editor, and a comment: edges every pond with the bank kit; the bank's GridMap sits half a cell off the ground's, so each of its cells is on a corner of the ground grid and covers the four ground cells that meet there; Update shore looks at which of the four are water and puts the piece that fits, turned to face the water;
- a constant `MinimumWidth` is `3`, with a comment: a pond narrower than this many cells has walls that meet, and shows no water. Lesson 44 worked out why: each wall stands 0.2 meters into the water's cell, so one cell of water is only 0.1 meters wide;
- a static array `Shapes` of a prefix and the water cells, with a comment that the four cells around a corner are numbered `x * 2 + z`, with `x` and `z` 0 for the lower cell and 1 for the higher, and that each piece is the cells that are water when it is not turned, the way it comes out of Blender: `("bank_straight", [(0, 1), (1, 1)])`, `("bank_water_corner", [(1, 1)])`, `("bank_land_corner", [(0, 1), (1, 0), (1, 1)])`, and `("bank_diagonal", [(0, 1), (1, 0)])`. These match lesson 44's pieces: the water side of every piece is at +z, which in Blender is the front;
- a private record struct `Placement(string Prefix, int Turns)` is what the table gives for an answer. `Placements` is a static `Dictionary<int, Placement>`, built once by `BuildPlacements()`, with a comment: for each set of water cells around a corner, the piece to place and how many quarter turns it takes;
- `[Export] public GroundMap? Ground { get; set; }` is the ground to read, and `[ExportToolButton("Update shore")] public Callable UpdateShoreButton => Callable.From(UpdateShore);` is the button, as in lesson 34's scatter;
- `BuildPlacements()` loops over the shapes and over `turns` from 0 to 3. For each, `cells` is the answer with each water cell `Turn`ed: `cells |= 1 << (turnedX * 2 + turnedZ);`. It adds `table.TryAdd(cells, new Placement(prefix, turns))`, with a comment: a diagonal looks the same after two turns, so its first turns are the ones kept. `TryAdd` leaves an answer that is already in the table alone;
- `Turn(int x, int z, int turns)` has a comment: where a cell around the corner goes after quarter turns about the vertical axis. For each turn it does `(x, z) = (z, 1 - x);` and returns the pair. That is the cell one place around the corner, the way a quarter turn about the vertical axis moves a point in the plan;
- `public void UpdateShore()` starts by warning and returning, with `GD.PushWarning`, if `Ground` or `MeshLibrary` is missing. It takes `var used = Ground.GetUsedCells();` and returns if there are none. It calls `Clear()`, which empties the GridMap, so pressing the button again starts from nothing;
- `variants` groups the library's items by the prefix of their names: `MeshLibrary.GetItemList().GroupBy(...)`, with each group's items ordered, and a dictionary from the prefix, `bank_straight` or the like, to the list of items, `a`, `b`, and `c` for the straight pieces;
- `var water = used.Where(Ground.IsWater).Select(cell => new Vector2I(cell.X, cell.Z)).ToHashSet();` is every water cell, by its place in the grid, and `WarnAboutNarrowWater(water);` is described below;
- the loops cover every corner of the ground, which is one more than its cells in each direction: `low` is the smallest `x` and `z` among the used cells, `high` the largest, and `x` and `z` run from `low` to `high + 1`;
- for each corner, `cells` starts at 0, and each of the four cells that is in `water`, at `(x - 1 + cornerX, z - 1 + cornerZ)`, adds `1 << (cornerX * 2 + cornerZ)`;
- `if (!Placements.TryGetValue(cells, out var placement)) { continue; }` leaves a corner with no entry alone: all land and all water are not in the table;
- a comment says the variant comes from the cell, so pressing the button again puts the same pieces in the same places. `var items = variants[placement.Prefix];` and `var item = items[Mathf.PosMod(x * 7 + z * 13, items.Length)];` choose it, with `PosMod` because the cell's number can be negative;
- `var turn = new Basis(Vector3.Up, placement.Turns * Mathf.Pi / 2f);` is the piece's turn as a basis, and `SetCellItem(new Vector3I(x, 0, z), item, GetOrthogonalIndexFromBasis(turn));` places it. A GridMap stores a piece's rotation as one of 24 orthogonal orientations, and `GetOrthogonalIndexFromBasis` is how a basis becomes its number. That is the whole reason the rotation is built from a `Basis`;
- `placed` is counted, and `GD.Print($"Update shore placed {placed} pieces.");` says it;
- `WarnAboutNarrowWater(HashSet<Vector2I> water)` has a comment: water that is narrower than `MinimumWidth` in either direction has walls that meet. It finds the water cells whose shorter run, `Mathf.Min(Run(water, cell, Vector2I.Right), Run(water, cell, Vector2I.Down))`, is less than `MinimumWidth`, and pushes one warning if there are any, `GD.PushWarning($"{narrow.Count} water cells lie in water narrower than {MinimumWidth} cells, such as the one at {narrow[0]}.");`;
- `Run(...)` has a comment: how many water cells in a row there are through a cell, along a direction and against it. It counts the cell, then the cells beyond it in the direction, then those in the opposite one.

## Check the table

The first test of a table is every row of it. A small ground made for the purpose held all sixteen answers, each in a block of its own with land around it, and the script was run on it. The pieces it chose for the corner in the middle of each block were compared with a table worked out separately, by another program, one answer at a time. All sixteen agreed: no water and all water gave nothing, and the other fourteen gave the right piece turned the right way.

You can check the first part on your own pond too. The pond from lesson 44 was shaped by hand, with each piece's turn worked out by looking at it. The script, given the same pond, chooses the same twelve shapes and the same turns. Press the button now:

<Steps>

1. Select **Bank** and click **Update shore**. The Output panel says `Update shore placed 12 pieces.` The pond looks as it did, but the three straight variants may be in other places: the script picks them from the cell and you picked them as you liked.

2. Click it again. Nothing changes: the same twelve pieces in the same places. That is what the `Clear()` and the variant chosen from the cell are for. A button that placed different pieces every time would make a version control diff of the scene noisy, and no pond would ever stay as you liked it.

</Steps>

## Water that is too narrow

A pond one cell wide cannot be drawn. Each wall stands 0.2 meters into the water's cell, so two walls are 0.4 meters in, and one cell is 0.5 meters wide: a channel 0.1 meters wide, with the two walls' lumps meeting in it. The script warns about this in the Output panel.

<Steps>

1. Paint a one-cell channel of Water, and click **Update shore**. The Output panel shows a warning of the form `12 water cells lie in water narrower than 3 cells, such as the one at (0, 1).` The warning counts every water cell in the channel and names one of them. The bank is placed anyway.

2. Erase the channel with the GridMap's erase tool, and press the button again.

</Steps>

The rule counts a water cell as narrow when its shorter run, along x or along z, is under three. A cell in the arm of a long, wide pond has a long run one way and a run of three or more the other, so it is fine. A cell in a channel has a run of one one way. The script does not forbid it, because a game might want it, and a warning that names the cell is more useful than a refusal.

## Try it

- Paint an island of one land cell in the middle of a wide pond, with at least three cells of water on every side, and press the button. Four land corners go round it. What happens with an island of two cells that touch at a corner? The diagonal piece appears, with its two notches.
- Change `MinimumWidth` to `2`. What does a channel two cells wide look like, with the lumps on its two walls? Put it back if you do not like it.
- The straight pieces are chosen by `x * 7 + z * 13`. Change the numbers and press the button: the same pond gets its three variants in other places. Is there a number that makes a long bank repeat?

## If it does not work

- **There is no Update shore button.** The script was not built, or it is not on the **Bank** node. Click the hammer, and check the node's **Script**. The class needs `[Tool]` above it.
- **The button does nothing.** **Ground** or the node's mesh library is empty, and the Output panel says so. Assign **GroundTiles**, and check that **Mesh Library** is `bank_kit.glb`.
- **The pieces stand in the wrong places.** The **Bank** node's position is not `-0.25, 0, -0.25`, or its cell size is not `0.5`, from lesson 44.
- **A wall faces the land.** The piece's water side in Blender was not at the front, the way lesson 44 built it. The table assumes the water side is at +z.
- **Nothing is placed.** There is no Water in the ground: the **Is Water** property of the Water tile is off. Open `ground_tile_set.tres`.
- **The same pond gets different pieces each time.** The variant was chosen at random. It must come from the cell, so the button can be pressed twice.
- **The script does not compile: `Where(Ground.IsWater)`.** `GroundMap` has no `IsWater` method. Add it, and build.
- **Grass grows in the new water.** Press **Scatter** on the **Grass** node.
