## What a shader is

A shader is a small program that runs on your graphics card and decides how a surface is drawn. Godot runs it for every part of a surface on every frame, so it must be short and fast. A 3D shader has two main parts you write:

- The [vertex function](/reference/glossary/#vertex-function), `vertex()`, runs once for every corner point of the mesh. It can move points and pass values along.
- The [fragment function](/reference/glossary/#fragment-function), `fragment()`, runs once for every pixel the surface covers on screen. It decides that pixel's color, roughness, and other surface properties.

Godot then lights the result with your sun and sky. You only describe the surface; you do not write the lighting.

## Write the shader, piece by piece

You write the shader in four pieces. After each piece, save it with **File > Save** in the Shader Editor (<Kbd keys="Ctrl+Alt+S" mac="Cmd+Option+S" />) and look at the tile in the 3D view.

<Aside type="tip" title="Saving shaders">
In the Shader Editor, <Kbd keys="Ctrl+Alt+S" mac="Cmd+Option+S" /> saves the shader. Plain <Kbd keys="Ctrl+S" mac="Cmd+S" /> saves the scene. A moment after you stop typing, Godot shows your changes in the 3D view, but they only reach the file when you save. Until then, the file's name in the Shader Editor's list shows `(*)` after it.

The first time you save a shader Godot has newly created, it may ask about **The following files are newer on disk**. What you typed is what you want, so click **Ignore external changes**, then save again.
</Aside>

### Piece 2: a texture

Now read the color from your grass [texture](/reference/glossary/#texture) instead. A [uniform](/reference/glossary/#uniform) is written as the word `uniform`, then its type, then its name. After the name you can add a colon and hints, separated by commas, and the line ends with a semicolon. Add, between `shader_type spatial;` and `fragment()`:

- a uniform named `albedo_texture` of type `sampler2D` (a texture the shader can read), with the hints `source_color`, `filter_linear_mipmap`, and `repeat_enable`

and change `fragment()` so it sets `ALBEDO` to the color of `albedo_texture` at the [mesh](/reference/glossary/#mesh)'s `UV` coordinates. The function `texture(albedo_texture, UV)` returns four numbers (red, green, blue, and alpha); take the first three by adding `.rgb` after it.

<Aside type="note" title="What the three hints do">
- `source_color` says this texture holds colors. Color images are stored in [sRGB](/reference/glossary/#srgb), and Godot converts them before lighting. Without the hint, the grass looks washed out.
- `filter_linear_mipmap` smooths the texture between pixels and uses its [mipmaps](/reference/glossary/#mipmap), the smaller copies made on import, when the ground is far away. Without it, distant ground shimmers.
- `repeat_enable` repeats the texture past its edges instead of smearing the last row of pixels. The ground needs it, as you see in piece 3.
</Aside>

### See the problem with UV

[UV](/reference/glossary/#uv) coordinates are stored in the mesh, so the texture is glued to the tile. That breaks as soon as a second tile is turned or overlaps the first. Try it:

<Steps>

1. Select `TestTile` and press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+D" mac="Cmd+D" /> to duplicate it. Godot names the copy `TestTile2`.

2. With `TestTile2` selected, set **Transform > Position** to `1.4`, `0.012`, `0.6` and **Rotation** **y** to `30`. The y of `0.012` lifts it 2 millimeters above `TestTile`, so where they overlap they do not fight over pixels. The copy shares the same material, so it shows the same texture.

3. To make the problem easy to see, select `TestTile`, find **Albedo Texture** under **Shader Parameters** again, and put `icon.svg` in place of the grass for a moment: click the grass picture in the field, choose **Quick Load...**, type `icon.svg`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. `icon.svg` is the Godot logo that every new project starts with, at the bottom of the FileSystem dock.

4. Save the scene with <Kbd keys="Ctrl+S" mac="Cmd+S" />.

</Steps>

<Figure
	src={uvProblem}
	alt="Two overlapping tiles, each showing one large Godot logo. The second tile's logo is turned 30 degrees with its tile, so the two logos cross each other where the tiles overlap."

Each tile shows its own whole image, turned with the tile. With grass the clash is subtler, but it is there at every tile edge. The fix is to ignore the mesh's UV and ask a different question: where is this pixel in the world?

Enabling mipmap generation and setting the texture compression mode to VRAM Compressed (S3TC).` Godot noticed you used the icon on a 3D surface and reimported it with the settings 3D textures need, the same ones you chose for your grass in lesson 13. It is harmless.
</Aside>

### Piece 3: world space

Change the shader so each pixel samples the texture by its position in the world:

- Declare a [varying](/reference/glossary/#varying) named `world_position` of type `vec3`. A varying carries a value from the vertex function to the fragment function.
- Add a `vertex()` function, shaped like `fragment()`, that sets `world_position` to the vertex's position in the [world](/reference/glossary/#world-space). Inside `vertex()`, `VERTEX` is the point's position relative to the mesh itself, and `MODEL_MATRIX` is the [matrix](/reference/glossary/#matrix) that places the mesh in the world. Multiplying the matrix by the position moves the point into world coordinates. The matrix needs four numbers, so build a `vec4` from `VERTEX` with a fourth number of `1.0`, which marks it as a position, and keep the first three numbers of the result with `.xyz`.
- Add a uniform `float` (a number with a decimal point) named `texture_scale`, with a default value of `0.5`: write `= 0.5` after the name. It is how many times the texture repeats per meter.
- In `fragment()`, sample the texture at the pixel's world position instead of `UV`. The ground is flat, so use only the world's X and Z axes, which `world_position.xz` gives you, and multiply them by `texture_scale`.

### Piece 4: roughness and comments

Finish the shader:

- Add a uniform `float` named `roughness` with the hint `hint_range(0.0, 1.0)` and the default `0.9`. The hint comes first, after a colon, and the default last: `: hint` then `= value`. Then set `ROUGHNESS` to it in `fragment()`. [Roughness](/reference/glossary/#roughness) 0.9 keeps grass matte, with no shiny highlights.
- Add a short comment, a line starting with `//`, above `albedo_texture` and above `texture_scale` that says what each one is for.

## Try it

- Select `TestTile2` and drag it around with the arrows of its move [gizmo](/reference/glossary/#gizmo), or turn it with the rings. The grass stays still while the tile slides over it, and there is never a seam.
- Change **Texture Scale** to `1` and then `0.25`. The grass gets smaller and bigger on both tiles at once, because they share one material. Set it back to `0.5`.

## If it does not work

- **The tile disappears and a line turns red in the Shader Editor.** The shader has an error, described at the bottom of the Shader Editor, such as `Error at line 5: Expected a ';'.` A missing semicolon is reported on the line *after* the one that needs it, so look one line up. The other common cause is a misspelled name, such as `albedo_texure`.
- **The grass looks pale and washed out.** The `source_color` hint is missing from `albedo_texture`.
- **The tile is plain white.** The texture is not assigned. Pick `grass_albedo.png` for **Albedo Texture** under **Shader Parameters** with **Quick Load...**.
- **The tile is white after your first save, and your typing is gone.** Godot asked about files newer on disk and **Reload from disk** was chosen, which loads the empty template again. Type the piece again, save, and choose **Ignore external changes** if Godot asks.
- **The texture smears into long stripes.** The `repeat_enable` hint is missing.
- **The tile flickers with stripes of gray.** It sits at the same height as the placeholder ground. Set its **Position** **y** to `0.01`.
- **Your shader changes disappear when you reopen the project.** The shader was not saved. Use <Kbd keys="Ctrl+Alt+S" mac="Cmd+Option+S" /> in the Shader Editor.
- **The test tiles are gone when you reopen the scene.** The scene was not saved. Add them again and press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+S" mac="Cmd+S" />.
