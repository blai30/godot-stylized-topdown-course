## The idea

Three small tricks, each simple on its own, make the grid disappear:

1. **Each tile overhangs its cell.** A tile is 0.9 meters wide on a 0.5 meter cell, so it reaches 0.2 meters into each neighbor.
2. **The overhang is cut with noise.** The shader keeps the whole cell, and keeps only part of the overhang, along a ragged line set by a [noise texture](/reference/glossary/#noise-texture). The cut is made with [alpha scissor](/reference/glossary/#alpha-scissor): each pixel is drawn fully or not at all, so the edge stays crisp, like a brush stroke, not a blur.
3. **Each kind of ground has its own layer.** Sand sits lowest, dirt 4 millimeters above it, and grass 4 millimeters above that. Where grass meets dirt, the grass's ragged overhang lies on top of the dirt, as grass grows over a path's edge. Two tiles of the same kind overlap at the same height, but they show the same world-space texture, so you cannot see where one ends.

None of the tiles needs to know its neighbors. That is what keeps the shader short.

<Aside type="note" title="Why Center Offset and not Position">
The MeshLibrary keeps each tile's mesh, not its node's position, so the height must live in the mesh itself. **Center Offset** moves the plane inside its own mesh. Four millimeters is too little for the eye to see from the game camera, and enough for the graphics card to tell which surface is on top: at the same height, two different textures would flicker in stripes, which is called [z-fighting](/reference/glossary/#z-fighting).
</Aside>

## Piece 1: cut the overhang

Double-click `art/ground/ground.gdshader` in the FileSystem dock to open it in the Shader Editor. Change it so that:

- the comment above `albedo_texture` says the textures come from lessons 13 and 14 (change it if yours says otherwise);
- there are two new uniforms: a `float` named `overhang` with the default `0.2`, how far the tile reaches past its cell, and a `float` named `cell_size` with the default `0.5`, the GridMap's cell size, each with a comment;
- there is a second varying, a `vec2` (two numbers, here a position along x and z) named `tile_position`, which `vertex()` sets to `VERTEX.xz`: the point's position on the tile itself, from the tile's center, in meters;
- at the end of `fragment()`, a `float` named `outside` measures how far the pixel is past the cell's edge, as a share of the overhang: 0 inside the cell, 1 at the tile's outer edge. Write it as `length(max(abs(tile_position) - cell_size * 0.5, 0.0)) / overhang`;
- `ALPHA` is set to `1.0 - outside`, and `ALPHA_SCISSOR_THRESHOLD` to `0.5`.

<Aside type="note" title="How the distance works">
`abs(tile_position)` folds the four sides of the tile onto one, and subtracting half a cell leaves how far past the cell's edge the pixel is, along each axis. `max(..., 0.0)` turns everything inside the cell into 0, and `length` combines the two axes into one distance, which rounds the corners. `ALPHA` is how opaque the pixel is, and with `ALPHA_SCISSOR_THRESHOLD`, Godot draws every pixel with an alpha of 0.5 or more fully and skips the rest.
</Aside>

## Piece 2: a ragged edge

Replace the straight cut with noise. Change the shader so that:

- there is a uniform `sampler2D` named `edge_noise` with the hints `filter_linear_mipmap` and `repeat_enable`, and a uniform `float` named `edge_noise_scale` with the default `0.4`, how many times the noise repeats per meter, each with a comment;
- in `fragment()`, a `float` named `noise` reads the red channel of `edge_noise` at `world_position.xz * edge_noise_scale`;
- `ALPHA` is `noise + 0.5 - outside`, and `ALPHA_SCISSOR_THRESHOLD` stays the last line of `fragment()`;
- after that line, a `float` named `tone` reads the same noise again, at `world_position.xz * edge_noise_scale * 0.25`, four times as wide, and `ALBEDO` is multiplied by `0.75 + 0.5 * tone`, with a comment saying the ground brightens and darkens slowly so the texture's repeat does not show.

The noise is a value from 0 to 1 that changes smoothly across the ground. Inside the cell, `outside` is 0, so `ALPHA` is at least 0.5 and the cell is always covered, with no holes. At the tile's outer edge, `outside` is 1, so `ALPHA` is below 0.5 and nothing is drawn. Between them, where the cut falls depends on the noise, and the noise is different everywhere, so the edge wanders.

The last two lines use the noise a second time for something else. A tileable texture repeats every 2 meters, and over a wide field the eye finds the repeat. A slow change in brightness, which is different everywhere because it reads the noise by world position, hides it: the same grass is a little lighter here and a little darker there. The noise is 0.5 on average, so `0.75 + 0.5 * tone` averages 1 and the ground keeps its color.

Save the shader. Now make the noise texture.

<Steps>

1. Select `Grass`. Open its PlaneMesh, then its **Material**, and under **Shader Parameters**, click the **Edge Noise** field and under **New**, choose **NoiseTexture2D**.

2. Click the new texture to open it. Set **Width** and **Height** to `256` and check **Seamless**, so it repeats with no visible edge.

3. Click the **Noise** field and under **New**, choose **FastNoiseLite**. Click it to open it, and set **Frequency** to `0.03` and, under **Fractal**, **Octaves** to `3`. A low frequency makes broad, gentle wobbles; fewer octaves keep the edge clean instead of fuzzy.

## Smooth the outlines

The ground now looks right, but the outlines of the rocks and the player show small steps, because the graphics card decides each pixel as covered or not covered. **Anti-aliasing** fixes that. The kind you turn on here, **MSAA** for multisample anti-aliasing, tests several points inside each pixel along a model's edge and blends the result.

<Steps>

1. Choose **Project > Project Settings...** and click **Rendering > Anti Aliasing** in the list on the left.

2. Set **Quality > MSAA 3D** to **4x (Slow)**, and close the window.

	<Figure
		src={msaa}
		alt="Project Settings on Rendering, Anti Aliasing: MSAA 2D Disabled, MSAA 3D 4x, Screen Space AA Disabled, Use TAA off."
		caption="MSAA 3D at 4x."
	/>

</Steps>

MSAA smooths model outlines, not the ragged edge of your ground tiles: the shader cuts that edge, and its small steps look hand-drawn, which is what you want. A lower setting, **2x**, is cheaper on a weak graphics card.

## Try it

- Press <Kbd keys="F5" mac="Cmd+B" />. From the game camera, the path winds through the grass with no grid in sight, and the rocks have smooth outlines.
- Double-click `edge_noise.tres` in the FileSystem dock, open its **Noise**, and try a **Frequency** of `0.08`. The edges in the meadow turn jagged and busy. Set it back to `0.03`.
- In the tiles scene, select `Grass`, open its mesh and material as in the noise steps, set **Overhang** to `0.1`, export, and look: the steps of the grid come back. Set it back to `0.2` and export again. The overhang and the mesh size must match: a tile 0.9 meters wide on a 0.5 meter cell reaches 0.2 meters past it.

## If it does not work

- **The tiles look the same as before.** The MeshLibrary was not exported again after the change. Export it from `ground_tiles.tscn`.
- **There are holes in the ground.** **Size** is smaller than the 0.5 meter cell.
- **The edges look cut off straight.** **Size** is smaller than the cell plus twice the overhang, so the mesh ends before the noise does. With 0.5 meter cells and an overhang of 0.2, the size is 0.9.
- **Stripes flicker where two kinds of ground meet.** Two tiles have the same **Center Offset**. Give sand 0, dirt 0.004, and grass 0.008.
- **The ground is the same everywhere and the repeat shows.** The `tone` lines are missing, or **Edge Noise** is empty on that tile, which reads as white and gives a flat 1.25.
- **The edges are perfectly straight.** **Edge Noise** is empty on that tile's material: an empty texture reads as white, so the whole overhang is drawn. Load `edge_noise` into it.
- **The edges follow a visible repeating pattern.** The noise is not seamless. Check **Seamless** on `edge_noise.tres`.
