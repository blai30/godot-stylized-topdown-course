## What you will build

Two ground textures to sit beside your grass: dirt, with pebbles of many sizes and a few twigs, and sand, with soft ripples, damp patches, and a scatter of small stones and shell bits. You paint them by hand in Krita, with brushes you make yourself. In lesson 17 the three meet with soft edges on the ground.

## A texture that repeats

Ground is too big for one picture. Instead, one small square image repeats across it like floor tiles. For that to work, whatever leaves the image at its right edge must come back in at its left edge, and the same at the top and bottom. Paint a stroke that stops at the edge, and every copy shows a hard line there, a **seam**. A texture that avoids this is [tileable](../../reference/glossary/#tileable-texture).

Krita's **wrap-around mode** solves this while you paint. It shows copies of your image all around it, and a stroke that runs off one edge continues on the opposite edge. You paint on the copies as if they were one endless surface, and the seams never appear.

Your texture is 512 by 512 pixels and covers 2 by 2 meters of ground, so each meter gets 256 pixels. That number is the [texel density](../../reference/glossary/#texel-density). The grass detail from lesson 13 is 1024 pixels for the same 2 meters, so it is twice as fine; the hand-painted textures use bolder shapes, which suit that density.

## How to make textures match

Textures that sit side by side must look like one world. Three things keep them together:

- **The same scale.** Every texture covers 2 meters, so a pebble and a grass blade are drawn at sizes that make sense next to each other: a large pebble about 20 cm, 50 pixels across.
- **The same value range.** Each texture uses its own four-step ramp from your palette, from shadow to highlight, so none looks much darker or brighter than the others.
- **The same style.** Every feature is a clean shape with a darker base and a lit edge, painted back to front, like the blades of the grass detail.

The reference ramps from lesson 7, as hue, saturation, and value:

- **Dirt**: 20, 44, 40; 26, 46, 53; 32, 44, 66; 38, 38, 79
- **Sand**: 30, 38, 52; 36, 40, 65; 42, 36, 78; 48, 28, 90

## Start the dirt

<Steps>

1. Open Krita and choose **File > New...** (<Kbd keys="Ctrl+N" mac="Cmd+N" />). On the **Dimensions** tab, set **Width** and **Height** to `512` pixels.

	<Figure
		src={newDimensions}
		alt="Krita's Create new document dialog on the Dimensions tab: Width 512 and Height 512 in Pixels, Resolution 300 Pixels/Inch, Model RGB/Alpha, Depth 8-bit integer/channel, and an sRGB profile."
		caption="512 by 512 pixels: 2 meters of ground at 256 pixels per meter."
	/>

2. Click the **Content** tab, set **Name** to `dirt_albedo`, and click **Create**. [Albedo](../../reference/glossary/#albedo) is a surface's plain color; the name says what the texture is for.

3. Click the **foreground color** square in the toolbar, as in lesson 7. On the **HSV** tab, type the dirt ramp's **light** swatch, `32`, `44`, `66`, pressing <Kbd keys="Tab" /> after each, and click **OK**. Choose **Edit > Fill with Foreground Color** (<Kbd keys="Shift+Backspace" mac="Shift+Delete" />). With nothing selected, Krita fills the whole layer, **Paint Layer 1**.

4. Choose **View > Wrap Around Mode**, then zoom out a step with <Kbd keys="Ctrl+-" mac="Cmd+-" />. Krita now draws copies of the image all around it; while the image is one flat color, you cannot see them yet.

5. Press <Kbd keys="B" /> for the **Freehand Brush Tool**, search the Brush Presets docker for `Basic-2 Opacity`, and click it. It paints a hard-edged round dab. Set **Size** to `90` and **Opacity** to `25`. Fields such as **Size** and **Opacity** are sliders: a left click or drag changes the value by where you click. To type an exact number, right-click the field, type, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />.

6. Set the color to the **dark** swatch, `26`, `46`, `53`, and paint about six short, wandering strokes, each about a quarter of the image long. Then set the **highlight** swatch, `38`, `38`, `79`, and paint six more between them. Each see-through dab keeps its hard edge, so overlapping dabs build flat, painted blotches instead of a blurry smudge.

	<Figure
		src={blotches}
		alt="Brown canvas with large, soft-cornered blotches of slightly darker and lighter brown, each built from overlapping round dabs with visible edges."
		caption="Blotches: big shapes for the eye at a distance."
	/>

</Steps>

## Paint a pebble

A stylized pebble has three parts, each a clean shape: a dark **contact shadow** where it sits on the ground, the stone's **body**, and a **lit rim** along its top edge, where the light catches it. You paint it once, in grays, and make it a **stamp brush**, a brush that prints your painting over and over. Texture artists paint a few good shapes by hand and let a stamp repeat them, turned and sized a little differently each time.

<Steps>

1. Choose **Layer > New > Add Paint Layer**.

2. **The shadow.** Choose the **Elliptical Selection Tool** (<Kbd keys="J" />) and drag a flat oval near the middle of the image, about 90 pixels wide and 35 tall. Set the color to a gray a little darker than the stone (Saturation `0`, Value `48`), and fill the selection (<Kbd keys="Shift+Backspace" mac="Shift+Delete" />). Choose **Select > Deselect** (<Kbd keys="Ctrl+Shift+A" mac="Cmd+Shift+A" />).

3. **The body.** Choose the **Polygonal Selection Tool**: click and hold the selection tool button in the toolbox, and pick it from the group that opens. Click seven points around a stone shape about 90 pixels wide, sitting on the upper part of the shadow so the shadow peeks out below it, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" /> to close the shape. Uneven corners make it look chipped, like a real stone. Set the color to a mid gray (Saturation `0`, Value `55`), and fill.

	<Figure
		src={pebbleBody}
		alt="A gray seven-sided stone shape outlined by the selection's marching ants, sitting on a darker gray oval that shows below it."
		caption="The body, still selected, on its shadow."
	/>

4. **The rim.** Keep the stone selected: a selection keeps paint inside it, so you can paint along its edge without going over. Press <Kbd keys="B" />, pick **Basic-2 Opacity**, set **Size** to `16` and **Opacity** to `100`, and set a light gray, value `60`. Drag one stroke along the stone's upper edges, close inside the outline.

5. **The shine.** Set **Size** to `8` and a lighter gray, value `64`, and add one short stroke near the top left. Then deselect.

	<Figure
		src={pebble}
		alt="The finished gray pebble: a dark oval shadow below, a mid-gray chipped body, a light band along its upper edges, and a small bright mark at the top left."
		caption="Shadow, body, rim, shine: four shapes make a stone."
	/>

</Steps>

<Aside type="note" title="Why this works">
Light comes from above in your game, so tops are lit and the ground under an object is shaded. You do not paint light and shadow on every pebble by hand: you paint them once into the stamp, and every printed pebble carries them. The brush will also turn each pebble to a random angle, so its lit side faces a different way each time; at this size that reads as variety, not as a second light.
</Aside>

## Make the pebble brush

<Steps>

1. Hide **Paint Layer 1** and **Background** with their eyes, draw a rectangle selection (<Kbd keys="Ctrl+R" mac="Cmd+R" />) around the pebble and its shadow, press <Kbd keys="B" />, and pick **Basic-2 Opacity**.

2. Press <Kbd keys="F5" mac="Fn+F5" /> to open the **Brush Editor**. At its bottom, uncheck **Temporarily Save Tweaks To Presets**. With it checked, Krita remembers every change you make to a built-in brush until you close Krita, so the next time you pick that brush it is not the one you expect. Unchecked, your changes live only in the brushes you save. Krita remembers this setting.

3. Click **Brush Tip** in the editor's middle column, the **Predefined** tab, and **Stamp**. Name it `Pebble`, uncheck **Create mask from color**, and click **Save**.

4. Set **Brush mode** to **Lightness map**, so the tip's middle gray paints the foreground color, darker grays paint darker, and lighter grays lighter. Right-click **Spacing**, type `5`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />: pebbles print far apart along a stroke.

	<Figure
		src={pebbleTip}
		alt="The Brush Tip page with the Pebble tip selected, Spacing 5.00, and Brush mode Lightness map."
		caption="Your pebble as a brush tip."
	/>

5. Check the box beside **Rotation** and click its name, then in its inputs check **Fuzzy Dab** and uncheck **Pressure**: each pebble lies at a random angle.

6. Check the box beside **Size** and click its name, check **Fuzzy Dab** and uncheck **Pressure**, click the curve's left point, and type `33` in the percentage field beside the curve and press <Kbd keys="Tab" />: pebbles print from a third of full size to full, from small gravel to big stones.

	<Figure
		src={pebbleSize}
		alt="The Size option with Fuzzy Dab checked and a curve rising from 33% at the left to 100% at the right."
		caption="From a third of full size to full."
	/>

7. Check **Scatter**. Click **Save New Brush Preset...**, name it `Pebble`, click **Save**, and close the editor with <Kbd keys="F5" mac="Fn+F5" />.

8. Deselect, show **Paint Layer 1** and **Background** again, hide **Paint Layer 2**, and click **Paint Layer 1**.

</Steps>

## Make a twig brush

A pen tablet makes a stroke thin where you press lightly. A mouse presses evenly, so its strokes are the same width from end to end. Krita's brushes can taper by another rule instead: **Fade** shrinks the brush as the stroke gets longer. You use it for twigs and small roots.

<Steps>

1. In the **Brush Presets** docker, click the **Search** box at the bottom and type `Basic-5 Size`. Two brushes are left; hold the pointer over each to see its name, and click **b) Basic-5 Size**, not **Basic-5 Size Opacity**.

	<Figure
		src={presetSearch}
		alt="The Brush Presets docker with Basic-5 Size typed into its Search box at the bottom and two brushes left in the list: hold the pointer over each to read its name."
		caption="Search narrows the list."
	/>

2. Press <Kbd keys="F5" mac="Fn+F5" /> to open the Brush Editor. Click **Size** in the options list. In the list of inputs that appears, check **Fade**, then uncheck **Pressure**. (Krita keeps at least one input checked, so check the new one first.)

3. Click the word **Fade** to show its settings below the list. Right-click the **Length** field, type `45`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. The number sets how long a stroke runs before the brush has shrunk to a point.

4. Above the curve, click the second button, the line falling from top left to bottom right. The curve now starts at 100% and falls to 0%: the brush starts at full size and shrinks to a point by the end of the length.

	<Figure
		src={taperSettings}
		alt="The Brush Editor showing the Size option: Fade checked in the inputs list and Pressure unchecked, Length 45 below the list, and a curve falling in a straight line from 100% at the top left to 0% at the bottom right."
		caption="Size driven by Fade: full size at the start of a stroke, a point at the end of a short stroke."
	/>

5. Click **Save New Brush Preset...** at the top right of the editor. Set the name to `Twig` and click **Save**. Your brush now has its own entry in the Brush Presets docker. Press <Kbd keys="F5" mac="Fn+F5" /> again to close the editor.

	<Figure
		src={savePreset}
		alt="The Save New Brush Preset dialog with the name Twig typed in and a preview of the brush stroke."
		caption="A brush of your own, saved with your Krita settings."
	/>

</Steps>

<Steps>

1. Pick your **Pebble** brush in the Brush Presets docker, set **Size** to `60` and **Opacity** to `100`. Set the dirt ramp's **highlight** swatch and paint about three long, wandering strokes across the image. Pebbles should cover only a small part of the dirt; leave most of it bare.

2. Pick your **Twig** brush, set **Size** to `7`, and set the **dark** swatch. Paint about ten short, slightly curved strokes in different directions, each about a tenth of the image long: twigs and small roots. The brush tapers them to a point.

	<Figure

3. Zoom out with <Kbd keys="Ctrl+-" mac="Cmd+-" /> until you see about three copies of the image across the canvas, and look for a spot that repeats. A single very light or very dark pebble repeats every 2 meters and gives the pattern away; pick **Basic-2 Opacity**, set **Opacity** to `100`, and paint over it with the dirt ramp's **dark** swatch.

## Paint the sand

<Steps>

1. Create a new image named `sand_albedo` the same way, 512 by 512, fill it with the sand ramp's **light** swatch, `42`, `36`, `78`, and turn on wrap-around mode.

2. **Damp patches.** Pick **Basic-2 Opacity**, set **Size** to `110` and **Opacity** to `20`, set the **dark** swatch, `36`, `40`, `65`, and paint about five short, wandering strokes.

	<Figure
		src={damp}
		alt="Golden sand with a few faint, slightly darker patches."
		caption="Damp patches: sand stays soft, so keep them faint."
	/>

3. **Ripples.** Set **Size** to `5` and **Opacity** to `60`. With the **dark** swatch, paint about sixteen long, gently wavy lines across the image from left to right, one every 30 pixels or so from top to bottom, each about half to three quarters of the image wide. Then set the **highlight** swatch and paint a lighter line close above each dark one: the crest of the ripple catching the light.

	<Figure
		src={ripples}
		alt="Sand with rows of thin, gently wavy lines: a darker line with a pale line right above it, repeated down the image."
		caption="A ripple is a shadow line with a lit line above it."
	/>

4. **Stones and shells.** Pick your **Pebble** brush, set **Size** to `40` and **Opacity** to `100`, set the **dark** swatch, and paint four wandering strokes: a few small stones. Then pick **Basic-2 Opacity**, set **Size** to `6`, set the **highlight** swatch, and click about twenty-five single dabs here and there: shell bits.

	<Figure
		src={sandDetails}
		alt="The sand with ripples, a few small stones with shadows, and tiny pale dots."
		caption="Small stones and shell bits."
	/>

5. Zoom out and check the repeat, then save it as `art_source/ground/sand_albedo.kra` and export it as `art/ground/sand_albedo.png`, the same way as the dirt.

	<Figure

		caption="Sand, repeated."
	/>

</Steps>

## Import them into Godot

<Steps>

1. In Godot, click `art/ground/dirt_albedo.png`, open the **Import** tab, set **Compress > Mode** to **VRAM Compressed**, check **Mipmaps > Generate**, and click **Reimport (\*)**, as for the grass detail in lesson 13.

2. Do the same for `art/ground/sand_albedo.png`.

	<Figure
		src={importSand}
		alt="The Import dock for sand_albedo.png: Compress Mode VRAM Compressed and Mipmaps Generate checked, with the Reimport (*) button."
		caption="The same settings for every ground texture."

## Try it

- In the dirt image, try your **Pebble** brush with the grass ramp's colors. The same stamp makes green moss-covered stones.
- Try the **Twig** brush with **Size** `30`. Longer strokes still end in a point, because the size falls to nothing after the Fade length.
- Set the Pebble brush's **Spacing** lower, such as `0.3`, and paint one stroke. The pebbles pile into a gravel path.

## If it does not work

- **Paint went outside the stone.** The stone was not selected when you painted the rim. Undo, select it again with the Polygonal Selection Tool, and paint the rim.
- **The polygon did not close.** Press <Kbd keys="Enter" /> after the last point, or click on the first point again.
- **Pebbles print as gray-brown squares.** Paint Layer 1 or Background was visible when you clicked **Stamp**. Hide them and make the stamp again.
- **Ripples or damp patches print as pebbles.** You are painting with the Pebble brush. Pick **Basic-2 Opacity** in the Brush Presets docker again.
- **Strokes go to the wrong image.** Another image was open. Close it with **File > Close**, or click the right image's tab above the canvas.
