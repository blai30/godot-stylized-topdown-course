## What you will build

A `Grass` node in the meadow with a **Scatter** button. Press it, and the node reads the painted ground and grows a field: about 700 clumps on the grass tiles, thinly on the dirt, none on the sand, and 85 flowers among them. The field is saved in the scene, so the game loads it as it is.

## Why a MultiMesh, and why blocks

The field is 700 clumps of 285 triangles, about 200,000 triangles. A graphics card draws that easily, but it does not draw 700 separate nodes easily: each one is a separate request, and the cost of requests, not triangles, is what a field of small things runs out of first. A **`MultiMesh`** is one mesh with a list of places, and the card draws all of them in one request. That is why lesson 30 made a clump a patch of 95 blades and not a tuft of 16: for the same blades, there are a sixth of the requests.

One `MultiMesh` for the whole field would be drawn whenever any of it showed. So the field is cut into **blocks** 16 meters on a side, and each kind of clump gets one `MultiMeshInstance3D` in each block. Godot skips a block that is off the screen. The meadow is 20 meters across, so there are four blocks, and with three clumps and two flowers, up to twenty `MultiMeshInstance3D` nodes.

How the script works:

- **What it needs.** `Ground` is the `GroundMap`, which knows the tiles. `Models` is the `grass_preview.tscn` of lesson 31: a scene with a `MeshInstance3D` for each model, each with its material. The scatter reads the **mesh** and the **material** from each. Models are found by name: `grass_clump...` and `flower...`.
- **Squares.** `Squares()` walks every 1 meter square that touches a painted cell. The ground's cells are half a meter, so a square covers four.
- **Two clumps and a flower in each square.** Each clump gets its own half of the square, so no stretch of ground is bare: the first is placed in the west half, the second in the east, each a little off-center by a random amount. The flower goes anywhere in the square. A clump is turned by any angle about the vertical and scaled between 0.9 and 1.1.
- **A hash, not a roll.** Each square seeds its own random generator from its coordinates and the `Seed`. So what a square grows depends on that square alone: change the ground next door, or scatter again, and this square grows exactly what it did. Every random number is drawn whether or not it is used, so one decision never shifts the next.
- **Density.** A clump grows if its roll is below the `GrassDensity` of the tile at its position, so grass at `1` grows on every square, dirt at `0.1` on a tenth of them, and sand never. A flower grows if its roll is below `FlowerDensity`.
- **The data in each instance.** `INSTANCE_CUSTOM`, a `Color` with four numbers, is the instance's data for the shader of lesson 31. Its first number is the wind phase. For a flower the other three are its petal color, in linear color, from one of the four `FlowerColors`.
- **Blocks.** Each growth is put in a list for its model and its block, and each list becomes a `MultiMeshInstance3D` named after the model and the block, with the model's mesh and material, and no shadow: a field of shadows is expensive and the ground's own is enough. The nodes are added as children, and `Owner` is set to the scene's root, which is what makes the scene save them.
- **Scattering again.** `Clear()` removes the old nodes first.

## Add it to the meadow

<Steps>

1. Open `meadow.tscn`. Select `GrassPreview`, the node from lesson 31, press <Kbd keys="Delete" mac="Cmd+Backspace" />, and confirm. The `Models` file stays.

2. Select the root, add a **Node3D**, and name it `Grass`. In the Inspector, open the **Script** row's menu, choose **Quick Load...**, and pick `GrassScatter.cs`. The node's own properties appear.

3. Set **Ground** to `GroundTiles`: click **Assign...** and choose it in the list. Set **Models** to `grass_preview.tscn` with **Quick Load...**. Leave **Seed** at `1`.

4. Click **Scatter**. The Output panel prints `Scattered 784 in 20 meshes.`: 699 clumps and 85 flowers. The 3D view fills with grass.

5. Click **Scatter** again. It prints the same line, and nothing changes: the same squares grow the same things. Change **Seed** to `2` and click it once more, and you have a different field. Set it back to `1`, scatter, and save the scene with <Kbd keys="Ctrl+S" mac="Cmd+S" />.

<Aside type="tip" title="The saved nodes are the field">
The scene file is now a few hundred kilobytes larger, because it holds every position. That is the point: the game does not run the scatter. It loads the nodes, and draws them. If you delete the `Grass` node's children, the field is gone until you scatter again.
</Aside>

With the field, the game's own counters read, at the default zoom, 144 draw calls and about 247,000 triangles in a frame, and 120 frames a second. At the closest zoom it runs at about 97. The field is the bulk of the triangles. It is within what a laptop does, and these numbers are why the clump is a big patch and the blocks are not smaller.

<Aside type="note" title="What this lesson leaves out">
A world that streams in, with ground that changes while the game runs, needs more than this. The field would thin as the camera zooms out, since a far blade is smaller than a pixel. It would shrink to nothing at the edge of the view, so nothing pops. It would skip blades whose feet are on a wall or in water. Each of those is a real cost for a big streamed world, and none is needed for a meadow of this size and a camera with this range. Module 9's generator is where the first of them would arrive.
</Aside>

## Try it

- Set `Seed` to a few other numbers, and scatter each time. The ground's data decides where grass grows, and the seed decides how it falls.
- Change the grass tile's **Flower Density** to `0.8` for a flower meadow, or `0` for none. Scatter again.
- Give the dirt tile a **Grass Density** of `0.3`, and see the tufts cross the path. Paint some dirt over a stretch of grass with the **GridMap** and scatter again: the grass leaves it.
- Remove two of the four entries of **Flower Colors**. The flowers use what is left.
- Add a fourth clump to `grass_preview.tscn`, such as a `grass_clump_d` of lesson 30, and scatter. The script finds it by its name.

## If it does not work

- **Nothing grows, and the Output says "GrassScatter needs a Ground and Models".** One of the two properties is empty. If **Ground** will not accept `GroundTiles`, the **GroundMap** script lacks `[Tool]`: add it and build.
- **The Scatter button is not there.** The script has not built, or the node has no script. Click **Build Project** and check that the node's script is `GrassScatter.cs`.
- **The field grows, but a clump stands in the air or in the ground.** The grass models are not at the ground in their scene, or the `Grass` node is not at the origin. Both should be at `0, 0, 0`.
- **Flowers are all white.** The petal color comes from the instance data: check that **Flower Colors** is not empty, and that `flower.gdshader` reads `INSTANCE_CUSTOM.yzw`.
- **Grass grows on the sand.** The sand tile's **Grass Density** is not `0`.
- **An error about a type that is `null` in the editor.** One of the three scripts of the ground has no `[Tool]`.
- **A scatter makes a different field each time.** The generator is seeded from something other than the square, such as the time. Seed it from `SquareSeed`.
