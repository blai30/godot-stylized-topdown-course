## Why grass comes in clumps

A meadow needs a lot of blades, a few hundred to a square meter, and a game cannot afford to treat each one as a separate thing. Godot draws many copies of one mesh cheaply with a `MultiMeshInstance3D`, but it pays a fixed cost for every copy, however small the mesh is. A copy of a 16-blade tuft costs nearly as much as a copy of a 95-blade patch. So the efficient grass is **a few large patches**, and the patches must be big enough to have many blades each and still tile into one carpet when you scatter them with their edges overlapping.

Three decisions fix the clump's look:

- **Blades are tiny, thin, and bent.** A blade is a quad from the foot to a joint, and a triangle from the joint to the tip: five vertices, three triangles. It is 5 to 7 centimeters wide at the foot and 28 to 36 centimeters tall, and it bends, the tip leaning 15 to 50 degrees from upright. A straight blade looks like a spike, and a bent one like grass.
- **Each blade faces away from the center, give or take 40 degrees.** The clump fans out like a shaving brush, and neighboring clumps blend.
- **Each blade carries two numbers for the shader.** They are stored in the vertex colors:

| Channel | Holds | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Red | A random number per blade, its **phase** | Wind, so blades do not sway in step |
| Alpha | The vertex's share of its blade's height, 0 at the foot and 1 at the tip | Color, from dark roots to light tips, and how far the vertex moves in the wind |

The grass is not shaded from a texture. The shader of lesson 31 reads the ground's color under the blade, and the alpha tells it where on the blade the vertex is.

## Three bends

A straight blade looks like a spike. You will make three bent copies, bent 15, 32, and 50 degrees from upright, and the group will pick one at random for each blade. Bending moves two rows of vertices forward, along **Y**, and a little up, so the blade keeps its height of 1.

<Steps>

1. Over the 3D view, press <Kbd keys="Shift+D" />, then <Kbd keys="Esc" />: that makes a copy and drops it where it was. Press <Kbd keys="F2" />, type `blade_mid`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Do it again from the copy for `blade_high`. The outliner now lists `blade_high`, `blade_low`, and `blade_mid`: three straight blades on top of each other.

	<Figure

		caption="Three copies, in place."

2. Click `blade_low` in the outliner, press <Kbd keys="Tab" />, and in the front view box-select the two vertices of the joint row, as before. Press <Kbd keys="G" /> and type the first row of the table, X, Y, and Z with <Kbd keys="Tab" /> between them, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Then box-select the tip, press <Kbd keys="G" />, and type the second row's tip number. Press <Kbd keys="Tab" /> to leave Edit Mode. Do the same for the other two blades:

## The Grass Clump group

You build this group the way you built lesson 26's: in the Geometry Nodes workspace, with the nodes added by <Kbd keys="Shift+A" />, named with <Kbd keys="F2" />, filled in, and linked, in three chapters. Lesson 26's **The group** section explains the three things you do in every chapter, if they are not fresh. There are 24 nodes this time, besides the group's two ends.

<Steps>

1. Click the **Geometry Nodes** tab at the top of the window. With `grass_clump_a` selected, click **New** in the node editor's header. Blender makes a group and a Geometry Nodes modifier. Rename the group `Grass Clump`: double-click its name in the header, or press <Kbd keys="F2" /> over it.

	<Figure
		src={newGroup}
		alt="The Geometry Nodes workspace: a spreadsheet, the 3D view with the filled circle, and below them the node editor with a Group Input joined to a Group Output."
		caption="A new group on the footprint."
	/>

2. Point at the node editor and press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+Space" mac="Cmd+Space" /> to make it fill the window.

</Steps>

The group has one input besides the geometry, a whole number named **Seed**. You will not add it in a menu: when you link the **Group Input**'s empty socket, the small gray circle under **Geometry**, to a node's input, Blender makes a new group input with that input's name and type. The tables say which link does it: **Group Input, Seed** goes to **Blade feet, Seed** in the first chapter, so the group's input is named `Seed`, and changing it re-rolls the clump.

### Chapter 1: Blade feet

The first chapter picks the points the blades grow from, and stores two numbers on every point: its phase and its height. Storing them on the points means they travel with each blade when the blades are made.

<Steps>

1. Add these nodes, name them, and set their values:

	| Node | Settings |
	|---|---|
	| **Blade feet**, from Distribute Points on Faces | method **Random**, Density `150` |
	| **Wind phase**, from Random Value | type **Float**, Min `0`, Max `1`, Seed `1` |
	| **Store phase**, from Store Named Attribute | type **Float**, domain **Point**, Name `phase` |
	| **Blade height**, from Random Value | type **Float**, Min `0.28`, Max `0.36`, Seed `2` |
	| **Store height**, from Store Named Attribute | type **Float**, domain **Point**, Name `height` |

2. Make these links:

	| From | To |
	|---|---|
	| Group Input, Geometry | Blade feet, Mesh |
	| Group Input, Seed | Blade feet, Seed |
	| Blade feet, Points | Store phase, Geometry |
	| Wind phase, Value | Store phase, Value |
	| Store phase, Geometry | Store height, Geometry |
	| Blade height, Value | Store height, Value |
	| Store height, Geometry | Group Output, Geometry |

	<Figure
		src={feetNodes}
		alt="Blender's node editor: a Group Input with Geometry and Seed, joined to a Blade feet node, then two Store nodes in a row, then a Group Output, with two Random Value nodes below feeding the stores."
		caption="Chapter 1: the points, with a phase and a height on each."
	/>

</Steps>

A density of 150 points per square meter over a circle of 0.636 square meters gives about 95 blades. The number varies a little from one seed to the next: **Distribute Points on Faces** rolls for every point.

### Chapter 2: Blades

The second chapter puts a blade on every point: one of the three, at random, turned to face away from the center, scaled to its own width and height. Its own height is the number stored in chapter 1, read back.

<Steps>

1. Add these nodes, name them, and set their values. **Blades** has a **Collection** field: click it and choose `grass_blades` from the list.

	| Node | Settings |
	|---|---|
	| **Blades**, from Collection Info | space **Relative**, Collection **grass_blades**, **Separate Children** checked, **Reset Children** checked |
	| **Pick a blade**, from Random Value | type **Integer**, Min `0`, Max `2`, Seed `3` |
	| **Position**, from Position | nothing |
	| **Outward**, from Vector Math | operation **Normalize** |
	| **Wobble**, from Random Value | type **Vector**, Min `-0.84, -0.84, 0`, Max `0.84, 0.84, 0`, Seed `4` |
	| **Add wobble**, from Vector Math | operation **Add** |
	| **Turn away**, from Align Rotation to Vector | axis **Y**, pivot **Z** |
	| **Blade width**, from Random Value | type **Float**, Min `0.05`, Max `0.07`, Seed `5` |
	| **Read height**, from Named Attribute | type **Float**, Name `height` |
	| **Blade size**, from Combine XYZ | nothing |
	| **Blade on each point**, from Instance on Points | **Pick Instance** checked |
	| **Realize blades**, from Realize Instances | nothing |

2. Make these links. **Blade feet**'s output is already joined to **Group Output**: the link from **Realize blades** replaces it.

	| From | To |
	|---|---|
	| Store height, Geometry | Blade on each point, Points |
	| Blades, Instances | Blade on each point, Instance |
	| Pick a blade, Value | Blade on each point, Instance Index |
	| Position, Position | Outward, Vector |
	| Outward, Vector | Add wobble, Vector |
	| Wobble, Value | Add wobble, Vector (second) |
	| Add wobble, Vector | Turn away, Vector |
	| Turn away, Rotation | Blade on each point, Rotation |
	| Blade width, Value | Blade size, X |
	| Read height, Attribute | Blade size, Y |
	| Read height, Attribute | Blade size, Z |
	| Blade size, Vector | Blade on each point, Scale |
	| Blade on each point, Instances | Realize blades, Geometry |
	| Realize blades, Geometry | Group Output, Geometry |

	<Figure
		src={bladesNodes}
		alt="Blender's node editor: the first chapter at the left, and to its right a Collection Info node, Random Value nodes, vector math nodes, and an Instance on Points node feeding a Realize Instances node and the Group Output."
		caption="Chapter 2: a blade on each point."
	/>

</Steps>

How it works, in the order the data flows:

- **Collection Info** with **Separate Children** hands over the three blades as three instances, and **Reset Children** drops their positions, so each blade starts at its own origin, at the ground.
- **Pick Instance** on **Instance on Points** puts one of them on each point, the one numbered by **Pick a blade**: 0, 1, or 2. This is why the blades are in their own collection.
- **Turn away** turns each blade about the vertical so its bend points the way the **Add wobble** vector does. **Outward** is the point's position from the clump's center, as a direction, so the blade bends away from the center. **Wobble** adds a random vector of up to 0.84 either way, which is a turn of up to about 40 degrees from straight out.
- **Blade size** is the blade's scale: the width on X, the height on Y and Z. Because the blades are one meter wide and tall, the number is the size in meters.

### Chapter 3: Colors

The last chapter writes the vertex colors. The phase becomes red. The alpha is each vertex's height above the ground, divided by its blade's height: 0 at the foot, 1 at the tip. Green and blue stay 0.

<Steps>

1. Add these nodes, name them, and set their values:

	| Node | Settings |
	|---|---|
	| **Read phase**, from Named Attribute | type **Float**, Name `phase` |
	| **Blade position**, from Position | nothing |
	| **Height above ground**, from Separate XYZ | nothing |
	| **Read height again**, from Named Attribute | type **Float**, Name `height` |
	| **Share of height**, from Math | operation **Divide**, Clamp **on** |
	| **Pack colors**, from Combine Color | mode **RGB**, Green `0`, Blue `0` |
	| **Store colors**, from Store Named Attribute | type **Color**, domain **Point**, Name `Col` |

2. Make these links:

	| From | To |
	|---|---|
	| Blade position, Position | Height above ground, Vector |
	| Height above ground, Z | Share of height, Value |
	| Read height again, Attribute | Share of height, Value (second) |
	| Read phase, Attribute | Pack colors, Red |
	| Share of height, Value | Pack colors, Alpha |
	| Realize blades, Geometry | Store colors, Geometry |
	| Pack colors, Color | Store colors, Value |
	| Store colors, Geometry | Group Output, Geometry |

	<Figure
		src={colorsNodes}
		alt="Blender's node editor: a Realize blades node at the left joined to a Store colors node, with a Read phase, a Pack colors, and a small chain of Position, Separate XYZ, Divide, and Read height again nodes feeding it, and a Group Output at the right."
		caption="Chapter 3: the vertex colors."
	/>

</Steps>

The **Divide** works because each blade is a straight scale of a blade whose tip is exactly 1 meter high: a vertex's height above the ground, over its blade's height, is its share of the way to the tip. **Clamp** keeps it between 0 and 1.

## The flowers

A flower is a few separate parts, each a simple shape you make by hand, then joined: a **stem**, a **center**, and **petals**. Every part gets its color from a short group of its own, so by the time you join them the colors are in the mesh. The petals' mask goes in alpha, as the blade's share of its height does for grass: 1 for a petal, 0 for the rest. The shader will use it to let the petals flutter and to tint them from a list of colors.

First hide the clumps, so the origin is free: over the 3D view press <Kbd keys="A" />, then <Kbd keys="H" />, and <Kbd keys="Alt+A" /> to deselect.

### The stem and the center

Both are the same shape: a square, pulled up, with its top drawn together to a point.

<Steps>

1. Press <Kbd keys="Shift+A" />, type `plane`, press <Kbd keys="Enter" />, and press <Kbd keys="F2" /> to name it `stem`. Press <Kbd keys="Tab" />, <Kbd keys="A" />, and <Kbd keys="S" />, type `0.012`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />: a square 2.4 centimeters across. ### Painting a part

The colors come from a group named **Paint Part** with four inputs: **Red**, **Green**, **Blue**, and **Alpha**. It packs them into a color and stores it as `Col`, so it is two nodes. You build it on the stem.

<Steps>

1. Click `stem`, click the **Geometry Nodes** tab, and click **New**. Rename the group `Paint Part`, and make the node editor fill the window.

2. Add these nodes, name them, and set their values:

	| Node | Settings |
	|---|---|
	| **Pack color**, from Combine Color | mode **RGB** |
	| **Store color**, from Store Named Attribute | type **Color**, domain **Point**, Name `Col` |

3. Make these links. Each of the four links from **Group Input** starts at its empty socket, and makes a new input named for the socket it lands on:

	| From | To |
	|---|---|
	| Group Input, Red | Pack color, Red |
	| Group Input, Green | Pack color, Green |
	| Group Input, Blue | Pack color, Blue |
	| Group Input, Alpha | Pack color, Alpha |
	| Group Input, Geometry | Store color, Geometry |
	| Pack color, Color | Store color, Value |
	| Store color, Geometry | Group Output, Geometry |

	<Figure
		src={paintNodes}
		alt="Blender's node editor: a Group Input with Geometry, Red, Green, Blue, and Alpha, a Pack color node, a Store color node, and a Group Output."
		caption="Paint Part: four numbers in, one vertex color out."
	/>

4. Click the shield beside the group's name in the node editor's header. That is **Fake User**: Blender throws away a group that nothing uses when the file is saved, and after you join the flower parts nothing will. The shield keeps it, and lesson 34 will use it again.

	<Figure
		src={fakeUser}
		alt="The node editor's header: the group's name, Paint Part, followed by a shield button, the Fake User button."
		caption="The shield keeps the group in the file."
	/>

5. Go back to the Layout workspace and the modifier tab. The modifier has four fields. For the stem, a dark green, type `0.05`, `0.14`, `0.02`, and `0`: click each field, press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" />, type, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />.

</Steps>

The numbers are linear color values, which is what the shader reads, so a dark green looks like `0.05, 0.14, 0.02` and not like a hex code.

## Import into Godot

<Steps>

1. Switch to Godot. It imports `grass.glb` when it sees the new file. Click `art/nature/grass/grass.glb` in the FileSystem dock and open the **Import** tab.

2. Under **Meshes**, uncheck **Generate LODs**, and click **Reimport**. Godot makes simpler copies of a mesh for when it is far away. A clump is already a few triangles per blade, and the scatter will draw hundreds of them together, so the importer's rule for choosing a level, which depends on the distance to the camera, would swap blades out of a patch as you zoom.

## Try it

- Change **Density** in **Blade feet** from `150` to `100` for a thinner clump, or `200` for a denser one.
- Change **Min** and **Max** in **Blade height** to `0.15` and `0.2` for a short lawn.
- Make a fourth bend: duplicate `blade_high` and move its tip by `0.62` in Y, and change **Max** in **Pick a blade** from `2` to `3`. Blades that nearly lie down read as windswept.
- Give the cup more petals: add a sixth and turn the copies by 60 degrees.

## If it does not work

- **The clump is empty.** **Blades** has no **Collection**, or `grass_blades` is empty. Choose `grass_blades` in its field.
- **There are far fewer than 95 blades, or only one kind.** **Pick Instance** is unchecked, so only the first blade is used, or **Separate Children** is unchecked.
- **All the blades lean the same way.** **Outward** is not linked to **Add wobble**, or **Turn away**'s axis is **X**.
- **The blades lie flat on the ground.** A blade is flat if **Reset Children** is off, or the **Blade size** values are not linked to **Y** and **Z**.
- **The tip of a blade is at the origin.** You chose **At Cursor** and not **At Center** when merging. Undo and merge again.
- **The colors are white in Godot.** The meshes need the `Col` attribute, and the exporter must write the active color. Check that **Store colors** names it exactly `Col`.
- **A petal is dark in Blender's Attribute view.** Its **Paint Part** values are not `1, 1, 1, 1`. The modifier is applied when you convert, so check the values before that step.
