## What you will build

Three small things that finish the ground. **Pebbles** lie on the path and sparsely in the grass, and **mushrooms** stand here and there in it, both scattered by the same script as the grass. And a few **dirt patches**, soft brown ovals of bare ground, lie in the meadow with the grass cleared from under them. By the end the meadow is no longer a lawn.

<Figure
	src={mid}
	alt="The meadow at noon from above: grass with scattered small flowers, three soft brown dirt patches in the grass with no blades in them, a dirt path, and a few pebbles on the path."
	caption="The meadow with its dirt patches."
/>

Two of the three come from the pipeline you already have. A pebble and a mushroom are meshes in `grass.glb`, colored with vertex colors by the **Paint Part** group of lesson 30, and `GrassScatter` places them. The patches are the new idea: a **`Decal`**, a box that paints a texture onto whatever surfaces are inside it, which needs no mesh and no change to the tiles.

## Rounded boxes

A pebble and a mushroom cap are both a box with its corners rounded off. You make one the way lesson 24 rounded a trunk: a cube, scaled, with **Subdivision Surface** applied, which turns a box into a smooth, soft shape of 24 faces. Every part starts the same way.

Open `art_source/nature/grass/grass.blend` from lesson 30 in Blender. It still has the clumps and the flowers, and the **Paint Part** group, which you made in lesson 30 and kept with **Fake User**, the shield beside its name. Click the `grass` collection in the outliner, so new objects go in it.

<Steps>

1. Over the 3D view press <Kbd keys="Shift+A" />, type `cube`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Press <Kbd keys="F2" /> and name it `pebble_a`.

2. Press <Kbd keys="S" /> and type `0.09`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0.07`, <Kbd keys="Tab" />, `0.045`, then <Kbd keys="Enter" />: the cube is now a flat box 18 centimeters wide, 14 deep, and 9 high (rounding will shrink it to about 15, 12, and 7.5). Press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" /> and choose **Scale**, so that size becomes the mesh's own.

3. Raise the mesh, not the object, so the object's origin stays on the ground: press <Kbd keys="Tab" /> for Edit Mode, <Kbd keys="A" />, <Kbd keys="G" />, <Kbd keys="Z" />, `0.0375`, <Kbd keys="Enter" />, and <Kbd keys="Tab" /> to leave. The pebble now rests with its bottom at the ground. Then press <Kbd keys="G" />, <Kbd keys="X" />, `-0.5`, <Kbd keys="Enter" /> to move the object aside while you work. You will clear that move later, and the mesh will not change.

4. Click the **Modifiers** tab, **Add Modifier**, type `subdivision`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Move over the modifier's panel and press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" /> to apply it.

	<Figure

## Scatter them

The scatter finds the models by name, as it did the clumps. It now handles each kind of prop the same way, so there is nothing to add for a new one except a density on the ground.

<Steps>

2. Replace `GrassScatter.cs` with the version in the solutions below. What changed:

	- A table, `Props`, lists the kinds that grow one to a square: each has the start of its models' names and a function that reads its density from a tile. Flowers, pebbles, and mushrooms are three rows. A kind with no model in the scene is skipped.

## Dirt patches

A patch is a decal. A **`Decal`** node is a box, and inside the box it paints its **Albedo** texture onto every surface it touches, as a sticker projected from above. No mesh and no tile has to change, and a decal can be moved, turned, and stretched like any node.

For the texture, the patch needs a shape whose edge fades out to nothing, so it blends into the ground. A **`GradientTexture2D`** does that: a gradient filled outward from the middle, brown in the middle and transparent at the rim.

<Steps>

1. In the meadow, select the root, add a **Node3D**, and name it `Patches`. Select it, add a **Decal** under it, and name it `Patch1`.

2. In the Inspector set the decal's **Position** to `1.9, 0, -0.7`, its **Rotation** to `0, 20, 0`, and its **Size** to `2.4, 0.6, 1.6`. The decal is a box 2.4 meters wide, 0.6 high, and 1.6 deep, with its middle on the ground: it reaches 30 centimeters above and below.

3. Under **Textures**, set **Albedo** to a **New GradientTexture2D**, and click it to open it. Set **Fill** to **Radial**, **Width** and **Height** to `256`, **Fill From** to `0.5, 0.5` and **Fill To** to `1, 0.5`: the gradient runs from the middle of the texture to the middle of its right edge, so the rim is where the gradient ends. Set **Gradient** to a **New Gradient**, and give it three colors: `7A5638` at offset `0`, `8A6342` at `0.55`, and `8A6342` with an alpha of `0` at `1`. In the gradient's editor, click a color marker, set its offset, and click the color to change it.

The patches are on the ground, but the grass still stands in them: a decal paints the surface under the blades, and the blades are above it. The grass must not grow there. That is the `Patches` property of `GrassScatter`: every `Decal` that is a child of that node clears the grass and the props from the ground inside its box. A point is inside when, in the decal's own frame, it falls within the ellipse the box makes on the ground, so a turned or stretched patch clears what it covers and nothing else.

<Steps>

1. Select the `Grass` node, click **Assign...** beside **Patches**, and choose `Patches`.

## Try it

- Move a patch with <Kbd keys="G" /> in the 3D view, and click **Scatter**: the grass leaves where the patch arrives, and returns where it left.
- Stretch a patch to `5, 0.6, 1.2` and turn it, for a muddy track.
- Change **Albedo Mix** on a decal to `0.7`, so a patch is more transparent and lets the grass texture through.
- Set the sand tile's **Mushroom Density** to `0.3` and scatter: mushrooms on sand. Then set it back.
- Add a second mushroom to the file, a taller one, named `mushroom_b`, from a stem and a cap, and scatter. The script finds it by its name, and each mushroom is a random one of the two.

## If it does not work

- **No pebbles or mushrooms grow.** `grass_preview.tscn` has no node starting with `pebble` or `mushroom`, or the densities are all `0`. Open the scene and check the names.
- **The props are white or gray in the game.** `flower.tres` is not on them. Set **Material Override** on all three.
- **A mushroom stands in the ground up to its cap.** The part was moved up in Object Mode, so its origin is above the ground. Lift the mesh in Edit Mode, as in step 3 of Rounded boxes, and set the object's location back to 0.
- **The patches are not visible.** The decal's box does not reach the ground. Its **Position** `y` is `0` and its **Size** `y` is `0.6`, so the box spans the ground. Also check that **Albedo** is set.
- **A patch is a hard-edged brown square.** The gradient's last color is not transparent, or **Fill To** is `1, 1`.
- **Grass still grows in the patches.** `Patches` is empty on the `Grass` node, or the decals are not children of that node.
- **A patch paints the grass blades brown.** Blades inside a decal's box are tinted by it. Check that **Patches** is assigned on the scatter node and scatter again, so the grass leaves the box.
