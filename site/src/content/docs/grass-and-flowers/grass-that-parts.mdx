## What you will build

The grass now stands still around the player: the capsule walks through blades without moving one. After this lesson the blades near the player's feet lean away from it and sink, and spring back as it walks on. The effect is small, 25 centimeters of lean at most, and it is what makes the player stand in the grass and not on it.

The shaders cannot see the player. So the player's position goes into a **shader global**, as the wind did in lesson 27: a small script on the player writes it every frame, and the grass and flower shaders read it.

The four numbers are the player's position, X, Y, and Z, and how far its push reaches, W, in meters. A W of 0 means no push, and it is the value when the player is not in the game.

## The shared include

Grass and flowers both need to push a vertex away from the point, so the function lives in an include that both shaders use, as the wind does.

<Steps>

1. In the Shader Editor, choose **File > New Shader Include...**, set **Path** to `res://art/shaders/ground_cover.gdshaderinc`, and click **Create**. Type the include from the solution below the steps. Its pieces:

	- `global uniform vec4 push_point;`, with a comment saying what its four numbers are;
	- two constants: `PUSH_DISTANCE` `0.25`, how far a vertex leans at the most, and `PUSH_FLATTEN` `0.5`, how much of its height it can sink;
	- a function `vec3 push_aside(vec3 vertex, vec3 ground, float mask)`. `ground` is the spot on the ground the vertex is rooted at, and `mask` says how much of the part moves.

How `push_aside` works:

- **How close.** `away` is the vector from the player to the part's spot on the ground, and `push` is `1 - distance / radius`, clamped from 0 to 1: 1 at the player's feet, falling to 0 at the edge of the radius, and 0 beyond. A radius of 0 gives 0.
- **Lean.** The vertex moves along `away`, normalized, by `PUSH_DISTANCE * mask * push`: up to 25 centimeters, away from the player, and only as far as the mask lets it.
- **Sink.** Its height above the ground is scaled by `1 - PUSH_FLATTEN * mask * push`, so at the feet a blade's tip comes down to half its height. This is what reads as the grass being stepped on.
- **The mask.** For a blade it is the share of its height, so the foot, with a mask of 0, never moves and the tip moves most. For a flower it is the petal mask: the petals lean and the stem stays.

## Call it from the shaders

<Steps>

1. Open `grass.gdshader`. Under the includes add `#include "res://art/shaders/ground_cover.gdshaderinc"`. In `vertex()`, after the line that adds `grass_sway`, add a comment and a line that pushes the vertex: `VERTEX = push_aside(VERTEX, vec3(VERTEX.x, clump_origin.y, VERTEX.z), COLOR.a);`

## The script on the player

<Steps>

## Several characters

The template has one push point, because it has one character. A game with enemies or animals that should part the grass has two ways to go. Send a `mat4` to the shader, with one character in each of its four columns, and loop over them in `push_aside`, applying each in turn: it is the same function in a `for` loop. Or keep a short list in a texture. Both are more code than this lesson, and neither changes how the push works.

## Try it

- Change `Radius` on the `PushPoint` to `1.2` for a player that clears a wide path, or `0.3` for a small animal.
- Change `PUSH_DISTANCE` to `0.5`, and the blades throw far to the side. Change `PUSH_FLATTEN` to `0.9` and the grass is trampled flat under the feet.
- Add a second `PushPoint` node on a rock. It will fight the player's for the one global: only the last to write wins. That is why the shader needs a list of points before it can push for more than one character.

## If it does not work

- **The grass does not move at all.** The global is not named `push_point`, or it is not a `vec4`. Check **Project Settings > Globals > Shader Globals**. The name is also in `PushPoint.cs`.
- **The grass leans away from the wrong place.** A second `PushPoint` is writing the same global. Keep only one.
- **The whole clump jumps away.** The push uses the clump's root and not the vertex's own spot. The call in `grass.gdshader` must pass `VERTEX.x` and `VERTEX.z`.
- **The blades are pushed into the ground and the flowers float.** `ground.y` is wrong: the grass uses `clump_origin.y` and the flower `MODEL_MATRIX[3].y`.
- **The grass parts around the origin when the player is gone.** The global is `0, 0, 0, 0` and the shader treats a radius of 0 as a push. `push_aside` must give a `push` of 0 when `push_point.w` is 0.
- **A shader error complains about `push_aside`.** The function is unknown because the include is not in the shader: add the `#include` line.
