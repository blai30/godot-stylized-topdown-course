## Reuse, then vary

A game world needs many rocks, but you do not need many rock models. Two ideas carry you far:

- **Reuse.** A new model can start as a copy of a finished one, keeping everything you set up: the material, the modifiers, the exporter. You only change what makes it different.
- **Variation.** Every copy you place can be turned, scaled, and even squashed a little. From the game camera, a rock turned 120 degrees and made 20% flatter looks like a different rock.

What makes a rock read as a different rock from above is its **silhouette**, its outline as a flat shape. The top-down camera sees mostly the outline and the top, so you check the outline first.

## Start from the small rock

<Steps>

1. Open `rock_small.blend` in Blender.

2. Choose **File > Save As** (<Kbd keys="Ctrl+Shift+S" mac="Cmd+Shift+S" />). Blender asks **Create new directory?**; click **Create**.

3. Click the file name field at the bottom, press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" />, type `rock_large.blend`, press <Kbd keys="Enter" />, and click **Save As**. You are now working in the new file; `rock_small.blend` stays as it was.

4. Rename everything after the new model, as in lessons 9 and 10:

	- the object: with the pointer over the 3D view, press <Kbd keys="F2" />, press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" /> to select the old name, type `rock_large`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />;
	- the mesh: click the name field at the top of the **Data** tab (the green triangle), press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" />, type `rock_large`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />;
	- the collection: click it in the Outliner, press <Kbd keys="F2" /> over the Outliner, press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" />, type `rock_large`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />.

## Check the silhouette from above

<Steps>

1. At the top right of the 3D view, open the **Viewport Shading** panel (the small arrow right of the four round shading buttons). Under **Lighting**, click **Flat**. The panel shortens; under **Color**, in the **Object** row, click **Custom**. Press <Kbd keys="Esc" />.

	<Figure
		src={shadingFlat}
		alt="The Viewport Shading panel with Lighting Flat and the Object color set to Custom, a light gray color field below."
		caption="Flat lighting and one color: no shading, only the outline."
	/>

2. Press <Kbd keys="Numpad 7" /> to look from the top and <Kbd keys="Numpad ." /> to frame the rock. You see a flat light shape: the rock's silhouette.

	<Figure
		src={silhouetteBefore}
		alt="The rock from the top in flat shading: a light gray, nearly rectangular shape with rounded corners."
		caption="Before: the outline from lesson 9, stretched to the new size. Its corners are still close to a box's."
	/>

3. Make the outline irregular. Press <Kbd keys="Tab" /> for Edit Mode, <Kbd keys="1" /> for vertices, and <Kbd keys="Alt+Z" /> for X-ray. From the top, each corner of the outline is two vertices stacked on top of each other, one on the base and one around the middle. For three or four corners and the middle of one long side: press <Kbd keys="Alt+A" />, press <Kbd keys="B" /> and drag a small box around the stack, then press <Kbd keys="G" />, move the mouse to push it out or pull it in, and click. Be bold: push some by 5 to 10 cm.

	<Figure
		src={reshaped}
		alt="The rock from the top in Edit Mode with X-ray: the outline now has uneven corners and one side pushed out."
		caption="The outline after moving four stacks of vertices."
	/>

4. Select everything with <Kbd keys="A" /> and choose **Mesh > Transform > Randomize**. Set **Amount** to `0.04` and pick a **Random Seed** different from the one you used in lesson 9, so the two rocks get different bumps.

5. Randomize moved the base off the ground. Look from the front (<Kbd keys="Numpad 1" />), press <Kbd keys="G" /> then <Kbd keys="Z" />, and move the rock until its lowest point touches the red line, as in lesson 9. Press <Kbd keys="Alt+Z" /> to turn X-ray off and <Kbd keys="Tab" /> for Object Mode.

6. Look from the top again (<Kbd keys="Numpad 7" />). The silhouette should look like a stone, not a box.

	<Figure
		src={silhouetteAfter}
		alt="The rock's silhouette from the top after reshaping: an irregular shape with many uneven sides."
		caption="After: an outline no box would have."
	/>

7. Put the shading back: in the **Viewport Shading** panel, click **Studio** under **Lighting** and **Texture** in the **Object** row.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip" title="Squint at it">
A silhouette check works best with a quick glance, or with your eyes half closed. If you can tell your rocks apart that way, players will too. The same check works for trees and characters later in the course.
</Aside>

## A second ramp in one move

Your rock still reads the stone ramp's swatches. Your second stone ramp sits exactly four swatches to the right, half the image across, so you can move every UV at once.

<Steps>

1. Click the **UV Editing** tab. Over the 3D view on the right, press <Kbd keys="3" /> for faces and <Kbd keys="A" /> to select them all.

2. Move the pointer over the UV Editor and press <Kbd keys="A" /> to select all the UVs there. Press <Kbd keys="G" />, then <Kbd keys="X" />, type `0.5`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Every UV moves half the image to the right: the shadow, dark, and light swatches of the first stone ramp become those of the second.

	<Figure
		src={uvMoved}
		alt="The UV Editor with the palette: the UV points sit on the second stone ramp in the top row's right half, and the Sidebar shows UV Vertex X 0.688, Y 0.938."
		caption="All UVs moved 0.5 to the right, onto the second ramp of the row."
	/>

3. Click the **Layout** tab, press <Kbd keys="Tab" /> if the header says **Edit Mode**, and orbit around your large rock.

	<Figure
		src={colored}
		alt="The large rock in Blender in blue-gray colors: a lighter top and darker sides."
		caption="The large rock in the reference palette's second ramp, a cool stone."
	/>

</Steps>

<Aside type="note" title="Why a different ramp">
Two kinds of stone side by side give a world variety for free: a warmer one and a cooler one tell the eye there is more than one rock type, even before their shapes differ. Because your palette lays ramps out in the same pattern, any ramp is one move away.
</Aside>

## Export the large rock

The exporter came along with the file, and so did its **File Path**: it still points to `rock_small.glb`. Exporting now would overwrite the small rock, so change the path first.

<Steps>

1. On the **Collection** tab, click the folder button of the exporter's **File Path**.

2. Click the path field at the top, press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" />, type the path of your project's `art\nature\rock_large\` folder, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Click **Create**.

3. Click the file name field, press <Kbd keys="Ctrl+A" mac="Cmd+A" />, type `rock_large.glb`, press <Kbd keys="Enter" />, and click **Accept**.

	<Figure

## Vary the copies

Place the three large rocks among the small ones, and give each a different turn and scale. Scaling one axis less than the others **squashes** a copy into a new shape: a large rock scaled to 70% height reads as a low, flat slab.

Select each rock in the Scene dock and set its **Transform** in the Inspector, as in lesson 11:

| Rock | Position | Rotation Y | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| RockLarge | -5, 0, 0 | 20 | 1, 1, 1 |
| RockLarge2 | 3.2, 0, -4.8 | -110 | 1.1, 0.7, 1.1 |
| RockLarge3 | -3.6, 0, 3.4 | -160 | 0.9, 0.8, 0.9 |

If typing one **Scale** value changes all three, the values are linked: click the chain icon at the right of **Scale** in the Inspector to unlink them, then type each one.

<Figure
	src={rockInspector}
	alt="The Inspector for RockLarge2: Position 3.2, 0, -4.8, Rotation 0, -110, 0, and Scale 1.1, 0.7, 1.1."
	caption="The squashed copy: 70% of its height, 110% of its width."
/>

<Aside type="note" title="Rules for variation">
- **Turn freely** around Y; every angle shows a new outline from above.
- **Scale between about 0.7 and 1.3.** Further, and the bevels and the collision look wrong for the size.
- **Squash, do not stretch, the height.** A flatter rock looks natural; a taller one starts to look like a pillar.
- **Group them.** Rocks look natural in clusters of two or three, a large one with small ones at its foot, not spread evenly.
- **Keep Position Y at 0**, and leave Rotation X and Z at 0, so the base stays on the ground.
</Aside>

## If it does not work

- **The small rock in Godot changed into the large one.** The exporter still pointed to `rock_small.glb` when you exported. Open `rock_small.blend` and export it again with its own exporter, then fix the large rock's **File Path** and export again.
- **Ctrl+A > Scale did nothing.** The rock was not selected after you clicked the collection. Click the rock and apply again.
- **The bevels look sharper than the small rock's.** The **Amount** is still 4 cm; set it to `0.08`.
- **The large rock has the same colors as the small one.** The UVs did not move: in the UV Editor, select all with <Kbd keys="A" /> before <Kbd keys="G" />, <Kbd keys="X" />, `0.5`.
- **Part of the rock turned white or green.** Only some UVs moved, or they moved by the wrong amount. Undo and move all of them by exactly `0.5` along X.
- **A new material file appeared next to rock_large.glb.** **Extract Path** was not set; delete that file, set the path to `art/palette`, and reimport.
- **The silhouette view stays shaded.** **Lighting** must be **Flat**; with **Studio**, the shape still has light and dark sides.
