## What you will build

Two changes that make the generator something you can live with.

The first is that **it leaves your work alone**. At the moment Generate wipes the ground and paints all of it. Paint a lake by hand, press the button, and the lake is gone. The generator will learn which cells it painted, and take away and paint again only those.

The second is that **the game can generate the level itself**, when it starts, from the saved seed or from a new one each run.

## Whose cells are these?

Open the ground GridMap and it is a grid of numbers: this cell is grass, that one is water. Nothing in a cell says who painted it. So for the generator to leave your work alone it has to remember its own, and the simplest way is a list. When it paints, it writes down every cell it painted, and the item it painted there. The next time, it goes through the list. A cell that still holds what the generator painted is its own, and is taken away to be painted again. **A cell that holds something else was painted over by you**, and it is not touched. Trees are not grown on it either.

That makes three kinds of cell:

| Cell | In the list, holding what was painted? | The next Generate |
|---|---|---|
| Painted by the generator | Yes | Taken away and painted again, perhaps as something else |
| Painted over by you | In the list, but holds something else | Left as it is, and not on the new list |
| Empty, or painted before the list | No | Painted if empty and inside the level, left alone if painted |

It works because you paint something the generator did not. Water over grass, or dirt over grass, shows. Grass over grass does not: the cell holds what the generator painted, and the next Generate takes it away like any other.

It also tells you what to expect the first time. The generated level you made in lesson 50 was painted by a generator that kept no list, and its cells are in no list. To the new generator, all of that ground is yours, and Generate would paint nothing. A **Clear** button starts again: it empties the ground, the shore, the grass, the trees, and the list.

It calls `Ground?.Clear()`, `Bank?.Clear()`, `Grass?.Clear()`, `ClearProps()`, and `Painted.Clear()`;
- `_Ready` returns at once `if (Engine.IsEditorHint() || !GenerateOnReady)`: the editor has a button for this, and a game that did not ask for it generates nothing. Otherwise, `if (Profile is not null && NewSeedEachRun)` it sets `Profile.Seed = (int)GD.Randi();`, with a comment: the new seed is only in memory, the profile's file on disk is not changed. Then it calls `Generate()`. A script named `_Ready` runs when the node enters the scene, after its siblings above it in the tree have, which is why `WorldGenerator` is the last node and the ground is ready by then;
- `Generate` is where the idea lives. After the checks, `var started = Time.GetTicksMsec();`. Then, for each `painted` in `Painted`, `cell` is `new Vector3I(painted.X, painted.Y, painted.Z)`, and `if (Ground.GetCellItem(cell) == painted.W)` the cell still holds what was painted there, and it is taken away: `Ground.SetCellItem(cell, (int)GridMap.InvalidCellItem);`. `byHand` is what is left, `Ground.GetUsedCells().Select(cell => new Vector2I(cell.X, cell.Z)).ToHashSet()`, with a comment: what is left on the ground was painted by hand;
- the loop over the level's cells has two outcomes. A cell in `byHand` goes into `cells` as the tile that is already there, `Ground.Tiles!.Find(Ground.GetCellItem(new Vector3I(x, 0, z))) ?? land`, and the loop `continue`s. Any other cell goes into `cells` as water or land as before, and into a new `HashSet<Vector2I> generated`. `cells` holds every cell, so the narrow-water rule sees your water too, and `generated` holds only the ones that are the generator's to change;
- the narrow-water loop passes `generated` to `NarrowWater`, which returns only the narrow cells in it: `narrow.Where(generated.Contains).ToList()`, with a comment: the cells among the generated ones that lie in water too narrow for the bank. Water you painted narrower than three cells is not turned to land, and Update shore warns about it, as it did in lesson 47. It is your water;
- the ground is written from `generated` only, and the list is made as it goes: `Painted.Clear();`, then for each cell `Ground.SetCellItem(new Vector3I(cell.X, 0, cell.Y), cells[cell].Item);` and `Painted.Add(new Vector4I(cell.X, 0, cell.Y, cells[cell].Item));`. `Ground.Clear()` is gone: the cells to take away were taken away at the top;
- `PlaceProps(half, byHand)` takes the hand-painted cells, and a candidate is skipped if `byHand.Contains(cell)`, with the other tests. That is how trees stay out of a clearing you painted. `IsNearWater` asks the ground, so trees also keep from a lake you painted;
- the loop at the top of `PlaceProps` that emptied `Props` moves into `ClearProps()`, so that `Clear` can use it too, and `PlaceProps` begins with `ClearProps();` and `var parent = Props!;`, and adds each tree to `parent`;
- the final `GD.Print` reports `generated.Count` cells, the seed, and the time: `from seed {Profile.Seed}, in {Time.GetTicksMsec() - started} ms`.

## Generate when the game starts

So far the level was generated in the editor, and saved with the scene: the 9,216 ground cells, the shore, the grass, and the 73 trees are in `generated.tscn`, which is about a megabyte. A game that runs the scene loads them. Often that is what you want. But the same code can run when the game starts.

<Steps>

1. Select **WorldGenerator**, check **Generate On Ready**, save the scene, and run it with **Run Current Scene**, <Kbd keys="F6" mac="Cmd+R" />. The game generates the level as it starts, from the profile's seed, 15. It is the same level: the cells and the trees are identical to the saved ones.

The `GD.Randi()` seed can be negative, and that is fine: the profile's `Sample` takes the seed's remainder with `PosMod`, which is never negative, so a seed of any sign slides the noise to a point.

When you like a level, the seed in the game's Output panel brings it back: type it into the profile's **Seed**, uncheck **New Seed Each Run**, and the same level comes every time. A game that lets a player share a world shares that number.

## Bake it, or generate it live?

You now have both, and the choice is a real one. Neither is better in general.

**Bake the level** (generate in the editor, keep it in the scene) when:

- you want to look at it, fix it, and keep your changes. A baked level is a level like any other: you can move a tree, paint a path, and nothing changes it. With the list, you can even regenerate and keep the touches;
- the game should start fast. Loading a scene is cheaper than making it. On this machine the generator took 70 to 120 milliseconds in the editor to paint the ground, edge the shore, scatter the grass, and grow the trees for a level of 48 meters. That is a short pause, and a bigger level has a longer one;
- every player should see the same level, and you should be able to check it.

**Generate it live** (when the game starts) when:

- a new level for every run is the game, or a seed is something a player can type or share;
- you do not want the level in version control. The generated scene is a megabyte of text that changes whenever you press Generate. The profile is about a kilobyte, and describes the same level;
- a player's own world must be made on the spot.

The generator is the same code in both cases, and there is nothing to choose between them in what it can do: the **Generate On Ready** box is the only switch.

<Aside type="note">
	A bigger world, 500 meters or a whole map, would want more than this: generating a piece at a time as the player nears it, and doing the work off the main thread so the game does not stutter. Neither is built here. The same ideas hold. A chunk is a part of the level with its own cells, its own list, and its own seed made from the world's seed and its place, as each square meter of trees already is.
</Aside>

## Try it

- With **Generate On Ready** and **New Seed Each Run** both on, run the game a few times and watch the Output panel. Which seeds give the most lakes? Write one down, set it in the profile, and turn **New Seed Each Run** off.
- Paint a one-cell channel of water by hand over grass and press Generate. What does Update shore say? The generator leaves your water alone, narrow or not.
- Paint a path of **Sand** across the level, over the generated grass, and press Generate with another seed. The path stays, and the lakes and trees move around it. What happens where a path crosses a lake the new seed makes?
- Paint **Grass** over a generated lake and press Generate. Then think about why it is gone: the cell is yours, but it holds grass, and the generator cannot tell it from its own.

## When it goes wrong

- **Generate paints nothing, and the Output panel says 0 cells.** The ground holds cells that are in no list, usually from a level generated before the generator kept one. Press **Clear** and then **Generate**.
- **My painted cells were wiped.** You pressed **Clear**, which wipes everything. Or you painted the item the generator had painted, grass over grass, which the list cannot tell from the generator's own. Paint something it did not, such as water, dirt, or sand.
- **A cell I painted keeps coming back as the generator's tile.** It holds the same item as the generator painted there. Pick another tile.
- **The game starts with an empty level.** **Generate On Ready** is checked, but one of the generator's exports is empty, and `Generate` returned with a warning. Look at the Output panel.
- **The game shows the same level every run.** **New Seed Each Run** is not checked.
- **The editor level changes after running the game.** It should not: the new seed lives in the game, in memory. If the profile's **Seed** in the Inspector has changed, `Generate` was run in the editor.
- **The player starts in a lake.** The player is not in the clearing at the middle of the level, or you painted water over the middle by hand.
