## What you will build

A **Generate** button. Press it and a 48 by 48 meter level appears: a meadow with four lakes, banked and grassed, from one seed. Press it again and nothing changes. Change the seed, press it, and the lakes are somewhere else.

<Figure

/>

It does nothing that you have not done by hand. It paints Grass and Water tiles into the same ground GridMap you painted in lesson 16, then presses **Update shore** (lesson 47) and **Scatter** (lesson 32). The new part is the decision, made for every cell: water or land?

## From a number to a tile

Lesson 49 gave you a profile that can answer, for any point on the ground, a number from -1 to 1. A lake is where that number is low, so the rule is one comparison: **below the water level, a cell is water, and everywhere else it is grass.** The profile gets the level, `-0.2`, which in the pictures of the last lesson is the dark gray, a little below the middle.

That rule alone draws lakes, but not ones the rest of the template can live with. Three more rules fix what it gets wrong:

- **Water on the edge.** A lake that reaches the edge of the level would have a bank with nothing beyond it. Water within four cells of the edge becomes land.
- **Water where the player starts.** The player begins at the middle of the level, and must not begin in a lake. Water within six cells of the middle becomes land.
- **Water too narrow for the bank.** Lesson 47 found that water narrower than three cells shows no water, and warns. A generator that made such water would fill the Output panel with warnings. So narrow water becomes land, and it goes on until there is none, because taking away a narrow arm can leave another narrow arm behind.

The first two are one line each. The third already exists: `BankMap` can already tell which water cells are too narrow, but only inside a private method that warns. The generator needs the answer itself, so that is one of the changes.

## The generator

Make `src/Generation/WorldGenerator.cs`, a C# script that inherits `Node`. Write it so that:

- the file starts with `using` lines for `System.Collections.Generic`, `System.Linq`, `Godot`, and `TopDownTemplate.Ground`, and the class is `public partial class WorldGenerator : Node`, in the `TopDownTemplate.Generation` namespace, with `[Tool]` above it, and a comment: makes the level from a `GenerationProfile`; Generate paints the ground from the terrain noise, then edges the water with the bank and grows the grass, as the buttons on those nodes do; it paints the whole ground again each time, so the same profile gives the same level;
- two constants have comments. `EdgeCells` is `4`: water this close to the edge of the level, in cells, becomes land, so the level ends on grass. `SpawnCells` is `6`: water this close to the middle of the level, in cells, becomes land, so the player starts on dry ground;
- the exports are `Profile` (`GenerationProfile?`), `Ground` (`GroundMap?`), `Bank` (`BankMap?`), `Grass` (`GrassScatter?`), and `Water` (`MeshInstance3D?`), with a comment on the last: the water's plane, stretched to cover the level. A generator that finds its nodes through exports, as `BankMap` does with `Ground`, does not break when you rename or move a node;
- `[ExportToolButton("Generate")] public Callable GenerateButton => Callable.From(Generate);` is the button, as in the last two scripts;
- `Generate()` begins by finding what it needs. `water` is `Ground?.Tiles?.Tiles.FirstOrDefault(tile => tile.IsWater)`, the tile that says it is water, and `land` is `Ground?.Tiles?.Find(Profile?.LandTile ?? "")`, the tile the profile names. If the profile, any node, either tile, or the water's mesh as a `PlaneMesh` is missing, it calls `GD.PushWarning` with what it needs and returns. The check writes the plane out as a pattern, `Water?.Mesh is not PlaneMesh plane`, which both tests and names it for later;
- `half` is `Profile.Size / 2`. A dictionary `cells` from `Vector2I` to `GroundTile` takes one entry for every cell, `x` from `-half.X` up to but not including `half.X` and `z` likewise, so the level is centered on the origin. Each entry is `IsWater(cell, half) ? water : land`;
- a comment says water too narrow for the bank to draw becomes land, until none is left. A `for` loop takes `narrow = NarrowWater(cells, water)`, and while `narrow.Count > 0`, sets each of those cells to `land` and takes `NarrowWater` again. The loop condition and the update are the same call, so the loop ends when a pass finds none;
- only now is the GridMap touched. `Ground.Clear()` empties it, and a loop over `cells` calls `Ground.SetCellItem(new Vector3I(cell.X, 0, cell.Y), tile.Item)`. All the work above was done in a dictionary, so the GridMap is written once, from a finished picture, and never shows a half-made level;
- `Bank.UpdateShore()` and `Grass.Scatter()` are called, in that order, as you would press the two buttons: the grass reads the water's tiles, and the bank reads them too;
- `size` is the level's size in meters, `new Vector2(Profile.Size.X, Profile.Size.Y) * Ground.CellSize.X`, and `Water.Scale = new Vector3(size.X / plane.Size.X, 1f, size.Y / plane.Size.Y)` stretches the plane to it. The plane is a mesh shared by every level that uses the water scene, so the generator scales the node and leaves the mesh alone. The water shader works from the world's positions, so stretching the plane does not stretch the ripples;
- `GD.Print` says how many cells there are and how many are water;
- `IsWater(Vector2I cell, Vector2I half)` finds the cell's distance to the nearest edge, `edge`, as the smallest of the four distances `cell.X + half.X`, `half.X - 1 - cell.X`, and the same for `Y`. If `edge < EdgeCells || cell.Length() < SpawnCells`, it returns `false`: the two rules above. Otherwise `point` is the middle of the cell in meters, `(new Vector2(cell.X, cell.Y) + Vector2.One / 2f) * Ground!.CellSize.X`, and it returns `Profile!.TerrainAt(point) < Profile.WaterLevel`;
- `NarrowWater(Dictionary<Vector2I, GroundTile> cells, GroundTile water)` is a private static method that collects the water cells into a `HashSet<Vector2I>` and returns `BankMap.NarrowWater(...)` of them.

Two choices in it are worth a second look.

**It paints everything, every time.** `Generate` empties the ground and writes all 9,216 cells. That is what makes it repeatable: the level is a function of the profile and nothing else, and no cell can keep something from an earlier press. It also means anything you painted by hand into this ground is gone when you press the button. Lesson 52 is about that, and changes it.

**It asks the noise about the middle of a cell.** The cell at (0, 0) covers meters 0 to 0.5, and its middle is (0.25, 0.25). Asking about a corner instead would work, and would give a level shifted by a quarter of a cell. Pick a convention and keep it, and the same cell always gets the same answer.

## Check it

These numbers were read from the generated level, with seed 15.

- 9,216 cells: 7,544 grass and 1,672 water, 18 percent of the level.
- Update shore placed 416 bank pieces.
- No water cell lies within four cells of the edge or within six cells of the middle.
- No water cell is in water narrower than three cells.
- The water plane is 19.6 meters scaled by 2.449, which is 48 meters.
- Pressing Generate again gave the identical ground, cell for cell.
- Seed 16 gave a different level: 2,970 cells differed, with 2,064 water. Setting the seed back to 15 and pressing the button gave the first level again, cell for cell.

That last line is the point of the module. The seed is the level. A seed written in a save file brings the level back, and two players with the same seed see the same lakes.

## Try it

- Move **Water Level** on the profile to `-0.1`, and press Generate. The same seed gives 2,352 water cells in seven lakes, against 1,672 at `-0.2`. Put it back to `-0.2`.
- Change the profile's **Seed**, press Generate, and look at the lakes. Seeds 1, 13, and 15 all give a handful of lakes of a good size, and seed 7 gives mostly ponds.
- Change **Size** to `64` by `64` and generate: a 32 meter level. Which of the lakes are still there?
- A sand shore would be a second threshold between water and land, a few hundredths above the water level, with its own tile. Where would it go in `Generate`? What would the narrow rule do with it? Dirt patches would be a third noise in the profile, asked about the same way. This template keeps to grass, water, and trees, and leaves both for a game that wants them.

## When it goes wrong

- **Generate does nothing, and the Output panel warns that the generator needs something.** One of the five exports is empty, or the ground's tile set has no tile named like **Land Tile**. Check the names in `ground_tile_set.tres`.
- **There is no water.** The terrain never reaches the water level at this seed. Raise **Water Level**, or try another seed. The check in lesson 49 showed that the share of water varies from seed to seed.
- **The lakes are there but have no bank.** The **Bank** property is empty, or **Bank** has no **Ground**. Select it and check its **Ground** property from lesson 47.
- **The player is in the water when the game starts.** The player is not at the origin. The clearing is around the middle of the level, so the player should start there.
- **The player falls through the floor.** The **Ground** placeholder was deleted, or the box's top is below ground. Its **Position** `y` should be `-0.5` and its **Size** `y` `1`.
- **The level is bigger than the water.** The **Water** property is empty. The plane is the meadow's 19.6 meters until the generator stretches it.
- **Grass grows in the lakes.** Scatter ran before the ground was painted. In the generator it runs last, so check that you did not reorder the calls.
