## A candidate in every square meter

The grass scatter of lesson 32 gave every one-meter square its own plants. The trees do the same. In every square meter of the level there is one **candidate**: a point somewhere in the square, a turn, a size, and a question: should a tree grow here? The answer comes from the rules, asked in this order:

1. **Which kind?** The rules take turns on a roll, each one's share set by its **Weight**. With an oak of 3 and a pine of 2, three candidates in five are oaks.
2. **Is it clear of the edge and the start?** The same cells that kept water away keep trees away.
3. **Is the forest thick here?** The forest noise from lesson 49, at the candidate's point, is turned into a chance. Below the rule's **Starts At** the chance is 0, above its **Full At** it is 1, and in between it rises evenly. A second roll decides whether it grows. This is what makes groves: where the noise is high, almost every candidate grows, and where it is low, none does.
4. **Is it clear of the water?** Not within the rule's **Shore Distance**.
5. **Is it clear of other trees?** Not within the rule's **Spacing** of one that already grew.

A candidate that fails any of these is not a tree, and the ones that pass are placed. The code asks them in this order, cheapest first, so the spacing check, which looks at every tree so far, runs on the fewest.

## A rule is a resource

The five numbers, and the scene to grow, are what a rule is, and a resource holds them. `PropRule` is a resource of the same kind as `GroundTile` or `GenerationProfile`: no node, saved with the profile, and edited in the Inspector.

Make `src/Generation/PropRule.cs`, a C# script that inherits `Resource`. Write it so that:

- the class is `public partial class PropRule : Resource`, in the `TopDownTemplate.Generation` namespace, with `[Tool]` and `[GlobalClass]`, and a comment: one kind of prop that grows in the forest, and where it may stand;
- `[Export] public PackedScene? Scene { get; set; }` is the thing to grow;
- `Weight` is a `float`, `1f`, with a comment: how often this prop is chosen, against the other rules, and `[Export(PropertyHint.Range, "0,10,0.1")]`;
- `StartsAt` and `FullAt` are `float`s with `[Export(PropertyHint.Range, "-1,1,0.05")]`, `0f` and `0.5f`, with one comment over both: the forest noise where this prop starts to grow, and where it grows as thickly as it can;
- `Spacing` and `ShoreDistance` are `float`s, both `2f`, with `[Export(PropertyHint.Range, "0,10,0.1")]` and one comment over both: the least distance in meters to any other prop, and to water.

## The generator grows the trees

The generator gets one more export, a node to put the trees under, and a method that grows them. It also gets a small tidying: the rule that keeps water off the edge and the start now keeps trees off them too, so it moves into a method both can ask.

Open `src/Generation/WorldGenerator.cs` and change it so that:

- the comment on `SpawnCells` now says water and props keep this far from the middle of the level, in cells, so the player starts on open ground;
- there is a new export, `[Export] public Node3D? Props { get; set; }`, below `Water`, with a comment: the node the props are added under. `Generate` checks it with the rest, `Props is null`, and the warning names it;
- in `Generate`, after `Grass.Scatter();`, `var props = PlaceProps(half);` grows the trees, and the final `GD.Print` adds `, and {props} props`;
- the first lines of `IsWater` that decide the edge and the middle become `IsKeptClear(Vector2I cell, Vector2I half)`: a private static method that finds the distance to the nearest edge, as before, and returns `edge < EdgeCells || cell.Length() < SpawnCells`, with a comment: whether a cell is too near the edge of the level or its middle to hold water or props. `IsWater` starts with `if (IsKeptClear(cell, half)) { return false; }`.

Then the new methods:

- `PlaceProps(Vector2I half)` returns how many props grew. It has a comment: grows the props, one candidate in each square meter, in the same order every time; each candidate rolls everything it might need whether or not it is used, so a square comes out the same however its neighbors do. It begins by emptying `Props`: `RemoveChild` and `QueueFree` for each child, so pressing Generate twice does not double the forest;
- `rules` are the profile's rules that have a scene, `Profile.Rules.Where(rule => rule.Scene is not null).ToList()`, `totalWeight` is their weights added up, and `placed` is a `List<Vector2>` of where trees stand. `extent` is the half of the level in meters, `new Vector2I(Mathf.FloorToInt(half.X * cellSize), Mathf.FloorToInt(half.Y * cellSize))`, and two loops walk every square meter, `x` from `-extent.X` and `z` from `-extent.Y`;
- `cellSize` is `Ground!.CellSize.X`, declared before the loops. For each square, `square` is `new Vector2I(x, z)`, and a `RandomNumberGenerator` named `random` is seeded from it with `SquareSeed(square)`, and draws, in a fixed order, the candidate's `point` (the square's corner plus two random numbers from 0 to 1), a `roll`, a `pick` (a random number times `totalWeight`), a `turn` (a random number times `Mathf.Tau`), and a `scale` from 0.85 to 1.2. All five are drawn for every square, even one that will not grow a tree, so the draws never move: what square (4, 7) rolls does not depend on what squares around it did;
- `rule` is `PickRule(rules, pick)`, and `cell` is the ground cell the point is in, `Mathf.FloorToInt(point.X / cellSize)` and the same for `Y`. The candidate is skipped, with `continue`, if `rule is null`, if `IsKeptClear(cell, half)`, if `roll >= Density(rule, point)`, if `IsNearWater(cell, rule.ShoreDistance)`, or if any `placed` point is nearer than `rule.Spacing`. Order the checks cheapest first, so the spacing check, which looks at every tree so far, runs on the fewest;
- a tree is `rule.Scene!.Instantiate<Node3D>()`, named `Prop_{x}_{z}`, so the names are the same every press and a version control diff shows what moved, with `Position` at `point` on the ground, `Rotation` of `turn` about the vertical, and `Scale` of `Vector3.One * scale`. It is added to `Props`, and `prop.Owner = Owner;`, with a comment: owned by the scene's root, so the scene saves it. The point goes into `placed`;
- `PickRule(List<PropRule> rules, float pick)` has a comment: the rule a roll between 0 and the total weight lands on, when the rules take turns along it in order. It takes each rule's weight off `pick` until `pick` goes below 0, and returns that rule. For weights of 3 and 2, a pick below 3 is the oak and the rest is the pine;
- `Density(PropRule rule, Vector2 point)` has a comment: how likely a prop is to grow at a point, 0 where the forest noise is at `StartsAt`, and 1 where it is at `FullAt`. It is `Mathf.Clamp(Mathf.Remap(Profile!.ForestAt(point), rule.StartsAt, rule.FullAt, 0f, 1f), 0f, 1f)`;
- `IsNearWater(Vector2I cell, float distance)` looks at every cell within `Mathf.CeilToInt(distance / cellSize)` cells in each direction, and returns `true` for the first one within `distance` meters that is water, `Ground.IsWater(new Vector3I(...))`. It is the same `IsWater` that Update shore used in lesson 47;
- `SquareSeed(Vector2I square)` has a comment: a number from the square and the seed, the same on every machine. It is lesson 32's, with the profile's seed: `unchecked((ulong)((square.X * 73856093) ^ (square.Y * 19349663) ^ (Profile!.Seed * 83492791)))`.

Two things in this are worth going over.

**Why every roll is drawn every time.** Suppose the random numbers were drawn only when a candidate got that far, say the turn only for trees that pass. Then moving the water changes which candidates pass, which changes how many numbers each square has used, and a square nowhere near the water gets different numbers. A lake that moves a meter would change trees across the level. Drawing all five for every square makes each square's numbers depend on its own coordinates and the seed, and nothing else.

**Why the spacing check is still repeatable.** The spacing rule depends on what has already grown, so the order matters: whether the tree at (5, 5) grows depends on whether (4, 5) did. That is fine, as long as the order is fixed. The loops go along `x` and `z` in the same direction every time, so the same seed grows the same trees. A parallel loop would not.

## Check it

These numbers were read from the level, with seed 15.

- 79 trees, 51 oaks and 28 pines, which is 65 and 35 percent, near the 3 to 2 of the weights.
- Every tree is owned by the scene, so the scene saves it.
- The closest two trees are 2.00 meters apart, which is the spacing.
- No tree stands in water, in the clearing around the middle, or within four cells of the edge.
- The nearest water cell to any tree is 1.81 meters away, a little under the 2 meters of the rule. The rule measures from the middle of the cell the tree stands in to the middle of a water cell, and a tree can stand up to about 0.35 meters from the middle of its own cell. The shore is a margin of about two meters, not an exact one.
- The forest noise averages 0.31 at the trees, against -0.01 over the level. The trees are where the noise is high.
- Pressing Generate again gave the same 79 trees, the same names, positions, turns, and sizes.
- Seed 16 gave a different forest. Setting 15 again and pressing the button gave the first one back, tree for tree.

## Try it

- Raise the pine's **Starts At** to `0.2`. The pines now keep to the middle of the groves, and the oaks make the edges. Look at a grove, and then put it back.
- Raise both **Spacing** values to `3`, or lower them to `1.2`. How does a grove change? Look at the count in the Output panel.
- Set a rule's **Shore Distance** to `0.5`. The trees come to the water's edge. Why does the bank, with its lip, still look right?
- A rock, a bush, or a mushroom is one more rule: a `PropRule` with its scene and a weight. Lesson 54 adds a bush this way. A rule that should grow in the open as well as in the forest would take a **Starts At** of `-1`.

## When it goes wrong

- **No trees, and no warning.** The rules have no scene, or the **Forest** noise never gets high enough at this seed. Check **Scene** on both rules, and look at the forest noise in the preview texture from lesson 49.
- **The warning says the generator needs something.** **Props** is empty. Assign **Trees** to it.
- **Twice as many trees after the second press.** The generator did not empty **Props**. The `foreach` over its children has to come before the loops.
- **Trees grow inside the lake.** The ground was painted after the trees. `PlaceProps` must run after the ground is written, because `IsNearWater` asks the GridMap.
- **The trees move when you press Generate again.** Something in a candidate's numbers is not from the square and the seed: a `GD.Randf()` or a `RandomNumberGenerator` that was not seeded. Every random number must come from the square's own generator.
- **Trees are in two heaps, with nothing between them.** The forest noise has a high frequency, or the **Full At** is low. Both show in the preview texture.
- **The player is inside a tree at the start.** `IsKeptClear` is not used in `PlaceProps`, or `SpawnCells` is `0`.
