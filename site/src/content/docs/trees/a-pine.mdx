## What you will build

A pine, and three of them planted among the oaks. It is the whole pipeline of lessons 24 to 28 again, shorter, because most of it carries over: the shader, the wind, the trunk's bark, the collision script. What changes is the species: a straight trunk with no limbs, an atlas of needles, and a crown that is not a dome of clumps but a stack of skirts, one ring of cards for each tier.

## How a pine differs

An oak's crown is a dome made of clumps, and its trunk forks. A pine has one thing the eye reads, its **tiers**: horizontal layers of needles that start wide near the bottom and narrow to the tip, each hanging a little lower at its outer edge. Under that, a straight trunk. So this lesson changes three things and nothing else:

- **The skeleton** is a leader: one chain of vertices straight up, with no forks. Needles hide the thin limbs, so a pine needs none.
- **The atlas** is needles: long thin strokes fanned out of a twig, painted with the **Twig** brush you made in lesson 14.
- **The crown group** is short. There is no envelope and no clumps: it places six rings of points at six heights, with a card on each point leaning out and down.

The foliage shader of lesson 27 is not touched. It reads the same data from a card, so a pine's cards need only to carry it.

## Paint the needles

<Steps>

1. In Krita, make a new 1024 by 1024 image named `pine_atlas` with one layer, clear it to transparent, and turn on the 512 pixel grid, as in the first steps of lesson 25: **File > New...**, **Select > Select All**, **Edit > Clear**, **Select > Deselect**, and **Settings > Dockers > Grid and Guides**.

2. Press <Kbd keys="B" /> and pick **Basic-2 Opacity**. Set **Size** to `8` and **Opacity** to `100`. Set a dark blue-gray, hue `215`, saturation `10`, value `30`. In each cell, drag a twig from near the bottom of the cell up through its middle, to about a quarter of the way down from the top, bending it a little. Keep 16 pixels or more clear of the cell's lines.

	<Figure
		src={twigs}
		alt="Krita with a see-through canvas split into four cells, each with one thin, slightly bent dark twig in the middle."
		caption="Four twigs."
	/>

3. Pick your **Twig** brush from lesson 14. It starts every stroke at full size and tapers it to a point, which is exactly a needle. Set **Size** to `14`. Pick the first gray from this table, hue, saturation, and value:

	| Gray | Hue | Saturation | Value |
	|---|---|---|---|
	| Cool, mid | 215 | 14 | 52 |
	| Cool, dark | 210 | 18 | 44 |
	| Cool, light | 222 | 10 | 60 |
	| Warm, mid | 34 | 18 | 56 |

4. Paint needles on each twig: short strokes that leave the twig and sweep outward and upward, about 100 to 200 pixels long, thirty or so to a side, from the twig's foot to near its tip, a little longer in the middle than at the ends. Change the gray every so often, so a spray mixes all four.

	<Figure
		src={atlas}
		alt="Krita with four sprays of gray-blue and gray-brown needles, each a fishbone of long tapering strokes leaving a dark twig, one spray in each cell."
		caption="Four sprays of needles."
	/>

5. Save the file as `pine_atlas.kra` in a new `art_source\nature\tree_pine` folder in your project, and export it as `pine_atlas.png` into a new `art\nature\tree_pine` folder, leaving **Store alpha channel** checked.

6. In Godot, click `art/nature/tree_pine/pine_atlas.png`, open the **Import** tab, set **Compress > Mode** to **VRAM Compressed**, check **Mipmaps > Generate**, and click **Reimport (\*)**, as for the oak's atlas.

</Steps>

## The crown group

The crown is a short group on a plane. The plane is only a place to hang the modifier on: the group builds everything from nothing.

<Steps>

1. With the pointer over the 3D view, press <Kbd keys="Shift+A" />, search `plane`, and press <Kbd keys="Enter" />. Press <Kbd keys="F2" /> and name it `needles`.

2. Click the **Geometry Nodes** tab and click **New** in the node editor's header. Make the editor fill the window with <Kbd keys="Ctrl+Space" mac="Cmd+Space" />, and build the group in four chapters, as in lesson 26: add each node with <Kbd keys="Shift+A" />, name it with <Kbd keys="F2" />, set its fields, and link them.

</Steps>

### Chapter 1: tiers

The first chapter makes six rings, one at each tier's height, each smaller than the one below.

<Steps>

1. Add these nodes:

	| Node | Settings |
	|---|---|
	| **Tier heights**, from Mesh Line | mode **Offset**, Count `6`, Start Location `0, 0, 1.5`, Offset `0, 0, 0.76` |
	| **Tier ring**, from Mesh Circle | Vertices `14`, Radius `1` |
	| **Tier number**, from Index | nothing |
	| **Tier reach**, from Map Range | type **Float**, From Min `0`, From Max `5`, To Min `0.9`, To Max `0` |
	| **Ring size**, from Combine XYZ | Z `1` |
	| **Ring on each height**, from Instance on Points | nothing |
	| **Realize rings**, from Realize Instances | nothing |

2. Make these links:

	| From | To |
	|---|---|
	| Tier heights, Mesh | Ring on each height, Points |
	| Tier ring, Mesh | Ring on each height, Instance |
	| Tier number, Index | Tier reach, Value |
	| Tier reach, Result | Ring size, X |
	| Tier reach, Result | Ring size, Y |
	| Ring size, Vector | Ring on each height, Scale |
	| Ring on each height, Instances | Realize rings, Geometry |
	| Realize rings, Geometry | Group Output, Geometry |

</Steps>

<Figure
	src={tiersNodes}
	alt="The node editor with chapter 1's seven nodes: Tier heights and Tier ring, a Tier number, Tier reach, and Ring size chain, and Ring on each height with Realize rings, joined to the Group Output."
	caption="Chapter 1: seven nodes."
/>

**Tier heights** is a line of six points, 76 centimeters apart, from 1.5 meters up to 5.3. **Tier ring** is a circle of 14 points. **Ring on each height** puts a copy of the circle on every point. **Tier number** is each point's number, 0 to 5, and **Tier reach** turns it into a radius from `0.9` for the bottom tier to `0` for the top, where the cards meet at the axis in a point, so **Ring size** scales each circle by it. The rings are small because each card is 1.5 meters long and centered on its ring point, so its outer half reaches 0.75 meters past the ring, to about the radius a pine's tier would have.

### Chapter 2: a card on each point

<Steps>

1. Add these nodes:

	| Node | Settings |
	|---|---|
	| **Position**, from Position | nothing |
	| **Flatten**, from Vector Math | operation **Multiply**, Vector (second) `1, 1, 0` |
	| **Outward**, from Vector Math | operation **Normalize** |
	| **Half outward**, from Vector Math | operation **Scale**, Scale `0.5` |
	| **Add up**, from Vector Math | operation **Add**, Vector (second) `0, 0, 1` |
	| **Facing**, from Vector Math | operation **Normalize** |
	| **Store tier normal**, from Store Named Attribute | type **Vector**, domain **Point**, Name `tier_normal` |
	| **Face this way**, from Align Rotation to Vector | axis **Z**, pivot **Auto** |
	| **Roll outward**, from Align Rotation to Vector | axis **Y**, pivot **Z** |
	| **Card**, from Grid | Size X `1.5`, Size Y `1.5`, Vertices X `2`, Vertices Y `2` |
	| **Card UVs**, from Store Named Attribute | type **2D Vector**, domain **Face Corner**, Name `UVMap` |
	| **Card on each point**, from Instance on Points | nothing |

2. Make these links:

	| From | To |
	|---|---|
	| Position, Position | Flatten, Vector |
	| Flatten, Vector | Outward, Vector |
	| Outward, Vector | Half outward, Vector |
	| Half outward, Vector | Add up, Vector |
	| Add up, Vector | Facing, Vector |
	| Realize rings, Geometry | Store tier normal, Geometry |
	| Facing, Vector | Store tier normal, Value |
	| Facing, Vector | Face this way, Vector |
	| Face this way, Rotation | Roll outward, Rotation |
	| Outward, Vector | Roll outward, Vector |
	| Card, Mesh | Card UVs, Geometry |
	| Card, UV Map | Card UVs, Value |
	| Store tier normal, Geometry | Card on each point, Points |
	| Card UVs, Geometry | Card on each point, Instance |
	| Roll outward, Rotation | Card on each point, Rotation |
	| Card on each point, Instances | Group Output, Geometry |

</Steps>

<Figure
	src={cardsNodes}
	alt="The node editor with chapter 2's twelve nodes: a row of Vector Math nodes from Position to Facing, two Align Rotation to Vector nodes, a Card and its UVs, and Card on each point."
	caption="Chapter 2: twelve nodes."
/>

- The first six nodes work out each point's **direction**. **Position** is where a ring point is, and **Flatten** keeps only its X and Y, so the vector points straight out from the trunk. **Outward** makes it length 1. **Half outward** halves it, **Add up** adds `0, 0, 1`, and **Facing** scales the result to length 1: a direction that is mostly up, tipped outward. That is the way a tier's top faces, and it is also the normal you want the crown shaded by, so **Store tier normal** writes it onto each point, as `tier_normal`, as lesson 26 wrote `env_normal`.
- **Face this way** turns a card so that it faces that direction: the card lies like a skirt tilted down toward its outer edge. **Roll outward** spins it about that axis until the card's **Y** axis points along **Outward**: the card is centered on its ring point, and its far half sweeps outward past the ring.
- **Card** is the same flat square as the oak's, here 1.5 meters, and **Card UVs** stores its UVs.

### Chapter 4: colors and normals

<Steps>

1. Add these nodes:

	| Node | Settings |
	|---|---|
	| **Read phase**, from Named Attribute | type **Float**, Name `phase` |
	| **Read UVs**, from Named Attribute | type **Vector**, Name `UVMap` |

	|---|---|

	src={shadingNodes}

The oak's blue channel was how deep in the crown a card sat. A pine's depth is simpler: it rises from `0.4` at the stem edge of a card to `1` at its far edge, so each skirt is darker where it meets the trunk and lighter at its rim, and the shader's depth darkening gives the tiers their layered look. The custom normal is just the stored `tier_normal`.

## Try it

- Change `6` in **Tier heights** to `8`, the **Offset** to `0, 0, 0.56`, and **From Max** in **Tier reach** to `7`: a taller pine with more tiers. Its top tier is at 5.42 meters, so move the trunk's tip vertex up a little too. Change **To Max** in **Tier reach** to `0.2`, and the top opens into a ring.
- Raise **Radius** in **Tier ring** or the `0.9` in **Tier reach**, for a pine with wide skirts.
- Change **Leaf Color** to a gray-blue, `5E7F8F`, for a spruce, with no other change.
- Paint a second atlas with fewer, longer needles and use it in a new material, for a different pine.

## If it does not work

- **The pine's crown is a flat disk or a cone with no gaps.** The cards are not leaning: check that **Face this way**'s **Vector** is linked to **Facing**, and that **Add up** adds `0, 0, 1`, not `0, 0, 0`.
- **The tiers do not narrow toward the top.** **Tier reach** has **To Min** and **To Max** reversed. **To Min** is `0.9`, the bottom tier.
- **The needles show a flat color with no strokes.** The atlas is not set in the material, or `needles.tres` still holds the oak's atlas.
- **The pine is bright lime green.** **Leaf Color** is still the oak's. Set it to `2F7A4C`.
- **The trunk shows through the top tier.** The skeleton's tip is higher than the top tier. The tip should end at `5.1`, and the top tier's cards are at `5.3`.
- **The cards are white in Godot.** The exporter's **Use Vertex Color** is not **Active**, or the mesh has no `Col` attribute. Do the two steps of Export and export again.
