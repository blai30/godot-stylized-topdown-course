## What you will build

Two shaders and a shared include that turn the oak's colored cards into a tree: leaves cut out of the cards, lit as one soft crown, and moving in the wind. By the end, the oak in the meadow has a crown that is lighter and warmer on top and darker and cooler beneath, that casts a leafy shadow, and that leans and flutters as a gust crosses it.

<Figure
	src={noon}
	alt="The game at noon from above: the player capsule beside a round green oak with a lighter, warmer top and a darker underside, casting a leafy shadow on the grass."
	caption="The oak at noon in the game."
/>

The wind is not a script. A shader can compute where each vertex should be from the time and its own position, so the wind is a few lines in the shader, and it works for any number of trees with no per-frame code. The only things set outside the shader are two numbers, the wind's direction and strength, which are **shader globals**, as the rim's color and strength were in lesson 23.

## What the shader reads

Lesson 26 gave each card three numbers in its vertex colors, and a normal. Here is how this lesson's shader uses them.

| Data | From | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Vertex color red, the **phase** | The card | Which of the atlas's four sprays the card shows, and the card's own flutter |
| Vertex color green | The card's far edge | How much the card flutters: none at the stem, most at the far edge |
| Vertex color blue, the **depth** | The card's place in the crown | Darkening the leaves deep inside |
| The custom normal | The card's corners | Lighting every card as part of one dome |
| The atlas's alpha | The picture you painted | Cutting leaves out of the card |
| The atlas's color | The picture you painted | A multiplier around mid-gray on the leaf color |

## Piece 1: the wind include

The wind goes in an include, because the bark and the cards must move by exactly the same rule, or the leaves would drift away from their branches.

<Steps>

1. Click **Shader Editor** at the bottom of the window. In its **File** menu, choose **New Shader Include...**. Set **Path** to `res://art/shaders/wind.gdshaderinc` and click **Create**.

	<Figure
		src={createInclude}
		alt="The Create Shader dialog: Type ShaderInclude, Path res://art/shaders/wind.gdshaderinc."
		caption="A new include beside the lighting one."
	/>

</Steps>

Type into it so that:

- a comment on the first line says it is wind for anything that sways: one gust front that crosses the meadow, and the sway and flutter it drives;
- `global uniform vec2 wind_direction;` and `global uniform float wind_strength;` read the globals, with a comment above them: which way the wind blows along the ground (x east, y south), and how hard, from 0 to 1;
- constants follow, each with a comment. The gust: `GUST_LENGTH` `24.0` meters, `GUST_SECONDS` `8.0`, and `GUST_FLOOR` `0.4`, the share of its strength that the wind never drops below. The sway: `SWAY_HZ` `0.5`, `TREE_BEND` `0.03`, and `CANOPY_HEIGHT` `6.0`. The flutter: `FLUTTER_HZ` `1.125` and `CARD_FLUTTER` `0.06`;
- a function `float gust_strength(vec3 world_position)` has a comment: the wind's strength here, now, as the wave's crest passes each spot in turn, from upwind to downwind. It takes `normalize(wind_direction)` as `heading`, then `float wave = 0.5 + 0.5 * sin(TAU * (TIME / GUST_SECONDS - dot(world_position.xz, heading) / GUST_LENGTH));`, which runs between 0 and 1, and returns `wind_strength * mix(GUST_FLOOR, 1.0, wave)`;
- a function `vec3 tree_sway(vec3 world_position, vec3 tree_origin, float tree_phase)` has a comment: how far the whole tree leans at a point on it, in world space, where the tree's own phase keeps a grove from swaying in step. It takes `normalize(wind_direction)` as `heading`, as `gust_strength` does, then computes `float rise = (world_position.y - tree_origin.y) / CANOPY_HEIGHT;`, `float swing = sin(TAU * (tree_phase + SWAY_HZ * TIME));`, and `float lean = TREE_BEND * CANOPY_HEIGHT * rise * rise * gust_strength(world_position) * swing;`, with a comment that the top moves most, since the lean grows with the square of the height, and returns `vec3(heading.x, 0.0, heading.y) * lean`;
- a function `vec3 card_flutter(vec3 world_position, float tree_phase, float card_phase, float far_edge)` has a comment: how far a card flutters, along its own direction, more at its far edge, in step with its own phase. It makes `float angle = TAU * card_phase;`, `vec3 direction = vec3(cos(angle), 0.6, sin(angle));` (a direction that leans a little up), and `float beat = sin(TAU * (tree_phase + card_phase + FLUTTER_HZ * TIME));`, and returns `direction * CARD_FLUTTER * far_edge * gust_strength(world_position) * beat`.

<Aside type="note" title="How the wind works">
`TIME` is the number of seconds the game has run. Every vertex asks the same question, "what is the wind where I am, right now?", and gets a different answer because the gust is a wave: `TIME / GUST_SECONDS` moves its crest along, and `dot(world_position.xz, heading) / GUST_LENGTH` offsets each spot by how far it is along the wind. The crest takes 8 seconds to repeat and is 24 meters long, so across a meadow some trees are in a gust while others are in a lull, and the gust crosses them in turn. That is what reads as wind, and no tree needs to know about the others.

The sway and the flutter each take their strength from the gust, so a lull stills them and a gust sets them off. The tree's `tree_phase` shifts its swing in time, and each card's red channel shifts its flutter, so no two move in step.

Godot's `TIME` starts over every hour. The rates here are chosen to make a whole number of cycles in an hour (1800 sways, 4050 flutters, and 450 gusts), so nothing jumps when it does.
</Aside>

## Piece 2: the foliage shader

<Steps>

1. In the Shader Editor, choose **File > New Shader...**, set **Path** to `res://art/shaders/foliage.gdshader`, keep **Mode** on **Spatial**, and click **Create**.

	<Figure
		src={createFoliage}
		alt="The Create Shader dialog: Type Shader, Mode Spatial, Path res://art/shaders/foliage.gdshader."
		caption="A shader for the cards."
	/>

</Steps>

Type into it so that:

- it starts with `shader_type spatial;` and `render_mode cull_disabled;`, then a blank line, then `#include "res://art/shaders/wind.gdshaderinc"`. A card is a single flat face, and `cull_disabled` draws both its sides, so it shows from behind too;
- `leaf_atlas` is a `sampler2D` uniform with `filter_linear_mipmap_anisotropic`, with a comment: the leaf atlas from lesson 25. It has no `source_color`, because the atlas is a table of multipliers and not a picture, and the shader reads its numbers as they are. `leaf_color` is a `vec3` with `source_color`, set to `vec3(0.408, 0.765, 0.263)`, with a comment: the color the atlas's middle gray becomes. `instance uniform float tree_phase = 0.0;` has a comment: set on each tree, so a grove does not sway in step. An **instance uniform** is a parameter each instance of a mesh carries for itself, where an ordinary uniform is shared by every use of the material;
- constants follow, each with a comment. `LEAF_GAIN` is `255.0 / 128.0`: the atlas stores a multiplier around 0.5, 128 of 255 means 1.0, and this gain keeps the leaf color on average. `CANOPY_TOP` is `vec3(1.25, 1.22, 0.95)`, lighter and warmer, for cards facing up, and `CANOPY_UNDER` is `vec3(0.5, 0.6, 0.6)`, darker and cooler, for cards facing down. `CANOPY_GRADE` is `vec2(-0.4, 0.85)`, the heights of the normal where the grade starts and is complete. `CANOPY_INNER` is `0.55`, and `CANOPY_DEPTH` is `vec2(0.3, 0.95)`: leaves deep in the canopy darken to `CANOPY_INNER`, from the depth `CANOPY_DEPTH.y` inward to `CANOPY_DEPTH.x`. `LEAF_WRAP` is `0.25`, with a comment: leaves wrap light less than rocks do, so each clump keeps a shaded side;
- three `varying` variables, `vec3 world_normal`, `float canopy_depth`, and `float atlas_cell`, carry values from `vertex()` to `fragment()`;
- `void vertex()` has the comments and lines below. `world_normal = normalize(MODEL_NORMAL_MATRIX * NORMAL);`, with a comment that this is the normal stored in lesson 26, kept in world space, because Godot flips `NORMAL` on the back of a card and this does not. `canopy_depth = COLOR.b;`. `atlas_cell = floor(COLOR.r * 3.999);`, with a comment that a card's phase also picks which of the atlas's four sprays it shows. `vec3 world_position = (MODEL_MATRIX * vec4(VERTEX, 1.0)).xyz;` and `vec3 tree_origin = MODEL_MATRIX[3].xyz;`. `vec3 wind = tree_sway(world_position, tree_origin, tree_phase) + card_flutter(world_position, tree_phase, COLOR.r, COLOR.g);`. And last `VERTEX += inverse(mat3(MODEL_MATRIX)) * wind;`, with a comment that the shader moves the vertex in model space, so the world-space push is turned around;
- `void fragment()` computes `vec2 cell = vec2(mod(atlas_cell, 2.0), floor(atlas_cell / 2.0));`, the cell's column and row in the atlas's 2 by 2, and `vec4 leaf = texture(leaf_atlas, (UV + cell) * 0.5);`. `ALPHA = leaf.a;` and `ALPHA_SCISSOR_THRESHOLD = 0.5;`. `NORMAL = normalize((VIEW_MATRIX * vec4(world_normal, 0.0)).xyz);`, with a comment: light every card with the stored normal, turned to view space. Then `float top = smoothstep(CANOPY_GRADE.x, CANOPY_GRADE.y, world_normal.y);` and `float inner = mix(CANOPY_INNER, 1.0, smoothstep(CANOPY_DEPTH.x, CANOPY_DEPTH.y, canopy_depth));`, then `ALBEDO = leaf_color * leaf.rgb * LEAF_GAIN * mix(CANOPY_UNDER, CANOPY_TOP, top) * inner;` and `ROUGHNESS = 1.0;`;
- `void light()` has the same body as lesson 23's, with `LEAF_WRAP` in place of `WRAP`: it computes `float wrapped = clamp((dot(NORMAL, LIGHT) + LEAF_WRAP) / (1.0 + LEAF_WRAP), 0.0, 1.0);` and adds `wrapped * ATTENUATION * LIGHT_COLOR / PI` to `DIFFUSE_LIGHT`.

Here is what the shader does, step by step.

- **Which spray.** A card's phase, red, is a random number from 0 up to but not including 1. `floor(phase * 3.999)` makes it 0, 1, 2, or 3, and that picks the cell. The card's UVs run 0 to 1 over the whole card, so `(UV + cell) * 0.5` is the same place inside that cell of the atlas.
- **Leaves.** `ALPHA` is the atlas's transparency. With `ALPHA_SCISSOR_THRESHOLD`, a pixel is either fully there or fully cut away, so a card keeps a hard leafy edge and still writes to the depth buffer, as a solid does. That is why the tree's *shadow* is leafy: the shadow pass cuts away the same pixels.
- **One soft crown.** Setting `NORMAL` from `world_normal` lights every card with the normal you stored in Blender, not the card's own. Lesson 26's custom normals make the whole crown shade as one dome.
- **The grade.** `top` is 0 where the stored normal points down and 1 where it points up. The card's color mixes from the cooler, darker `CANOPY_UNDER` to the warmer, lighter `CANOPY_TOP` as it does. `inner` darkens by the depth stored in blue, so leaves inside the crown read as in shade, and the surface leaves read as in the light.

<Aside type="note" title="What the leaf color is, and is not">
The oak's green comes from three things: `leaf_color`, the atlas's values near mid-gray, and the grade. Change `leaf_color` in the material and every leaf changes together, and the atlas keeps its warm and cool leaves. An autumn oak is the same material with a different `leaf_color`.

## Try it

- In the `wind_strength` global, set `1`. Run the game and watch the oak: it leans farther and flutters harder. Set it back to `0.6` after. A strength of `0` stills it.
- In `wind.gdshaderinc`, change `GUST_LENGTH` to `6.0`. The gusts become short, and a large tree is in a gust and a lull at the same time.
- In `foliage.gdshader`, set `LEAF_WRAP` to `0.5`, the rocks' value. The crown's shaded side gets lighter and the clumps lose some of their depth. Put it back after.
- Set `CANOPY_INNER` to `1.0`: the leaves deep inside are as bright as the ones on the surface, and the crown flattens.

## If it does not work

- **The shader reports "Unknown identifier".** A name is misspelled, or the include is missing. `tree_sway` and `card_flutter` come from `wind.gdshaderinc`, so check the `#include` line at the top of the shader.
- **The leaves are a flat black-green.** The atlas is not set: check **Leaf Atlas** in the material, and that `leaf_atlas.png` is the picture from lesson 25.
- **The cards are solid squares.** `ALPHA` is not set to the atlas's alpha, or `ALPHA_SCISSOR_THRESHOLD` is missing.
- **The leaves are too light or too dark overall.** The atlas's values should be near the middle: if you painted it much lighter or darker than mid-gray, scale `LEAF_GAIN` to match. Or change `leaf_color`.
- **The crown does not move.** The globals are not there, or `wind_strength` is `0`. Check **Project Settings > Globals > Shader Globals**, and that the material on `leaves` is the foliage shader.
- **The branches and the leaves move apart.** The trunk is not using `bark.gdshader`, or the trunk and the `leaves` node have different `tree_phase` values. Both stay at `0` until lesson 28's script sets them to the same number.
- **Both sides of the cards look different.** `world_normal` is not used. The cards must take their lighting from the normal in the vertex shader, not from `NORMAL` as Godot provides it.
- **A card shows the wrong part of the atlas, or a seam.** `(UV + cell) * 0.5` is the cell's place in the atlas: check the `0.5` and the cell calculation.
