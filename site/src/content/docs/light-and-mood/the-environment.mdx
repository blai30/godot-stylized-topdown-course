## What you will build

The meadow's atmosphere, in a scene of its own: a sky that colors every shadow, gentle contact shadows, a soft haze, and a tone mapper that keeps colors from burning out. You put all of it in `world/environment/world_environment.tscn`, so every level can share it and the next lessons can animate it.

## The idea

The [Environment](/reference/glossary/#environment) resource holds everything about the world that is not a model or a light. You met it in lesson 4. Now you tune each part for the look of the template, which is soft, a little hazy, and lit mostly by the sky.

| Part | What it does | Value you set |
|---|---|---|
| Sky | Draws the background and, more importantly, is the source of ambient and reflected light. | A gradient from a horizon color to a zenith color, from a shader you write. |
| [Ambient light](/reference/glossary/#ambient-light) | Light that arrives from the whole sky, so shadows are never black. | Taken from the sky, scaled to 0.65. |
| Reflected light | What shiny surfaces mirror. | Taken from the sky. |
| [Tonemapping](/reference/glossary/#tonemapping) | Squeezes bright light into what a screen can show. | AgX, with its defaults. |
| [Ambient occlusion](/reference/glossary/#ambient-occlusion) | Darkens creases and the foot of things, where the sky is blocked. | On, radius 1.5, intensity 1.0. |
| [Glow](/reference/glossary/#glow) | A soft halo around bright spots. | Soft light, intensity 0.45. Only visible at night. |
| [Fog](/reference/glossary/#fog) | A veil that grows with distance and lifts the darkest colors toward the sky's color. | Exponential, density 0.0006. |

The camera never sees much of the sky: it looks down at the ground. The sky still matters, because Godot lights every surface from it.

## Piece 1: a sky shader

Godot ships a `ProceduralSkyMaterial` that you used in lesson 4. Here you write the sky yourself, with the technique of lesson 15, so that later lessons can animate its two colors.

<Steps>

1. Switch to the `world_environment` tab and select the root node. In the Environment, set **Background > Mode** to **Sky**.

2. In the **Sky** section, click the **Sky** row's empty field and under **New** choose **Sky**. Click the new Sky to open it. Its **Sky Material** row already holds a `PanoramaSkyMaterial`. Click the down arrow at the right end of the row and, under **New**, choose **ShaderMaterial**.

3. Click the new material to open it. Click its **Shader** field and choose **New Shader...**. In the **Create Shader** dialog, **Mode** is already **Sky**, because Godot knows what the material is for. Set **Path** to `res://world/environment/sky.gdshader` and click **Create**.

	<Figure
		src={createShader}
		alt="The Create Shader dialog: Type Shader, Mode Sky, Template Default, Built-in Shader unchecked, Path res://world/environment/sky.gdshader."
		caption="The Create Shader dialog. The Mode is Sky."
	/>

</Steps>

A sky shader is not like the ground shader. It has no `fragment()` function. It has `sky()`, which Godot runs for every direction you could look in, and `EYEDIR` is that direction. `EYEDIR.y` is 1 straight up, 0 at the horizon, and negative below it.

Replace the shader's contents so that:

- it starts with `shader_type sky;`;
- it has two `vec3` uniforms with `source_color`, `zenith_color` and `horizon_color`, defaulting to `vec3(0.31, 0.66, 1.0)` and `vec3(0.56, 0.78, 1.0)`, a deeper and a paler daytime blue, with a short comment above them;
- it has a `float` uniform `ground_darkening` with `hint_range(0.0, 1.0)`, defaulting to `0.5`, with a comment: how much darker the sky gets looking down from the horizon;
- `sky()` first sets `float height = clamp(EYEDIR.y, 0.0, 1.0);`, with a comment that it is 0 at the horizon and 1 straight up;
- then it sets `COLOR = mix(horizon_color, zenith_color, height);`. `mix` blends from the first color to the second, by `height`;
- then it multiplies `COLOR` by `1.0 - ground_darkening * clamp(-EYEDIR.y, 0.0, 1.0)`, so looking down darkens the sky and the light that comes from below stays dim.

## Ambient and reflected light

Both the ambient and the reflected light take the sky as their **Source**. You set it explicitly, so the lighting no longer depends on the **Background** being the sky. The sky is also too bright: it is as strong as the sun. Scale it with **Background > Energy Multiplier**, which scales the sky's light, and set it to `0.65`.

Set:

- **Ambient Light > Source** to **Sky**;
- **Reflected Light > Source** to **Sky**;
- **Background > Energy Multiplier** to `0.65`.

<Figure
	src={ambientRows}
	alt="The Inspector with Background Energy Multiplier 0.65, Ambient Light Source Sky, and Reflected Light Source Sky."
	caption="Light from the sky, at 0.65."
/>

<Figure

<Aside type="note" title="Why 0.65">
By day, the template keeps the sky's light well below the sun's, so sunlit grass is several times brighter than grass in shade. Too much ambient light flattens the picture. The value comes from FactoryGame, the game this look follows, and lesson 21 animates it through the day.
</Aside>

## Tonemapping

The default, **Linear**, shows the renderer's light as it is and clips what is too bright. Set **Tonemap > Mode** to **AgX**, which eases bright colors toward white. Leave **Exposure**, **Agx White**, and **Agx Contrast** at their defaults.

<Figure
	src={tonemapRows}

AgX looks flat next to Linear. That is on purpose: the template's colors come from the palette and the light, not from the tone mapper, and a calm base leaves room for dusk and night to change the mood.

## Ambient occlusion

Where a rock meets the ground, the sky cannot reach, and the ground there should be a little darker. [Ambient occlusion](/reference/glossary/#ambient-occlusion) (SSAO) darkens those spots from the picture on screen. Check **SSAO > On**, and set:

- **Radius** to `1.5`: how far from a surface it looks for blockers, in meters;
- **Intensity** to `1.0`: the default is 2.0, which is heavy for this look;
- **Power** to `1.0`: the default is 1.5;
- **Detail** to `0.0`: detail darkens small cracks, and the soft models have none.

The effect is gentle on purpose. It costs graphics-card time on every frame, and the template defaults to it on. If your project must run on weak hardware, this is the first thing to turn off.

## Glow

Check **Glow > On**. Glow makes the brightest spots bleed a soft halo. Set:

- **Blend Mode** to **Soft Light**, which never brightens past white, and so suits a calm picture;
- **Intensity** to `0.45`;
- **Bloom** to `0.05`, a slight haze;
- **HDR Threshold** to `0.9`: only things brighter than this glow.

<Figure
	src={glowRows}
	alt="The Inspector's Glow section, on, with Levels, Intensity 0.45, Strength 1.0, Bloom 0.05, Blend Mode Soft Light, and HDR Threshold 0.9."
	caption="Glow, set for soft light."
/>

You will see little change by day: nothing in the meadow is bright enough to cross the threshold. Glow is for the night, when lamps and the moon do. Lesson 21 keys its intensity so that it is 0 by day.

## Fog

[Fog](/reference/glossary/#fog) hides distance and lifts the darkest colors toward the fog's color, so a shadow is never black but a deep sky blue. Check **Fog > On**, and set:

- **Light Color** to `#B8DBFF`, the pale blue of the horizon;
- **Aerial Perspective** to `0.5`, which tints the fog with the sky's color;
- **Sky Affect** to `0.0`, so the fog does not cover the sky;
- **Sun Scatter** to `0.15`, a little glow in the fog toward the sun.

Leave **Mode** on **Exponential**. Do not touch **Density** yet.

<Figure
	src={fogRows}
	alt="The Inspector's Fog section, on, with Mode Exponential, Light Color pale blue, Light Energy 1.0, and Sun Scatter 0.15."
	caption="Fog, with Godot's own density."
/>

Save the scene and press <Kbd keys="F5" mac="Cmd+B" />.

<Figure
	src={fogDefault}
	alt="The running game: the whole meadow is under a thick pale blue-gray haze, the grass and rocks barely visible."
	caption="Density 0.01 buries the meadow."
/>

Godot's default density, 0.01, is far too much. Fog thickens with the distance between the camera and what it sees, and this camera is 60 meters from the ground. Exponential fog hides `1 - e^(-density × distance)` of the view: at 0.01 and 60 meters, 45 percent.

The template wants about 3.5 percent at the ground, a veil you feel more than see. Solve for the density: 0.035 / 60 is about `0.0006`. Set **Density** to `0.0006`.

<Figure
	src={fogDensityRows}
	alt="The Inspector's Fog section with Density 0.0006."
	caption="Density for a camera 60 meters away."
/>

<Aside type="note" title="If you move the camera">
The density depends on the camera's distance from the ground, not on its zoom. The camera rig in lesson 5 zooms by changing the view's size, so its distance stays 60 and one density fits every zoom. If you set the camera farther away, divide 0.035 by the new distance.
</Aside>

## Try it

- Set the sky's **Zenith Color** to a pink and its **Horizon Color** to an orange, save, and look at the meadow. Lesson 21 animates exactly these two colors.
- Set **Ambient Light > Source** to **Disabled** and look: only the sun lights the meadow.
- Raise SSAO's **Intensity** to `3.0` and see how dark a contact shadow can get. Then put it back.

## If it does not work

- **The meadow stays dark and gray.** The meadow still has no instance of the environment, or the environment's **Background > Mode** is still **Clear Color**. Check the Scene dock for `WorldEnvironment` with the scene icon, and the mode.
- **The instance does not show your changes.** Save the `world_environment` scene with <Kbd keys="Ctrl+S" mac="Cmd+S" />, then switch to the meadow tab.
- **The sky is black or pink.** The shader failed to compile. Open the **Shader Editor** and read its message, which names the line. The first line must be `shader_type sky;`.
- **There is no SSAO.** SSAO needs the Forward+ renderer. A project made in lesson 3 uses it.
- **The meadow is a flat haze.** The fog **Density** is still `0.01`.
- **The editor says the Environment's changes were not saved.** The Environment is part of the scene file, so save the scene.
