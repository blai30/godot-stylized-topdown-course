## What you will build

A player that moves from cell to cell. It always stands in the middle of a cell, turns to face the key you press, steps to the next cell, and carries on stepping while you hold the key. Trees, rocks, and the pond stop a step before it starts.

## How a step works

Free walking moves the player a little every frame, in any direction. Tile movement moves it in whole steps:

1. The player is idle, in the middle of a cell.
2. A direction key is held. The player turns that way. If the next cell is free, it picks that cell's middle as its **destination**.
3. Each physics tick it moves toward the destination at a fixed speed. When it gets there it snaps exactly onto the middle and is idle again.
4. If a key is still held, the next step starts at once, so there is no pause between steps.

Whether the next cell is free is the physics engine's question, not yours. `TestMove` asks "if this body moved by this much, would it hit something?" and answers without moving anything. The player's collision layer and mask from lesson 58 decide what counts: the mask holds `World` and `Terrain`, so trunks, rocks, and the pond's bank all stop it.

## Write the script

<Steps>

1. In your code editor, create `src/Player/TileMovement.cs` and save the code below into it.

2. Click **Build Project**.

</Steps>

The script does these things:

- It extends `CharacterBody3D`, so the camera rig and `PlayerAnimation` work with it unchanged.
- It has cached names for the four move actions, and two `[Export]` properties: `CellSize`, one meter by default, and `StepSeconds`, how long a step takes, a fifth of a second.
- `_Ready` moves the player to the middle of the cell it was placed in, so even a player dropped anywhere in the editor starts on the grid.
- `_PhysicsProcess` starts a step if the player is idle. If it has a destination, it works out how far one tick moves it. When the destination is nearer than that, it snaps onto it and goes idle. Otherwise it moves along the line to it.
- `StartStep` reads the move actions with `Input.GetVector`, and returns if no key is held. It keeps only the stronger axis, so the player never moves diagonally. It turns to face that way with `LookAt`, then asks `TestMove` about the next cell, and sets the destination only if the answer is no.
- `CellCenter` turns any position into the middle of its cell, at the same height. The destination always goes through it, so a step ends on the grid whatever the position was.
- It sets `Velocity` while it walks, though it does not use `Velocity` to move. `PlayerAnimation` reads the body's velocity to blend from standing to walking, so the walk animation plays.

## Try it

- Play the meadow with <Kbd keys="F6" mac="Cmd+R" />. Tap <Kbd keys="D" /> and the player takes one step. Hold it and the player walks cell after cell, and when you let go it finishes the step it is on and stops in the middle of a cell.

<Figure
	src={walking}
	alt="The meadow from above, zoomed in, with the character walking left along the dirt path beside an oak."
	caption="Held, the steps join into a walk."
/>

- Walk into a tree, a rock, and the pond. The player turns to face them and does not move.
- Change **Step Seconds** to `0.4` for a slow, careful walk, or `0.1` for a quick one.
- Change **Cell Size** to `2`. The player steps two meters at a time, between the middles of two-meter cells.
- Add `GridCursor` from lesson 61 to the same level. Its cells are one meter wide too, so the lit cell is the cell the player stands in when the mouse is over the player.
- Make a turn take a moment, as in many farming games: in `StartStep`, when the direction differs from the way the player faces, turn and return without stepping the first time.

## If it does not work

- **The player does not move.** The script is not on the root of `player.tscn`, the build failed, or the move actions are missing from the input map. Check the **Script** row and the **MSBuild** panel.
- **The player walks through trees.** `TestMove` uses the player's collision mask. Check that **Mask** has the layers of the trees and rocks, `World` and `Terrain`, ticked.
- **The player stops in the middle of nowhere.** It is blocked by a shape you cannot see, such as a trunk's collision cylinder that is wider than the tree looks. Turn on **Debug > Visible Collision Shapes** to see them.
- **The player slides off the grid after a step.** Another script moves the body too. Take `PlayerMovement` off, or the grid and the other movement will fight.
- **The animation shows standing while the player steps.** `PlayerAnimation` is not on the character's `AnimationTree`, which lesson 59 does in the player scene. The script sets the velocity the animation reads.
